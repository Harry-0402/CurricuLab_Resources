Unit 3: Business Correspondence

Unit Overview

Business Correspondence refers to the formal exchange of information between organizations, customers, employees, and other stakeholders through letters, reports, notices, and official documents. Effective business correspondence ensures clear communication, maintains professional relationships, and supports organizational objectives. This unit covers trade letters, sales letters, grievance letters, reports, and summarization techniques.

Key Concepts

Business Correspondence

Definition

Business Correspondence is the written communication exchanged between individuals, organizations, customers, and government agencies for business purposes.

Objectives

  • Exchange information.
  • Maintain records.
  • Build business relationships.
  • Solve business problems.
  • Promote products and services.

Characteristics

  • Formal language.
  • Clear and concise.
  • Professional tone.
  • Accurate information.

Trade Letters

Definition

Trade Letters are formal letters exchanged during business transactions.

Importance

  • Facilitate business operations.
  • Create written records.
  • Clarify business terms.
  • Resolve disputes.

Types of Trade Letters

1. Inquiry Letter

Definition

A letter requesting information about products, services, prices, or terms.

Purpose

  • Obtain information.
  • Compare alternatives.
  • Evaluate suppliers.

Example

A retailer requesting a price list from a manufacturer.

2. Order Letter

Definition

A letter used to place an order for goods or services.

Contents

  • Product details
  • Quantity
  • Price
  • Delivery instructions

Example

A company ordering 100 office chairs from a supplier.

3. Credit Inquiry Letter

Definition

A letter seeking information about a customer's financial credibility.

Purpose

  • Assess creditworthiness.
  • Reduce financial risk.
  • Support credit decisions.

Example

A supplier checking a customer's payment history before extending credit.

4. Status Inquiry Letter

Definition

A letter requesting information about the reputation or financial status of a business organization.

Purpose

  • Verify reliability.
  • Assess business standing.
  • Support business partnerships.

5. Complaint Letter

Definition

A letter expressing dissatisfaction regarding products or services.

Purpose

  • Report problems.
  • Request corrective action.
  • Seek compensation or replacement.

Example

Customer complaining about a defective product.

6. Claim Letter

Definition

A letter requesting compensation for loss, damage, or poor service.

Example

Claim for goods damaged during transportation.

7. Adjustment Letter

Definition

A response letter issued by a company to resolve customer complaints or claims.

Purpose

  • Maintain goodwill.
  • Resolve disputes.
  • Restore customer satisfaction.

Example

Company replacing a damaged product free of cost.

8. Collection Letter

Definition

A letter reminding customers to pay outstanding dues.

Purpose

  • Recover payments.
  • Maintain cash flow.
  • Reduce bad debts.

Sales Letters

Definition

A Sales Letter is a persuasive business letter designed to promote products or services.

Objectives

  • Increase sales.
  • Attract customers.
  • Create awareness.
  • Encourage purchases.

Characteristics

  • Persuasive language.
  • Customer-focused.
  • Clear call to action.

Promotional Leaflets and Flyers

Definition

Printed promotional materials used to advertise products, services, events, or offers.

Features

  • Attractive design.
  • Short content.
  • Eye-catching visuals.
  • Contact information.

Advantages

  • Cost-effective.
  • Wide reach.
  • Easy distribution.

Consumer Grievance Letters

Definition

Letters written by consumers to report dissatisfaction regarding products or services.

Common Issues

  • Defective products.
  • Poor service.
  • Overcharging.
  • Delayed delivery.

Purpose

  • Seek redressal.
  • Protect consumer rights.
  • Resolve disputes.

Right to Information (RTI) Act

Definition

The RTI Act empowers citizens to request information from public authorities.

Objectives

  • Promote transparency.
  • Ensure accountability.
  • Provide access to information.

Example

Requesting information regarding government services or policies.

A Report is a formal document presenting facts, findings, analysis, and recommendations.

Importance

Feasibility Report

Definition

A report assessing whether a project or proposal is practical and viable.

Areas Evaluated

  • Technical feasibility
  • Financial feasibility
  • Operational feasibility
  • Legal feasibility

Example

Evaluating whether a new manufacturing plant should be established.

Investigative Report

Definition

A report prepared after examining a problem, incident, or issue.

Purpose

  • Determine facts.
  • Identify causes.
  • Recommend corrective actions.

Example

Investigation of a workplace accident.

Summarization

Definition

Summarization is the process of condensing information while retaining the main ideas.

Objectives

  • Save time.
  • Improve understanding.
  • Highlight important points.

Steps in Summarization

Read Original Text
↓
Identify Main Ideas
↓
Identify Supporting Points
↓
Remove Unnecessary Details
↓
Rewrite Concisely
↓
Present Coherently

Identification of Main and Supporting Points

Main Points

  • Central ideas.
  • Essential information.

Supporting Points

  • Examples.
  • Explanations.
  • Evidence.

Example

Main Point: Effective communication improves teamwork.

Supporting Points:

  • Reduces misunderstandings.
  • Enhances collaboration.
  • Improves productivity.

Unit 3 Summary

Business Correspondence is essential for effective organizational communication. Trade letters facilitate business transactions, sales letters promote products, reports support decision-making, and summarization helps present information concisely. Mastering business correspondence improves professionalism and organizational efficiency.
