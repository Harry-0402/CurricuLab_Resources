Unit 3: Business Correspondence

Introduction

Business organizations communicate daily with customers, suppliers, government agencies, employees, and other stakeholders. Such communication must be professional, clear, concise, and legally appropriate. Business Correspondence refers to the exchange of information through written communication in a business environment.

Business correspondence includes letters, reports, notices, circulars, emails, and summaries. Effective business correspondence helps organizations maintain good relationships, resolve issues, promote products, and make informed decisions.

Importance of Business Correspondence

  • Facilitates communication.
  • Maintains business relationships.
  • Creates legal records.
  • Supports decision-making.
  • Enhances organizational image.

Business Correspondence Process

Need for Communication ↓ Draft Message ↓ Review Content ↓ Send Communication ↓ Receive Response ↓ Take Action

Topic 1: Trade Letters

Explanation

Trade letters are formal letters exchanged between businesses and their stakeholders regarding commercial transactions.

These letters help maintain smooth business operations and professional relationships.

Types of Trade Letters

  1. Inquiry Letter
  2. Order Letter
  3. Credit Inquiry Letter
  4. Status Inquiry Letter
  5. Complaint Letter
  6. Claim Letter
  7. Adjustment Letter
  8. Collection Letter

Topic 2: Inquiry Letter

Explanation

An Inquiry Letter is written to obtain information about products, services, prices, terms, or business conditions.

Objectives

  • Gather information.
  • Compare alternatives.
  • Clarify doubts.

Structure

Sender Details ↓ Subject ↓ Request for Information ↓ Closing

Example 1

A retailer asks a manufacturer for product price details.

Example 2

A student requests information regarding an MBA program.

Topic 3: Order Letter

Explanation

An Order Letter is written to place an order for goods or services.

Information Included

  • Product details
  • Quantity
  • Delivery date
  • Payment terms

Example 1

A supermarket orders inventory from a supplier.

Example 2

A college orders computers for a laboratory.

Topic 4: Credit Inquiry Letter

Explanation

A Credit Inquiry Letter seeks information regarding the financial credibility of a customer or business.

Importance

  • Reduces credit risk.
  • Ensures safe transactions.

Example 1

A wholesaler checks the creditworthiness of a new retailer.

Example 2

A bank requests financial information before approving a loan.

Topic 5: Status Inquiry Letter

Explanation

A Status Inquiry Letter requests information about the reputation, reliability, and financial standing of an organization.

Purpose

  • Verify business reputation.
  • Assess reliability.

Example 1

A supplier checks the business standing of a potential customer.

Example 2

A company verifies a distributor's market reputation.

Topic 6: Complaint Letter

Explanation

A Complaint Letter is written when goods or services fail to meet expectations.

Reasons for Complaint

  • Defective products
  • Late delivery
  • Incorrect billing
  • Poor service

Features

  • Clear description of problem.
  • Supporting evidence.
  • Request for corrective action.

Example 1

A customer receives damaged goods and files a complaint.

Example 2

A student complains about incorrect fee charges.

Topic 7: Claim Letter

Explanation

A Claim Letter requests compensation or corrective action for losses, damages, or deficiencies.

Situations

  • Damaged goods
  • Missing products
  • Service failures

Example 1

A retailer claims compensation for damaged inventory.

Example 2

A passenger claims reimbursement for lost baggage.

Topic 8: Adjustment Letter

Explanation

An Adjustment Letter is a response to a complaint or claim.

It informs the customer about corrective measures taken.

Objectives

  • Resolve disputes.
  • Retain customers.
  • Restore goodwill.

Example 1

A company replaces defective products.

Example 2

A service provider refunds charges for poor service.

Explanation

A Collection Letter reminds customers about overdue payments.

Objectives

  • Recover outstanding amounts.
  • Maintain business relations.
  • Encourage timely payment.

Levels of Collection Letters

Stage Purpose
First Reminder Gentle reminder
Second Reminder Follow-up
Final Notice Strong request

Example 1

A supplier reminds a retailer about unpaid invoices.

Example 2

A coaching institute reminds students about pending fees.

Consumers may write grievance letters to seek information, clarification, or redressal regarding issues affecting their rights.

Example 2
