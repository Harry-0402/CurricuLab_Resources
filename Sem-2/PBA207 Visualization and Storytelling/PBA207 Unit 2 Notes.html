Unit II: Visualization of Structured Data

Introduction

Structured data refers to data that is organized in a predefined format such as rows and columns in databases, spreadsheets, data warehouses, and ERP systems. Since structured data follows a fixed schema, it is easier to store, retrieve, analyze, and visualize.

Visualization of structured data helps organizations understand customer behavior, business performance, operational efficiency, and market trends. Through charts, graphs, and dashboards, structured data can be transformed into meaningful insights that support strategic decision-making.

Topic 1: Introduction to Structured Data Visualization

Explanation

Structured data consists of organized information that can be easily stored and processed in relational databases.

Examples of Structured Data

  • Sales records
  • Customer databases
  • Employee records
  • Financial transactions
  • Inventory data

Need for Structured Data Visualization

Organizations generate large volumes of structured data daily. Visualization helps:

  • Identify trends
  • Compare performance
  • Detect anomalies
  • Monitor KPIs
  • Support business decisions

Characteristics of Structured Data

Feature Description
Organized Format Stored in rows and columns
Easy Retrieval Quickly searchable
Consistent Structure Fixed schema
High Accuracy Easier validation
Suitable for Analytics Supports statistical analysis

Example 1

Walmart visualizes sales data across stores to monitor product performance.

Example 2

A college stores student attendance records in spreadsheets and visualizes attendance percentages using charts.

Topic 2: Exploratory Analysis

Explanation

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) is the process of examining datasets to understand their characteristics before applying advanced analytical techniques.

EDA helps analysts:

  • Understand data distribution
  • Detect errors
  • Identify trends
  • Discover relationships
  • Generate hypotheses

Objectives of EDA

  1. Understand data structure
  2. Detect missing values
  3. Identify outliers
  4. Discover patterns
  5. Support decision-making

EDA Process

Data Collection
↓
Data Cleaning
↓
Summary Statistics
↓
Visualization
↓
Pattern Discovery
↓
Insights

Common EDA Visualizations

  • Histograms
  • Scatter plots
  • Box plots
  • Bar charts
  • Line charts

Example 1

A telecom company explores customer churn data to identify factors causing customer loss.

Example 2

A teacher analyzes examination scores to understand overall class performance.

Topic 3: Univariate Analysis

Explanation

Univariate Analysis examines only one variable at a time.

Its purpose is to understand the characteristics and distribution of a single variable.

Questions Answered by Univariate Analysis

  • What is the average?
  • What is the maximum value?
  • What is the minimum value?
  • How is data distributed?

Common Techniques

Frequency Distribution

Shows occurrence of values.

Mean

Average value of data.

Median

Middle value of data.

Mode

Most frequent value.

Charts Used in Univariate Analysis

Chart Purpose
Histogram Distribution
Bar Chart Category comparison
Pie Chart Percentage share
Box Plot Spread and outliers

Example 1

An HR department analyzes employee salaries to understand salary distribution.

Example 2

A school analyzes students' age distribution using histograms.

Topic 4: Multivariate Analysis

Explanation

Multivariate Analysis studies relationships among multiple variables simultaneously.

It helps understand how one variable influences another.

Objectives

  • Find correlations
  • Identify dependencies
  • Predict outcomes
  • Support decision-making

Examples of Variables

  • Sales
  • Advertising expenditure
  • Customer satisfaction
  • Profit

Multivariate Analysis Process

Select Variables
↓
Prepare Data
↓
Visualize Relationships
↓
Analyze Patterns
↓
Generate Insights

Common Visualizations

  • Scatter plots
  • Bubble charts
  • Heat maps
  • Correlation matrices
  • Parallel coordinate plots

Example 1

A company analyzes the relationship between advertising spend, customer visits, and sales revenue.

Example 2

A researcher studies how study hours, attendance, and assignments affect student performance.

Topic 5: Charts to Visualize Multiple Measures

Explanation

Business problems often involve multiple variables. Specialized charts are used to represent several measures simultaneously.

1. Bubble Chart

A bubble chart extends a scatter plot by adding a third variable represented through bubble size.

Example 1

Sales, profit, and customer count across regions.

Example 2

Students' marks, attendance, and study hours.

2. Heat Map

Displays values using color intensity.

Example 1

Monthly sales across regions.

Example 2

Employee attendance patterns.

3. Treemap

Uses rectangles to represent hierarchical data.

Example 1

Company revenue by product categories.

Example 2

Library books categorized by subject.

4. Radar Chart

Compares multiple performance indicators.

Example 1

Employee performance evaluation.

Example 2

Student skill assessment.

5. Combination Chart

Combines multiple chart types.

Example 1

Sales represented by bars and profit represented by a line.

Example 2

Monthly revenue and expenses comparison.

Topic 6: Modeling

Explanation

Modeling refers to creating analytical or statistical models that help predict future outcomes and support decision-making.

Visualization plays an important role during model development because it helps analysts understand data behavior and model performance.

Types of Modeling

Descriptive Modeling

Explains what happened.

Predictive Modeling

Predicts future outcomes.

Prescriptive Modeling

Suggests actions to achieve desired outcomes.

Modeling Workflow

Data Collection
↓
Data Cleaning
↓
Feature Selection
↓
Model Building
↓
Visualization
↓
Evaluation

Benefits of Visualization in Modeling

  • Better understanding of data
  • Identification of patterns
  • Performance evaluation
  • Error detection

Example 1

A bank builds a loan approval model and visualizes risk categories.

Example 2

A store predicts future sales based on historical purchasing patterns.
