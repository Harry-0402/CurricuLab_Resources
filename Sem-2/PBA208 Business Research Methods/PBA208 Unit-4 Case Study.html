Case Study: Improving Customer Retention Through Data Analysis at Airtel

1. Title

Why Are Customers Leaving? Airtel's Data Analysis and Hypothesis Testing Challenge

2. Company Background

Bharti Airtel is one of India's largest telecommunications service providers, serving hundreds of millions of customers across mobile, broadband, enterprise, and digital service segments. The company operates in a highly competitive industry characterized by price competition, technological innovation, and changing customer expectations. Customer retention is a critical performance indicator because acquiring new customers is significantly more expensive than retaining existing ones. Over the past year, Airtel has observed increasing customer churn in selected markets despite continued investments in network infrastructure and digital services. Management has therefore initiated a research project aimed at identifying the factors influencing customer retention and developing evidence-based intervention strategies.

3. Business Context

The telecommunications industry generates large volumes of customer data, including usage behavior, billing information, service quality metrics, customer support interactions, and demographic information. Companies increasingly rely on statistical analysis and hypothesis testing to transform these data into actionable business insights.

Airtel's management believes that understanding customer churn requires more than descriptive reporting. The company must analyze relationships among multiple variables and statistically evaluate whether observed differences are meaningful or simply due to random variation.

The Analytics Division has proposed a comprehensive data analysis initiative involving univariate, bivariate, and multivariate analysis followed by formal hypothesis testing. However, management must determine how to interpret the results and use them to guide strategic decisions.

4. Strategic Problem Situation

Airtel recently experienced a rise in customer churn within several metropolitan regions. Preliminary reports suggested that factors such as network quality, pricing, customer service, and digital engagement may influence customer retention.

Different departments have proposed competing explanations. The Marketing Department believes churn is driven primarily by pricing competition. The Network Operations team argues that service quality differences explain customer departures. Customer Service managers emphasize complaint resolution performance.

The company collected data from 25,000 customers, including demographic characteristics, monthly spending, service usage, customer satisfaction scores, complaint history, network quality ratings, and churn status.

Management now faces the challenge of determining which factors truly influence customer retention. Simple descriptive reports provide conflicting interpretations, and executives require statistically reliable evidence before making significant investments in retention initiatives.

The Chief Analytics Officer recommends applying structured data analysis and hypothesis testing techniques to identify key drivers of churn and prioritize strategic actions.

6. Decision Dilemma

Airtel's management must decide which customer retention strategy should receive priority investment.

Option 1: Focus primarily on pricing and loyalty programs.

Option 2: Invest heavily in network quality improvements.

Option 3: Prioritize customer service and complaint resolution.

Option 4: Implement an integrated retention strategy based on comprehensive analytical findings.

Option 1 may improve competitiveness but could reduce profitability. Option 2 addresses service quality concerns but requires substantial capital investment. Option 3 improves customer experience but may not resolve underlying operational issues. Option 4 offers a comprehensive solution but requires greater coordination and resources.

Management must determine which strategy is best supported by the research evidence.

7. Critical Thinking and Evaluation Questions

Question 1

Using the case data, analyze the customer churn problem. How can univariate, bivariate, and multivariate analysis help Airtel identify the key drivers of customer retention?

Question 2

Evaluate the four strategic alternatives. Which option should Airtel pursue and why?

Question 3

How should Airtel formulate and test hypotheses regarding the relationship between customer satisfaction, network quality, complaints, and customer churn?

Question 4

What statistical procedures, performance metrics, and monitoring systems should Airtel implement to continuously evaluate customer retention and the effectiveness of future interventions?

Model Answers

Answer 1

The case demonstrates that customer churn is influenced by multiple interconnected factors rather than a single cause. A structured analytical approach is therefore essential.

Univariate analysis helps management understand the distribution of individual variables. For example, overall churn increased from 9% to 15%, indicating a significant deterioration in retention performance. Customer satisfaction, network quality, and complaint frequency can each be analyzed independently to identify patterns.

Bivariate analysis reveals relationships between variables and churn. Customers with high satisfaction exhibit only 4% churn, compared with 28% among low-satisfaction customers. Similarly, customers experiencing poor network quality have a churn rate of 26%, while those reporting excellent service quality churn at only 5%. Complaint frequency demonstrates a similar pattern, with churn increasing from 6% among customers with no complaints to 31% among customers reporting three or more complaints.

Multivariate analysis is required because these variables likely interact. For example, poor network quality may increase complaints, which in turn reduce customer satisfaction and increase churn. Multivariate techniques can isolate the independent contribution of each factor while controlling for others.

The evidence strongly suggests that customer churn is associated with service quality, satisfaction, and complaint experiences. Therefore, management should avoid relying on a single explanation and instead use multivariate analysis and hypothesis testing to determine the most influential retention drivers.

Answer 2

Option 4, the Integrated Retention Strategy, represents the strongest strategic choice.

Option 1 focuses on pricing and loyalty programs. While pricing may influence retention, the case data provide stronger evidence linking churn to satisfaction, service quality, and complaints rather than price alone.

Option 2 addresses network quality, which appears highly significant. Customers reporting poor network quality experience churn rates five times higher than those reporting excellent service. However, network improvements alone may not resolve customer service issues.

Option 3 focuses on complaint management and customer support. Since churn increases substantially among customers with frequent complaints, this initiative would likely generate meaningful benefits. Nevertheless, complaint reduction alone may not address root causes.

Option 4 aligns best with the analytical evidence. The data indicate that churn is driven by multiple interconnected factors. Network quality influences customer satisfaction, which affects complaints and ultimately retention outcomes. Addressing only one factor may limit overall effectiveness.

Although the integrated strategy requires the highest investment, it directly targets the primary drivers identified through data analysis. By simultaneously improving service quality, customer experience, and loyalty initiatives, Airtel can maximize retention improvements while strengthening long-term competitive positioning.

Therefore, the Integrated Retention Strategy provides the most evidence-based and sustainable solution.
