Unit 2: Research Design

Unit Overview

Research Design is the blueprint or framework of a research study. It specifies how data will be collected, measured, and analyzed to answer research questions effectively. This unit covers research designs, validity, data collection methods, attitudinal scales, and questionnaire design.

Key Concepts

1. Research Design: Definition and Functions

Definition

Research Design is a systematic plan that guides the collection, measurement, and analysis of data in a research project.

Key Points

  • Acts as a blueprint for research.
  • Ensures efficient use of resources.
  • Minimizes bias and errors.
  • Improves reliability and validity.
  • Helps achieve research objectives.

Functions of Research Design

Function Purpose
Planning Organizes research activities
Control Reduces errors and bias
Data Collection Specifies methods and sources
Analysis Guides statistical techniques
Interpretation Assists in drawing conclusions

Example

A company planning a customer satisfaction survey decides the sample size, questionnaire format, and analysis method before collecting data.

2. Exploratory Research Design

Definition

Exploratory research is conducted when the problem is unclear and more information is needed.

Key Points

  • Flexible and unstructured.
  • Generates ideas and insights.
  • Helps define problems clearly.
  • Usually qualitative.

Methods

  • Literature review
  • Expert interviews
  • Focus groups
  • Case studies

Example

A startup investigates why customers are abandoning its mobile app.

3. Descriptive Research Design

Definition

Descriptive research describes characteristics, behavior, or situations without influencing them.

Key Points

  • Structured and formal.
  • Answers What, Who, When, Where.
  • Uses surveys and observations.
  • Quantitative in nature.

Example

Measuring customer satisfaction levels among bank customers.

4. Experimental Research Design

Definition

Experimental research studies cause-and-effect relationships by manipulating independent variables.

Key Points

  • Establishes causality.
  • Includes control and treatment groups.
  • Measures impact of one variable on another.

Types of Experimental Designs

A. Pre-Experimental Design

  • Simplest form of experiment.
  • No proper control group.
  • Limited validity.

Example: Testing employee performance before and after training.

B. Quasi-Experimental Design

  • Partial control over variables.
  • Random assignment may not be possible.

Example: Comparing sales performance of two existing branches.

C. True Experimental Design

  • Random assignment.
  • Control and experimental groups.
  • Highest level of validity.

Example: Testing effectiveness of two advertising campaigns.

D. Statistical Design

  • Uses statistical controls.
  • Helps improve precision.
  • Suitable for complex studies.

Comparison of Experimental Designs

Feature Pre-Experimental Quasi-Experimental True Experimental
Randomization No Limited Yes
Control Group No Partial Yes
Validity Low Moderate High
Cost Low Medium High

5. Validity of Research Instruments

Definition

Validity refers to the extent to which a research instrument measures what it is intended to measure.

Types of Validity

A. Face Validity

Meaning

The instrument appears to measure the intended concept.

Example

A customer satisfaction questionnaire appears relevant to respondents.

B. Content Validity

Meaning

The instrument adequately covers all aspects of the concept.

Example

An employee performance survey includes productivity, teamwork, attendance, and communication.

C. Construct Validity

Meaning

The instrument accurately measures the theoretical construct.

Example

A motivation scale truly measures employee motivation.

Comparison of Validity Types

Type Focus
Face Validity Appearance
Content Validity Coverage
Construct Validity Accuracy of theoretical measurement

6. Methods of Data Collection

Definition

Data collection refers to gathering information needed for research.

Types of Data

Primary Data

Collected directly from respondents.

Examples:

  • Surveys
  • Interviews
  • Observation
  • Experiments

Secondary Data

Already collected by others.

Examples:

  • Government reports
  • Journals
  • Company records
  • Websites

Data Collection Methods

Method Advantages Limitation
Interview Detailed information Time-consuming
Questionnaire Large coverage Low response rate
Observation Real behavior captured Observer bias
Experiment Establishes causality Expensive

7. Attitudinal Scales

Definition

Attitudinal scales measure opinions, attitudes, perceptions, and preferences.

Importance

  • Measures customer satisfaction.
  • Evaluates employee attitudes.
  • Supports market research.

Likert Scale

Definition

A scale where respondents indicate their level of agreement with statements.

Typical Format

Scale Value Response
1 Strongly Disagree
2 Disagree
3 Neutral
4 Agree
5 Strongly Agree

Example

"The company's service quality is excellent."

8. Questionnaire Designing

Definition

Questionnaire design is the process of preparing a structured set of questions for collecting data.

Objectives

  • Obtain accurate information.
  • Minimize misunderstanding.
  • Increase response rate.

Principles of Good Questionnaire Design

  • Use simple language.
  • Avoid ambiguous questions.
  • Avoid leading questions.
  • Keep questions relevant.
  • Maintain logical sequence.

Types of Questions

Open-Ended Questions

Respondents answer in their own words.

Example:
What improvements would you suggest for our service?

Close-Ended Questions

Fixed response options.

Example:
Are you satisfied with our service?

Important Definitions

  • Research Design – Blueprint of research activities.
  • Exploratory Research – Research for initial understanding.
  • Descriptive Research – Research describing characteristics.
  • Experimental Research – Research establishing cause-effect relationships.
  • Validity – Accuracy of measurement.
  • Face Validity – Apparent relevance of a measure.
  • Content Validity – Coverage of all dimensions.
  • Construct Validity – Measurement of theoretical concepts.
  • Primary Data – First-hand collected data.
  • Secondary Data – Previously collected data.
  • Likert Scale – Attitude measurement scale.
  • Questionnaire – Structured set of questions.
