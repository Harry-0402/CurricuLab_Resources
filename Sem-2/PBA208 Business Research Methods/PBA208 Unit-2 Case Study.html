Case Study: Designing Market Research for a New Electric Vehicle Launch at Tata Motors

1. Title

Getting the Research Right: Tata Motors' Research Design Challenge Before Launching a New Electric Vehicle

2. Company Background

Tata Motors is one of India's largest automobile companies and a market leader in the country's electric vehicle (EV) segment. The company manufactures passenger vehicles, commercial vehicles, and electric mobility solutions. Rising environmental awareness, government incentives, and growing fuel costs have accelerated EV adoption across India. To strengthen its market position, Tata Motors plans to introduce a premium electric SUV targeted at urban professionals and middle-income households. However, before committing large-scale investments in production, marketing, and distribution, management wants reliable market intelligence regarding customer preferences, pricing expectations, purchase intentions, and product features.

3. Business Context

The Indian electric vehicle market is evolving rapidly. Consumer awareness is increasing, but concerns regarding charging infrastructure, battery life, resale value, and purchase costs continue to influence buying behavior.

Automotive companies face significant risks when launching new products because customer preferences can vary considerably across regions, income groups, and demographic segments. Consequently, organizations increasingly rely on well-designed research studies to minimize uncertainty and support evidence-based decision-making.

Tata Motors recognizes that poor research design could lead to inaccurate conclusions, resulting in incorrect product positioning, ineffective marketing strategies, and financial losses. Therefore, management is evaluating different research design approaches to ensure that the collected data accurately reflects market realities.

4. Strategic Problem Situation

Tata Motors' product development team has finalized the prototype for a new premium electric SUV. The vehicle includes advanced connectivity features, AI-enabled driving assistance, and extended battery range capabilities.

Senior executives disagree regarding customer priorities. The Marketing Department believes customers are primarily concerned about affordability. Product Development argues that technology and driving experience will be the primary purchase drivers. Meanwhile, Sales teams suggest that charging infrastructure availability remains the most important factor.

The company plans to conduct a nationwide market research study. However, researchers are debating which research design should be adopted.

Some managers recommend exploratory research to better understand customer attitudes toward EV adoption. Others advocate descriptive research to measure market demand and customer preferences quantitatively. A third group proposes experimental research to test different pricing strategies and promotional campaigns.

Management must decide which research design, data collection methods, validity measures, and questionnaire structure will provide the most reliable information for the product launch decision.

6. Decision Dilemma

Tata Motors must determine the most appropriate research design for evaluating market demand.

Option 1: Conduct exploratory research using focus groups and interviews.

Option 2: Implement a descriptive survey-based study to measure customer preferences.

Option 3: Use experimental research to test alternative pricing and marketing strategies.

Option 4: Adopt an integrated research design combining exploratory, descriptive, and experimental approaches with validated questionnaires and structured data collection methods.

Option 1 provides rich insights but limited statistical reliability. Option 2 generates measurable data but may overlook hidden customer motivations. Option 3 identifies cause-and-effect relationships but is expensive and time-consuming. Option 4 provides comprehensive understanding but requires the highest investment and coordination effort.

Management must determine which approach best supports the product launch decision.

Model Answers

Answer 1

Tata Motors faces a complex research problem involving customer preferences, pricing sensitivity, technology adoption, and purchase intentions for a new electric vehicle. Given the substantial investment associated with vehicle production and launch, the quality of research findings will directly influence strategic success.

Research design is critical because different designs address different managerial questions. Exploratory research helps uncover customer motivations and concerns, descriptive research measures the extent of those concerns, and experimental research evaluates causal relationships such as the impact of pricing on purchase intention.

The case data reveal significant uncertainty. While 72% of respondents identify price as a major concern, substantial percentages also emphasize charging infrastructure, battery performance, and resale value. Without an appropriate research design, management may incorrectly prioritize one factor over another.

Data collection methods also affect research quality. Face-to-face interviews and test market experiments demonstrate higher expected response quality than online surveys. Selecting inappropriate methods could introduce bias and reduce accuracy.

Instrument validity is equally important. If questionnaires fail to measure customer attitudes accurately, the resulting decisions may be flawed. Face validity ensures questions appear relevant, content validity confirms coverage of all important dimensions, and construct validity verifies that the instrument measures the intended concepts.

Therefore, research design, data collection quality, and validity assessment are essential because they determine whether management receives reliable evidence for a high-stakes strategic decision.

Answer 2

Option 4, the Integrated Research Design, represents the strongest strategic choice.

Option 1 provides valuable exploratory insights through interviews and focus groups. However, it lacks the quantitative rigor required for major investment decisions. Option 2 generates measurable market estimates but may not uncover deeper customer motivations and barriers.

Option 3 offers strong causal evidence through experimental research but may not provide sufficient understanding of broader customer perceptions. Furthermore, experimental studies are expensive and typically focus on a limited number of variables.

Option 4 combines the strengths of all approaches. Exploratory research can identify important customer concerns and generate hypotheses. Descriptive research can measure the prevalence of those concerns across larger populations. Experimental research can then test alternative pricing, promotional strategies, and product configurations.

The strategic risks highlighted in the case justify this approach. Launch failure, incorrect pricing, and invalid research findings all have high impact scores. Reducing these uncertainties requires a comprehensive research framework rather than reliance on a single method.

Additionally, integrating validated questionnaires, Likert scales, and multiple data collection methods increases both reliability and validity. Although the cost is higher, the investment is relatively small compared to the financial consequences of an unsuccessful vehicle launch.

Therefore, Tata Motors should adopt the Integrated Research Design to obtain comprehensive, accurate, and actionable insights before making final product launch decisions.
