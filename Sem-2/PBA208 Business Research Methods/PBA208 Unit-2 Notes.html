Unit II: Research Design

Introduction

After identifying a research problem and developing hypotheses, the next important step is to design the research. Research Design acts as a blueprint or roadmap for conducting research. It specifies how data will be collected, measured, analyzed, and interpreted.

A good research design ensures that the research objectives are achieved efficiently, accurately, and economically. It minimizes errors and helps researchers obtain reliable and valid results.

According to the syllabus, this unit covers research design, types of research designs, validity of research instruments, methods of data collection, attitudinal scales, and questionnaire designing.

Topic 1: Research Design – Definition and Functions

Explanation

Research Design is the overall plan or framework that guides the collection and analysis of data for a research study.

Definitions

Research design specifies:

  • What information is needed.
  • How information will be collected.
  • From whom it will be collected.
  • How it will be analyzed.

Functions of Research Design

1. Provides Direction

Acts as a roadmap for research.

2. Ensures Accuracy

Reduces chances of errors and bias.

3. Facilitates Data Collection

Defines methods and procedures.

4. Saves Time and Cost

Avoids unnecessary activities.

5. Improves Reliability

Ensures consistent results.

Characteristics of a Good Research Design

Characteristic Description
Flexible Can accommodate changes
Reliable Produces consistent results
Valid Measures what it intends to measure
Economical Cost-effective
Objective Minimizes researcher bias

Example 1

A company planning a customer satisfaction survey creates a research design before collecting responses.

Example 2

A college conducts a placement study using a structured research plan.

Topic 2: Exploratory Research Design

Explanation

Exploratory Research Design is used when the problem is not clearly understood and more information is needed.

Objectives

  • Gain preliminary understanding.
  • Generate ideas.
  • Identify variables.
  • Develop hypotheses.

Characteristics

  • Flexible
  • Informal
  • Qualitative in nature
  • Useful in initial stages

Methods Used

  • Literature review
  • Focus groups
  • Expert interviews
  • Case studies

Flow of Exploratory Research

Problem Identification
↓
Information Gathering
↓
Exploration
↓
Insight Generation
↓
Hypothesis Development

Example 1

A startup investigates why customers are not downloading its mobile application.

Example 2

A restaurant explores reasons for declining customer visits.

Topic 3: Descriptive Research Design

Explanation

Descriptive Research Design describes characteristics of a population, phenomenon, or situation.

It answers:

  • Who?
  • What?
  • When?
  • Where?
  • How?

Characteristics

  • Structured
  • Quantitative
  • Large sample size
  • Statistical analysis possible

Methods

  • Surveys
  • Observation
  • Secondary data analysis

Applications

  • Customer profile studies
  • Market share analysis
  • Employee satisfaction surveys

Example 1

A company studies demographic characteristics of its customers.

Example 2

A university surveys students regarding campus facilities.

Topic 4: Experimental Research Design

Explanation

Experimental Research Design studies cause-and-effect relationships by manipulating one or more variables.

Purpose

To determine whether changes in one variable cause changes in another variable.

Components

Component Description
Independent Variable Variable manipulated
Dependent Variable Variable affected
Control Group Comparison group
Experimental Group Group receiving treatment

Types of Experimental Design

A. Pre-Experimental Design

Simplest form of experiment.

Characteristics:

  • No randomization
  • Limited control

Example 1

Testing customer response to a new product in a single store.

Example 2

Evaluating student performance after one training session.

B. Quasi-Experimental Design

Partial control over variables.

Characteristics:

  • No complete random assignment
  • Practical for field studies

Example 1

Comparing sales performance before and after marketing campaigns.

Example 2

Comparing performance of two existing employee groups.

C. True Experimental Design

Most rigorous design.

Characteristics:

  • Random assignment
  • Control group
  • High validity

Example 1

Testing effectiveness of two advertising campaigns using randomly selected customers.

Example 2

Medical trials involving treatment and control groups.

D. Statistical Design

Uses statistical techniques to control variations and improve accuracy.

Example 1

Agricultural experiments analyzing multiple factors.

Example 2

Market research studies involving large datasets.

Topic 5: Validity of Research Instruments

Explanation

Validity refers to the extent to which a research instrument measures what it is intended to measure.

A valid instrument provides accurate and meaningful results.

Types of Validity

1. Face Validity

Measures whether the instrument appears appropriate at first glance.

Example

A customer satisfaction questionnaire appears relevant to customer experiences.

2. Content Validity

Measures whether all aspects of a concept are covered.

Example

An employee satisfaction survey includes salary, work environment, growth opportunities, and management support.

3. Construct Validity

Measures whether the instrument accurately represents the theoretical concept.

Example

A leadership assessment accurately measures leadership qualities.

Comparison of Validity Types

Validity Type Focus
Face Validity Appearance
Content Validity Coverage
Construct Validity Theoretical Accuracy

Example 1

A bank validates a customer satisfaction questionnaire before conducting surveys.

Example 2

A university reviews examination questions to ensure syllabus coverage.

Topic 6: Methods of Data Collection

Explanation

Data collection is the process of gathering information for research purposes.

A. Primary Data Collection

Data collected directly from respondents.

Methods

1. Survey

Collecting information through questionnaires.

2. Interview

Direct interaction with respondents.

3. Observation

Watching and recording behavior.

4. Experiment

Conducting controlled studies.

B. Secondary Data Collection

Data already collected by others.

Sources

  • Government reports
  • Research journals
  • Company reports
  • Websites
  • Databases

Comparison: Primary vs Secondary Data

Feature Primary Data Secondary Data
Source Original Existing
Cost High Low
Time More Less
Accuracy Specific General

Example 1

A company surveys customers directly to collect primary data.

Example 2

A researcher uses Census data as secondary data.

Topic 7: Attitudinal Scales – Likert Scale

Explanation

Attitudinal scales measure opinions, beliefs, attitudes, and perceptions.

The most commonly used scale is the Likert Scale.

Likert Scale

Respondents indicate their level of agreement.

Example

Statement Scale
The service quality is excellent. 1 2 3 4 5
Strongly Disagree → Strongly Agree

Advantages of Likert Scale

  • Easy to administer
  • Easy to understand
  • Quantifiable
  • Widely accepted

Customer satisfaction surveys.

Example 2

Employee engagement surveys.

Topic 8: Questionnaire Designing

Explanation

A questionnaire is a structured set of questions used to collect information from respondents.

Good questionnaire design improves data quality and response rates.

Principles of Questionnaire Design

1. Define Objectives Clearly

Know what information is needed.

2. Use Simple Language

Questions should be easy to understand.

3. Avoid Ambiguity

Questions must be clear.

4. Avoid Leading Questions

Do not influence responses.

5. Logical Sequence

Arrange questions systematically.

Types of Questions

Open-Ended Questions

Respondents answer in their own words.

Example:

"What improvements would you suggest?"

Closed-Ended Questions

Respondents select from given options.

Example:

"How satisfied are you with our service?"

  • Very Satisfied
  • Satisfied
  • Neutral
  • Dissatisfied
  • Very Dissatisfied
