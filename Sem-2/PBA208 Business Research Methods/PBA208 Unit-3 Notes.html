Unit III: Sampling

Introduction

In research, it is often impossible to collect data from every individual, customer, employee, or object in the population because of limitations of time, cost, and resources. Therefore, researchers select a smaller representative group called a sample.

Sampling is one of the most important aspects of research methodology because the quality of research findings largely depends on the quality of the sample selected. A properly selected sample accurately represents the population and allows researchers to draw reliable conclusions.

This unit covers the concept of sampling, sampling design, types of sampling methods, sampling frame, sample size determination, and data processing techniques such as editing, coding, and tabulation.

Topic 1: Concept of Sampling

Explanation

Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of individuals or elements from a larger population for the purpose of conducting research.

Definitions

  • Population (Universe): Entire group under study.
  • Sample: Small representative portion of the population.
  • Sampling: Process of selecting the sample.

Example

Population = All MBA students in a university.

Sample = 200 MBA students selected for a survey.

Why Sampling is Necessary

1. Saves Time

Studying the entire population takes significant time.

2. Reduces Cost

Sampling is more economical than conducting a census.

3. Easier Administration

Data collection becomes manageable.

4. Faster Decision Making

Results are obtained quickly.

5. Practicality

Some populations are too large to study completely.

Characteristics of a Good Sample

  • Representative
  • Adequate size
  • Free from bias
  • Reliable
  • Accurate

Example 1

A bank surveys 500 customers from a customer base of 50,000.

Example 2

A college surveys selected students instead of interviewing every student.

Topic 2: Sampling Design

Explanation

Sampling Design is the plan or procedure used to select a sample from a population.

It specifies:

  • Who will be sampled.
  • How many individuals will be sampled.
  • How they will be selected.

Objectives of Sampling Design

  • Ensure representativeness.
  • Minimize bias.
  • Improve accuracy.
  • Reduce research costs.

Topic 3: Types of Sampling Designs

Sampling methods are broadly classified into:

  1. Probability Sampling
  2. Non-Probability Sampling
  3. Mixed Sampling Design

A. Probability Sampling

Explanation

In probability sampling, every member of the population has a known and non-zero chance of being selected.

Advantages

  • Less bias
  • More representative
  • Supports statistical analysis

Types of Probability Sampling

1. Simple Random Sampling

Every population member has an equal chance of selection.

Example 1

Randomly selecting 100 students from a list of 1,000 students.

Example 2

Lottery method for selecting survey participants.

2. Systematic Sampling

Every nth element is selected.

Example

Selecting every 10th customer entering a store.

3. Stratified Sampling

Population is divided into homogeneous groups called strata.

Example

Dividing employees into departments and selecting samples from each department.

4. Cluster Sampling

Population is divided into clusters, and entire clusters are selected.

Example

Selecting specific schools from a city for educational research.

B. Non-Probability Sampling

Explanation

In non-probability sampling, population members do not have equal chances of selection.

Selection depends on researcher judgment or convenience.

Types of Non-Probability Sampling

1. Convenience Sampling

Respondents are selected based on ease of access.

Example

Surveying shoppers available at a mall.

2. Judgment Sampling

Researcher selects participants based on expertise.

Example

Interviewing industry experts.

3. Quota Sampling

Population is divided into categories, and fixed quotas are assigned.

Example

Selecting equal numbers of male and female respondents.

4. Snowball Sampling

Existing respondents help identify additional respondents.

Example

Research involving rare professional groups.

C. Mixed Sampling Design

Explanation

Mixed Sampling combines probability and non-probability sampling techniques.

Advantages

  • Increased flexibility
  • Improved representation
  • Better practical applicability

Example 1

A company uses stratified sampling for customer categories and convenience sampling within each category.

Example 2

A researcher combines random selection with expert interviews.

Comparison: Probability vs Non-Probability Sampling

Feature Probability Sampling Non-Probability Sampling
Selection Chance Known Unknown
Bias Lower Higher
Statistical Analysis Suitable Limited
Cost Higher Lower
Accuracy Higher Moderate

Topic 4: Sampling Frame

Explanation

A Sampling Frame is the complete list of population elements from which a sample is selected.

It serves as the source for selecting respondents.

Examples of Sampling Frames

  • Customer databases
  • Employee records
  • Voter lists
  • Student enrollment records

Importance

  • Ensures proper coverage.
  • Reduces sampling errors.
  • Improves representativeness.

Example 1

A company uses its customer database as a sampling frame.

Example 2

A university uses the student registration list.

Topic 5: Sample Size Determination

Explanation

Sample Size refers to the number of observations or respondents selected for a study.

Choosing an appropriate sample size is critical because:

  • Too small → unreliable results.
  • Too large → increased cost and time.

Factors Affecting Sample Size

1. Population Size

Larger populations often require larger samples.

2. Desired Accuracy

Higher accuracy requires larger samples.

3. Budget

Research costs affect sample size decisions.

4. Research Objectives

Complex studies may require larger samples.

5. Variability in Data

More variability requires larger samples.

General Guidelines

Population Size Typical Sample Size
100 80–90
500 200–250
1000 250–300
5000+ 350–500

Example 1

A survey of 10,000 customers may require approximately 400 respondents.

Example 2

A university study involving 500 students may use 220 respondents.

Topic 6: Data Processing

Explanation

After collecting data, researchers must organize and prepare it for analysis.

Data processing includes:

  1. Editing
  2. Coding
  3. Tabulation

A. Editing

Explanation

Editing involves checking collected data for errors, omissions, and inconsistencies.

Objectives

  • Improve accuracy
  • Remove mistakes
  • Ensure completeness

Example 1

Checking survey forms for unanswered questions.

Example 2

Correcting duplicate entries.

B. Coding

Explanation

Coding involves assigning symbols or numbers to responses.

Example

Gender:

  • Male = 1
  • Female = 2

Satisfaction Level:

  • Very Satisfied = 5
  • Satisfied = 4
  • Neutral = 3
  • Dissatisfied = 2
  • Very Dissatisfied = 1

Benefits

  • Easier analysis
  • Faster processing
  • Improved organization

C. Tabulation

Explanation

Tabulation refers to arranging data into rows and columns.

Objectives

  • Summarize data
  • Facilitate analysis
  • Improve interpretation

Example

Satisfaction Level Frequency
Very Satisfied 50
Satisfied 80
Neutral 30
Dissatisfied 20
Very Dissatisfied 10
