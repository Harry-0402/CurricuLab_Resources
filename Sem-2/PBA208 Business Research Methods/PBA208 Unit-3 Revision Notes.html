Unit 3: Sampling

Unit Overview

Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of individuals or elements from a population to represent the whole population. It helps researchers save time, cost, and effort while obtaining reliable results. This unit covers sampling concepts, sampling designs, sample size determination, and data processing techniques.

Key Concepts

1. Concept of Sampling

Definition

Sampling is the process of selecting a representative portion of a population for research purposes.

Key Points

  • Population refers to the entire group under study.
  • Sample is a subset of the population.
  • Sampling helps reduce cost and time.
  • Results from a sample are generalized to the population.

Example

A company surveys 500 customers out of 50,000 customers to measure satisfaction.

2. Sampling Design

Definition

Sampling Design is a definite plan used to select sample units from a population.

Key Points

  • Improves research accuracy.

3. Types of Sampling Designs

A. Probability Sampling

Definition

Every member of the population has a known and equal chance of being selected.

Characteristics

  • Scientific method.
  • Less bias.
  • Higher reliability.
  • Suitable for statistical analysis.

Types of Probability Sampling

1. Simple Random Sampling

Every unit has an equal chance of selection.

Example: Lottery method.

2. Systematic Sampling

Selection at regular intervals.

Formula:

Sampling Interval (k) = Population Size ÷ Sample Size

Example: Selecting every 10th customer.

3. Stratified Sampling

Population divided into homogeneous groups (strata).

Example: Employees grouped by department.

4. Cluster Sampling

Population divided into clusters and selected randomly.

Example: Selecting certain cities for a survey.

B. Non-Probability Sampling

Definition

Selection is based on researcher judgment rather than random selection.

Characteristics

  • Faster and cheaper.
  • Greater risk of bias.
  • Useful in exploratory studies.

Types of Non-Probability Sampling

1. Convenience Sampling

Selecting easily available respondents.

Example: Surveying shoppers at a mall.

2. Judgment Sampling

Researcher selects respondents based on expertise.

Example: Interviewing industry experts.

3. Quota Sampling

Predefined quotas are established.

Example: 50 males and 50 females.

4. Snowball Sampling

Existing participants recruit additional participants.

Example: Research involving rare populations.

C. Mixed Sampling Design

Definition

Combination of probability and non-probability sampling techniques.

Advantages

  • Greater flexibility.
  • Better representation.
  • Improved accuracy.

Example

Selecting departments randomly and then using convenience sampling within departments.

4. Sampling Frame

Definition

A Sampling Frame is a complete list of all elements in the population from which a sample is selected.

Key Points

  • Must be accurate and updated.
  • Should include all population units.
  • Essential for probability sampling.

Examples

  • Employee database
  • Customer list
  • Voter list
  • Student records

Importance

  • Ensures representativeness.
  • Reduces sampling errors.
  • Improves reliability.

5. Sample Size Determination

Definition

Sample size refers to the number of observations selected from the population.

Factors Affecting Sample Size

  • Population size
  • Research objectives
  • Budget constraints
  • Time availability
  • Desired accuracy
  • Population variability

General Guidelines

Population Size Suggested Sample Size
Small Larger proportion
Large Smaller proportion often sufficient
High Variability Larger sample needed
Low Variability Smaller sample sufficient

Importance

  • Improves accuracy.
  • Reduces sampling error.
  • Enhances reliability.

Example

A survey of 400 customers from a population of 20,000 customers.
