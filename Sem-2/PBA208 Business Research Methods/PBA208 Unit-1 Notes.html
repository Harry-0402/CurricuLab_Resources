Unit I: Introduction to Business Research

Introduction

Business Research is a systematic and scientific process of collecting, analyzing, and interpreting information to solve business problems and support decision-making. In today's competitive business environment, organizations rely heavily on research to understand customer needs, market trends, competitor strategies, and operational challenges.

Business research helps managers make informed decisions rather than relying on assumptions or intuition. It reduces uncertainty and improves the quality of business decisions.

Topic 1: Types of Research

Explanation

Research can be classified based on its purpose, objectives, methods, and application.

1. Basic Research (Pure Research)

Basic research aims to expand knowledge and understanding without focusing on immediate practical application.

Characteristics

  • Generates new theories.
  • Increases knowledge.
  • Conducted mainly in academic institutions.

Example 1

A university researcher studies consumer behavior theories.

Example 2

A psychologist researches how people make purchasing decisions.

2. Applied Research

Applied research is conducted to solve a specific practical problem.

Characteristics

  • Problem-oriented.
  • Directly applicable.
  • Used by organizations and industries.

Example 1

A company studies declining sales to identify causes and solutions.

Example 2

A bank researches customer satisfaction to improve services.

3. Exploratory Research

Exploratory research is conducted when the problem is not clearly defined.

Objectives

  • Gain understanding.
  • Identify possible causes.
  • Develop hypotheses.

Example 1

A startup explores reasons for low customer engagement.

Example 2

A retailer investigates unexpected changes in customer preferences.

4. Descriptive Research

Descriptive research describes characteristics of a population or phenomenon.

Objectives

  • Answer who, what, when, where, and how questions.

Example 1

A survey describing customer demographics.

Example 2

A study of employee satisfaction levels.

5. Causal Research

Causal research identifies cause-and-effect relationships.

Objectives

  • Determine whether one variable affects another.

Example 1

Testing whether advertising increases sales.

Example 2

Studying the impact of discounts on customer purchases.

Comparison of Research Types

Research Type Purpose Example
Basic Generate knowledge Consumer behavior theory
Applied Solve practical problems Improve customer service
Exploratory Understand problems Identify causes of low sales
Descriptive Describe characteristics Customer survey
Causal Determine relationships Advertising impact study

Step 1: Identification of Problem

The first step is to clearly define the issue requiring investigation.

Example

Declining customer retention.

Step 2: Literature Review

Review previous studies related to the research topic.

Purpose

  • Understand existing knowledge.
  • Avoid duplication.
  • Identify research gaps.

Step 3: Research Objectives

Define what the study intends to achieve.

Example

To identify factors affecting customer loyalty.

Step 4: Hypothesis Development

Develop assumptions to be tested.

Step 5: Research Design

Select methods and procedures for conducting research.

Step 6: Data Collection

Gather information using:

  • Surveys
  • Interviews
  • Observation
  • Questionnaires

Step 7: Data Analysis

Analyze collected information using statistical tools.

Step 8: Interpretation

Convert results into meaningful conclusions.

Step 9: Report Preparation

Prepare findings and recommendations.

Example 1

A telecom company investigates customer churn using surveys and statistical analysis.

Example 2

A college conducts research to identify factors affecting student performance.

Topic 3: Formulation of Research Problem

Explanation

A research problem is a specific issue, difficulty, contradiction, or gap in knowledge that requires investigation.

A well-defined research problem forms the foundation of successful research.

Characteristics of a Good Research Problem

  • Clear and specific
  • Researchable
  • Relevant
  • Feasible
  • Significant

Sources of Research Problems

Internal Sources

  • Organizational issues
  • Employee concerns
  • Customer complaints

External Sources

  • Market changes
  • Competition
  • Government regulations

Steps in Problem Formulation

Identify Broad Area
↓
Review Literature
↓
Identify Gap
↓
Define Problem
↓
Set Objectives

Example of Research Problem

Broad Problem

Sales are declining.

Research Problem

What factors are causing declining sales among customers aged 18–25?

Example 1

A retail company investigates reasons for declining repeat purchases.

Example 2

A university studies causes of low student attendance.

Topic 4: Development of Research Hypothesis

Explanation

A hypothesis is a tentative statement or prediction regarding the relationship between variables.

It serves as the foundation for testing and analysis.

Definition

A hypothesis is an assumption made for the purpose of testing its validity.

Characteristics of a Good Hypothesis

  • Clear
  • Specific
  • Testable
  • Logical
  • Measurable

Types of Hypothesis

1. Null Hypothesis (H₀)

States that no relationship exists between variables.

Example

Advertising expenditure has no effect on sales.

2. Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)

States that a relationship exists.

Example

Advertising expenditure positively affects sales.

3. Simple Hypothesis

Involves one independent and one dependent variable.

Example

Training improves employee productivity.

4. Complex Hypothesis

Involves multiple variables.

Example

Training and motivation improve employee productivity and job satisfaction.

5. Directional Hypothesis

Predicts direction of relationship.

Example

Higher customer satisfaction leads to higher loyalty.

6. Non-Directional Hypothesis

States relationship exists without specifying direction.

Example

Customer satisfaction is related to loyalty.

Importance of Business Research

Explanation

Business research helps organizations:

  • Reduce uncertainty
  • Improve decision-making
  • Understand customer needs
  • Identify market opportunities
  • Solve organizational problems
  • Improve operational efficiency

Areas Where Business Research is Used

Area Application
Marketing Consumer behavior analysis
Finance Investment decisions
HR Employee satisfaction
Operations Process improvement
Strategy Market expansion

Example 1

Amazon conducts continuous customer research to improve shopping experiences.

Example 2

A local restaurant surveys customers to improve menu offerings.

Quick Revision Summary

  • Business research is a systematic investigation for decision-making.
  • Research may be basic, applied, exploratory, descriptive, or causal.
  • The research process follows a structured sequence.
  • A research problem defines the issue to be investigated.
  • A hypothesis is a testable assumption about variables.
  • Business research reduces uncertainty and improves organizational performance.
