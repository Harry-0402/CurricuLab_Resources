Unit 1: Introduction to Business Research

Unit Overview

Business Research is a systematic process of collecting, analyzing, and interpreting information to solve business problems and support decision-making. This unit introduces the fundamentals of research, different types of research, the research process, formulation of research problems, and development of hypotheses.

Key Concepts

1. Types of Research

Definition

Research can be classified based on its purpose, objective, and methodology used to solve a problem or generate knowledge.

Key Points

Based on Purpose

  • Basic (Pure) Research
    • Expands knowledge.
    • No immediate practical application.
  • Applied Research
    • Solves specific business problems.
    • Directly useful for decision-making.

Based on Objective

  • Exploratory Research
    • Conducted when the problem is not clearly defined.
    • Generates ideas and insights.
  • Descriptive Research
    • Describes characteristics of a phenomenon.
    • Answers "What", "Who", "When", and "Where".
  • Causal Research
    • Examines cause-and-effect relationships.

Based on Nature of Data

  • Qualitative Research
    • Non-numerical data.
    • Focuses on opinions, attitudes, and behavior.
  • Quantitative Research
    • Numerical data.
    • Uses statistical techniques.

Example

A company surveys customers to understand reasons for declining sales. This is applied and exploratory research.

2. Process of Research

Definition

The research process is a systematic sequence of steps followed to conduct research effectively.

Key Points

  1. Identify Problem
  2. Review Literature
  3. Formulate Objectives
  4. Develop Hypothesis
  5. Research Design
  6. Data Collection
  7. Data Analysis
  8. Interpretation
  9. Report Writing

3. Formulation of Research Problem

Definition

A research problem is a specific issue, difficulty, contradiction, or gap in knowledge that requires investigation.

Key Points

  • Foundation of the entire research study.
  • Clearly defines what is to be studied.
  • Guides data collection and analysis.
  • Must be specific and measurable.

Characteristics of a Good Research Problem

Characteristic Description
Clear Easy to understand
Specific Focused on a particular issue
Researchable Data can be collected
Relevant Useful for decision-making
Feasible Achievable within available resources

Sources of Research Problems

  • Business challenges
  • Previous studies
  • Industry trends
  • Customer complaints
  • Managerial decisions

Example

"Why has employee turnover increased by 20% in the last year?"

This is a clear and measurable research problem.

4. Development of Research Hypothesis

Definition

A hypothesis is a tentative statement or assumption about the relationship between two or more variables that can be tested scientifically.

Key Points

  • Acts as a guide for research.
  • Predicts possible outcomes.
  • Must be testable and measurable.
  • Based on theory, observation, or previous studies.

Characteristics of a Good Hypothesis

  • Clear
  • Specific
  • Testable
  • Logical
  • Relevant

Types of Hypothesis

Type Meaning
Null Hypothesis (H₀) No relationship exists
Alternative Hypothesis (H₁) Relationship exists
Simple Hypothesis One independent and one dependent variable
Complex Hypothesis Multiple variables involved
Directional Hypothesis Specifies direction of relationship
Non-Directional Hypothesis Indicates relationship but not direction

Example

Null Hypothesis (H₀)

Customer satisfaction has no impact on customer loyalty.

Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)

Customer satisfaction positively impacts customer loyalty.

Importance of Hypothesis

  • Provides focus.
  • Guides data collection.
  • Facilitates statistical testing.
  • Helps draw conclusions.

Important Definitions

  • Research – Systematic investigation to discover or verify knowledge.
  • Business Research – Research conducted to solve business problems and support decisions.
  • Research Problem – A specific issue requiring investigation.
  • Hypothesis – A tentative statement to be tested.
  • Exploratory Research – Research conducted to gain initial insights.
  • Descriptive Research – Research describing characteristics of a phenomenon.
  • Causal Research – Research examining cause-and-effect relationships.
  • Qualitative Research – Research based on non-numerical data.
  • Quantitative Research – Research based on numerical data.

5-Mark Revision Summary

  • Business research supports managerial decision-making.
  • Research may be exploratory, descriptive, causal, qualitative, or quantitative.
  • Research follows a systematic process from problem identification to report writing.
  • A research problem is the starting point of any study.
  • A hypothesis is a testable assumption regarding variables.
