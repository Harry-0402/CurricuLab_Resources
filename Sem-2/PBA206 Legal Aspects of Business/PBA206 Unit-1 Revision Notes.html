Unit 1: Law of Contract – The Indian Contract Act, 1872

Unit Overview

The Indian Contract Act, 1872 is one of the most important business laws in India. It governs agreements, contracts, rights, obligations, and remedies available to parties. A valid contract forms the foundation of business transactions. This unit covers essential elements of contracts, performance, discharge, breach, indemnity, guarantee, bailment, pledge, and agency.

Key Concepts

1. Contract

Definition

According to Section 2(h) of the Indian Contract Act, 1872:

"A contract is an agreement enforceable by law."

Formula

Agreement
+
Legal Enforceability
====================

Contract

Example

A agrees to sell his bike to B for ₹50,000 and B agrees. This becomes a contract if all legal requirements are satisfied.

4. Offer and Acceptance

Offer

Definition

A proposal made by one person to another expressing willingness to do or abstain from doing something.

Essentials of a Valid Offer

  • Must be clear and definite.
  • Must be communicated.
  • Must create legal relations.
  • Can be express or implied.

Acceptance

Definition

Acceptance means giving assent to an offer.

Essentials

  • Must be absolute and unconditional.
  • Must be communicated.
  • Must be within prescribed time.
  • Must be made by the offeree.

Example

A offers to sell a laptop for ₹40,000. B agrees. This is acceptance.

5. Consideration

Definition

Consideration means something in return for a promise.

Principle

"No Consideration, No Contract"

Essentials

  • Must move at the desire of the promisor.
  • May be past, present, or future.
  • Need not be adequate.
  • Must be lawful.

Example

A sells a car to B for ₹5 lakh. Money paid is consideration.

6. Capacity to Contract

Definition

A person must be legally competent to enter into a contract.

Competent Person

According to Section 11:

  • Major (18 years or above)
  • Sound mind
  • Not disqualified by law

Persons Not Competent

  • Minors
  • Persons of unsound mind
  • Disqualified persons

Example

A contract with a minor is void.

7. Free Consent

Definition

Consent is free when it is not caused by coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, or mistake.

Factors Affecting Free Consent

Factor Meaning
Coercion Force or threat
Undue Influence Dominating another person
Fraud Intentional deception
Misrepresentation False statement without intent
Mistake Wrong belief about facts

Example

Obtaining a signature through threats amounts to coercion.

8. Legality of Object

Definition

The purpose of a contract must be lawful.

Unlawful Objects

  • Illegal activities
  • Fraudulent acts
  • Immoral activities
  • Against public policy

Example

A contract for smuggling goods is void.

9. Contingent Contracts

Definition

A contract dependent upon the occurrence or non-occurrence of a future uncertain event.

Example

A promises to pay B ₹50,000 if B's ship arrives safely.

Characteristics

  • Depends on uncertain event.
  • Event must be collateral.
  • Enforceable only when event occurs.

10. Performance of Contract

Definition

Performance means fulfilling contractual obligations.

Types

Actual Performance

Parties perform obligations completely.

Attempted Performance (Tender)

Offer to perform obligations even if not accepted.

Example

Supplier delivers goods as promised.

Termination of contractual obligations.

Modes of Discharge

Contract ends after both parties fulfill obligations.

12. Quasi Contract

Definition

An obligation imposed by law even without a formal contract.

Features

  • No actual agreement.
  • Prevents unjust enrichment.
  • Created by law.

Example

A person mistakenly receives another person's goods and must return them.

13. Remedies for Breach of Contract

Definition

Legal solutions available to an aggrieved party when a contract is breached.

Remedies

Remedy Meaning
Damages Monetary compensation
Specific Performance Court orders performance
Injunction Restrains certain acts
Rescission Contract cancellation
Quantum Meruit Payment for work done

Example

Supplier fails to deliver goods; buyer claims damages.

14. Contract of Indemnity

Definition

A contract where one party promises to compensate another for loss.

Parties

Party Role
Indemnifier Gives compensation
Indemnified Receives compensation

Example

Insurance contracts are contracts of indemnity.

15. Contract of Guarantee

Definition

A contract to perform the promise or discharge the liability of a third person in case of default.

Parties

Party Role
Surety Gives guarantee
Principal Debtor Original borrower
Creditor Lender

Example

A guarantees repayment of B's loan.

Difference: Indemnity vs Guarantee

Basis Indemnity Guarantee
Parties Two Three
Liability Primary Secondary
Purpose Compensation for loss Assurance of payment
Example Insurance Bank Guarantee

16. Bailment

Definition

Transfer of goods by one person to another for a specific purpose with an obligation to return them.

Parties

Party Role
Bailor Owner giving goods
Bailee Person receiving goods

Example

Giving a car to a garage for repairs.

17. Pledge

Definition

Pledge is bailment of goods as security for repayment of debt.

Parties

Party Role
Pawnor Gives goods
Pawnee Receives goods

Example

Gold pledged to a bank for a loan.

Difference: Bailment vs Pledge

Basis Bailment Pledge
Purpose Any lawful purpose Security for debt
Ownership Remains with bailor Remains with pawnor
Sale of Goods Not allowed Allowed on default

18. Law of Agency

Definition

Agency is a relationship where one person acts on behalf of another.

Parties

Party Role
Principal Person represented
Agent Person acting

Creation of Agency

  • Express Agreement
  • Implied Agreement
  • Necessity
  • Ratification

Duties of Agent

  • Follow instructions.
  • Maintain accounts.
  • Act honestly.
  • Protect principal's interest.

Example

A travel agent booking tickets on behalf of a customer.

Important Definitions

  • Contract – Agreement enforceable by law.
  • Offer – Proposal made by one party.
  • Acceptance – Assent to the offer.
  • Consideration – Something in return.
  • Free Consent – Consent without coercion, fraud, etc.
  • Contingent Contract – Depends on uncertain future event.
  • Quasi Contract – Obligation created by law.
  • Indemnity – Compensation for loss.
  • Guarantee – Promise to answer for another's default.
  • Bailment – Delivery of goods for a purpose.
  • Pledge – Bailment as security for debt.
  • Agency – Relationship where one acts for another.
