Unit 3: Company Law – The Indian Companies Act, 1956

Unit Overview

A company is one of the most important forms of business organization. The Indian Companies Act, 1956 governs the formation, management, and winding up of companies. A company has a separate legal identity, perpetual succession, and limited liability. This unit covers company formation, MOA, AOA, prospectus, share capital, directors, company meetings, and winding up.

Key Concepts

1. Company

Definition

A company is an artificial legal person created by law, having a separate legal entity, perpetual succession, and common seal.

Characteristics of a Company

  • Separate Legal Entity
  • Perpetual Succession
  • Limited Liability
  • Common Seal
  • Transferability of Shares
  • Separate Property Ownership

Example

A company continues to exist even if its shareholders change.

2. Types of Companies

Based on Liability

A. Company Limited by Shares

Members' liability is limited to unpaid share value.

B. Company Limited by Guarantee

Members guarantee a fixed amount in case of winding up.

C. Unlimited Company

Members have unlimited liability.

Based on Ownership

Type Meaning
Private Company Restricts share transfer
Public Company Shares offered to public
Government Company Government owns majority shares
Foreign Company Incorporated outside India

Stages of Formation

Incorporation
↓

A. Promotion

Definition

Promotion is the process of identifying business opportunities and establishing a company.

Promoter

A person who undertakes the formation of a company.

Functions of Promoters

  • Idea generation.
  • Feasibility study.
  • Preparation of documents.
  • Arranging capital.
  • Registration formalities.

B. Incorporation

Definition

Legal registration of a company under the Companies Act.

Documents Required

  • Memorandum of Association (MOA)
  • Articles of Association (AOA)
  • Consent of Directors
  • Registration Forms

Result

Certificate of Incorporation issued by Registrar of Companies (ROC).

4. Memorandum of Association (MOA)

Definition

MOA is the fundamental document that defines the scope and objectives of a company.

Importance

  • Governs company's activities.
  • Defines relationship with outsiders.
  • Limits company powers.

Clauses of MOA

Clause Purpose
Name Clause Company's name
Registered Office Clause Location
Object Clause Business objectives
Liability Clause Liability of members
Capital Clause Authorized capital
Subscription Clause Initial subscribers

5. Articles of Association (AOA)

Definition

AOA contains rules and regulations for internal management of the company.

Contents

  • Share transfer rules.
  • Director powers.
  • Meeting procedures.
  • Dividend policies.
  • Voting rights.

Difference Between MOA and AOA

Basis MOA AOA
Purpose Defines objectives Internal management
Scope External relationship Internal relationship
Position Supreme document Subordinate to MOA
Mandatory Yes Yes (most companies)

6. Prospectus

Definition

A prospectus is a document issued by a public company inviting the public to subscribe to its shares.

Contents

  • Company information.
  • Capital structure.
  • Financial details.
  • Risk factors.
  • Objectives of issue.

Importance

  • Provides investment information.
  • Protects investors.
  • Ensures transparency.

Example

A company issuing an IPO publishes a prospectus.

8. Shares and Share Capital

Share

A share represents ownership in a company.

Types of Shares

Equity Shares

  • Voting rights.
  • Variable dividends.

Preference Shares

  • Fixed dividend.
  • Priority during liquidation.

Share Capital

Type Meaning
Authorized Capital Maximum capital permitted
Issued Capital Offered to public
Subscribed Capital Accepted by investors
Paid-up Capital Amount received by company

9. Promoters

Definition

Promoters are persons who undertake the formation of a company.

Duties

  • Act honestly.
  • Avoid secret profits.
  • Disclose material facts.
  • Protect company interests.

Liabilities

  • Misrepresentation.
  • Fraud.
  • Breach of duty.

10. Membership of a Company

Member

A person whose name appears in the register of members.

Ways of Becoming a Member

  • Subscription to MOA.
  • Share allotment.
  • Share transfer.
  • Transmission by law.

Rights of Members

  • Vote in meetings.
  • Receive dividends.
  • Inspect records.
  • Share in surplus assets.

11. Meetings and Proceedings

Importance

Meetings allow members to participate in company decisions.

Types of Meetings

Statutory Meeting

Held once after incorporation.

Annual General Meeting (AGM)

Held every year.

Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM)

Held for urgent matters.

Board Meetings

Meetings of directors.

12. Directors

Definition

Directors are persons elected to manage company affairs.

Functions

  • Policy formulation.
  • Decision making.
  • Resource management.
  • Compliance monitoring.

Duties

  • Act in good faith.
  • Avoid conflicts of interest.
  • Protect company assets.

Position of Directors

Directors act as:

  • Agents
  • Trustees
  • Officers of the company

13. Managers and Their Remuneration

Manager

An individual responsible for day-to-day management under the control of the Board.

Remuneration

May include:

  • Salary
  • Bonus
  • Commission
  • Incentives

14. Company Secretary

Definition

A Company Secretary is a key managerial officer responsible for legal and administrative compliance.

Functions

  • Maintain records.
  • Conduct meetings.
  • Ensure legal compliance.
  • Advise management.

Importance

Acts as a link between management, shareholders, and regulators.

15. Prevention of Oppression and Mismanagement

Oppression

Unfair treatment of minority shareholders.

Examples

  • Denial of voting rights.
  • Unfair decisions.

Mismanagement

Conduct of company affairs in a harmful manner.

Examples

  • Financial irregularities.
  • Misuse of company resources.

Remedies

  • Tribunal intervention.
  • Regulatory action.
  • Management restructuring.

16. Winding Up of a Company

Definition

Winding up is the legal process of closing a company and distributing its assets.

Modes of Winding Up

Compulsory Winding Up

Ordered by the court/tribunal.

Voluntary Winding Up

Initiated by members or creditors.

Winding Up Process
