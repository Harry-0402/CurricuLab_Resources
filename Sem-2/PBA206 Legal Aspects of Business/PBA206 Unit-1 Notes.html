1. Nature and Kinds of Contracts

Introduction

The Indian Contract Act, 1872 is one of the most important business laws in India. It governs how agreements are formed, enforced, and performed between individuals or organizations. In everyday business activities—such as buying goods, hiring employees, leasing property, or borrowing money—contracts form the legal foundation.

A contract ensures that promises made between parties are legally binding and enforceable by law.

The Act applies to the whole of India and lays down the rules relating to agreements, contracts, performance, and remedies for breach.

Key Concepts

  • Contract

  • Agreement

  • Promise

  • Offer and Acceptance

  • Legal Obligation

  • Void Agreement

  • Voidable Contract

Meaning of Contract

According to Section 2(h) of the Indian Contract Act, 1872:

“A contract is an agreement enforceable by law.”

This definition highlights two important elements:

ElementMeaning
AgreementA promise or set of promises between parties
Enforceable by lawThe agreement must be legally binding

Thus, every contract begins with an agreement, but not every agreement becomes a contract.

Meaning of Agreement

According to Section 2(e):

“Every promise and every set of promises forming consideration for each other is an agreement.”

Example

Rahul promises to sell his laptop to Aman for ₹20,000 and Aman agrees.

  • Rahul’s promise → sell laptop

  • Aman’s promise → pay ₹20,000

This mutual promise creates an agreement.

If the agreement satisfies legal requirements, it becomes a contract.

Relationship Between Agreement and Contract

Flow Representation

Offer → Acceptance → Agreement → Legal enforceability → Contract

So:

All contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts.

Example

Social Agreement (Not a Contract)

A friend invites another friend to dinner.

If the friend does not attend, the agreement cannot be enforced in court.

Therefore, it is not a contract.

Legal Agreement (Contract)

A supplier agrees to deliver goods worth ₹50,000 to a retailer.

If the supplier fails to deliver goods, the retailer can sue in court.

Therefore, it is a contract.

2. Types (Kinds) of Contracts

Contracts can be classified on different bases.

Classification Based on Validity

TypeMeaning
Valid ContractLegally enforceable agreement
Void ContractAgreement not enforceable by law
Voidable ContractEnforceable at the option of one party
Illegal ContractAgreement forbidden by law
Unenforceable ContractValid but cannot be enforced due to technical reasons

1. Valid Contract

A valid contract satisfies all legal requirements of the Indian Contract Act.

Essential conditions include:

  • Offer and acceptance

  • Free consent

  • Lawful consideration

  • Capacity of parties

  • Lawful object

Example

A agrees to sell his car to B for ₹3,00,000.

Both parties agree voluntarily.

This is a valid contract.

2. Void Contract

According to Section 2(j):

A void contract is a contract that ceases to be enforceable by law.

A contract may become void when:

  • It becomes impossible to perform

  • The law changes

  • Essential conditions fail

Example

A agrees to import goods from another country.

Later the government bans imports of those goods.

The contract becomes void.

3. Voidable Contract

According to Section 2(i):

A contract is voidable when it is enforceable at the option of one party but not the other.

This usually happens when consent is obtained by:

  • Coercion

  • Fraud

  • Misrepresentation

  • Undue influence

Example

A forces B to sell property.

B may either:

  • Accept the contract, or

  • Cancel it

Thus the contract is voidable at B’s option.

4. Illegal Contract

An agreement is illegal if its purpose is unlawful or forbidden by law.

Example

A hires B to steal confidential business documents.

This contract is illegal and void from the beginning.

Characteristics:

  • Not enforceable

  • Punishable under law

  • Related agreements also become void

5. Unenforceable Contract

An unenforceable contract is otherwise valid but cannot be enforced due to technical defects.

Example

A property sale agreement not registered or stamped properly.

The agreement exists but cannot be enforced in court.

Classification Based on Formation

TypeMeaning
Express ContractTerms stated clearly
Implied ContractFormed by conduct
Quasi ContractNot actual contract but imposed by law

1. Express Contract

Terms are clearly stated either orally or in writing.

Example

Signing an employment agreement.

2. Implied Contract

Formed through actions or conduct of parties.

Example

When a person boards a bus and pays the fare, an implied contract exists between passenger and transport company.

3. Quasi Contract

Quasi contracts are not actual contracts but obligations imposed by law to prevent unjust enrichment.

Example

A person finds lost goods and keeps them safe.

Law imposes responsibility similar to a contract.

Classification Based on Performance

TypeMeaning
Executed ContractFully performed
Executory ContractPerformance pending
Unilateral ContractOne party performs
Bilateral ContractBoth parties promise

1. Executed Contract

Both parties complete their obligations immediately.

Example:
Buying goods and paying cash.

2. Executory Contract

Performance is to be completed in future.

Example:
Agreement to deliver goods next month.

3. Unilateral Contract

Only one party makes a promise.

Example:
Reward for finding lost dog.

4. Bilateral Contract

Both parties exchange promises.

Example:
Seller promises to deliver goods; buyer promises to pay price.

Summary of This Section

  • The Indian Contract Act, 1872 governs agreements and contracts in India.

  • A contract = agreement + legal enforceability.

  • Not all agreements are contracts.

  • Contracts are classified based on:

    • Validity

    • Formation

    • Performance

  • Types include valid, void, voidable, illegal, express, implied, unilateral, bilateral, etc.

3. Essential Elements of a Valid Contract

Introduction

For an agreement to become a legally enforceable contract, it must satisfy certain conditions laid down in the Indian Contract Act, 1872. These conditions are known as the essential elements of a valid contract.

If any of these elements are missing, the agreement may become void, voidable, or unenforceable.

Key Concepts

  • Valid Contract

  • Offer and Acceptance

  • Lawful Consideration

  • Free Consent

  • Competent Parties

  • Lawful Object

  • Legal Obligation

Essential Elements (Overview)

ElementExplanation
Offer and AcceptanceOne party proposes, another accepts
Intention to Create Legal RelationsParties must intend legal consequences
Lawful ConsiderationSomething of value must be exchanged
Capacity of PartiesParties must be legally competent
Free ConsentAgreement must be voluntary
Lawful ObjectPurpose must not be illegal
Certainty of TermsTerms must be clear
Possibility of PerformanceContract must be capable of performance
Not Declared VoidAgreement must not fall under void agreements

1. Offer and Acceptance

Introduction

The first requirement of a valid contract is offer and acceptance. A contract begins when one party makes a proposal and the other party accepts it.

According to Section 2(a) of the Indian Contract Act:

“When one person signifies to another his willingness to do or abstain from doing something with a view to obtaining the assent of that other, he is said to make a proposal.”

Meaning of Offer

An offer is a proposal made by one party to another expressing willingness to enter into a contract.

The person making the offer is called the Offeror (Promisor) and the person accepting it is called the Offeree (Promisee).

Example

A says to B:

“I will sell my motorcycle to you for ₹50,000.”

This is an offer.

If B says “Yes, I accept”, the contract is formed.

Types of Offers

TypeExplanation
Express OfferMade by words (spoken or written)
Implied OfferMade by conduct
Specific OfferMade to a particular person
General OfferMade to the public
Cross OfferTwo identical offers made by parties
Counter OfferOffer made in response modifying original terms

1. Express Offer

Made clearly through spoken or written words.

Example
A sends an email offering to sell furniture for ₹20,000.

2. Implied Offer

Made through actions or behavior.

Example
A bus running on a route implies an offer to carry passengers.

3. General Offer

Made to the public at large.

Anyone fulfilling the conditions can accept the offer.

Example (Famous Case)

Carlill v. Carbolic Smoke Ball Co. (1893)

A company promised ₹100 to anyone who used its medicine and still got influenza.

When Mrs. Carlill used the product and got sick, the court held the company liable to pay ₹100.

4. Specific Offer

Made to a specific person.

Only that person can accept it.

Example
A offers to sell his laptop only to B.

5. Counter Offer

When the offeree modifies the terms of the original offer.

Example

A: I will sell my car for ₹3,00,000.
B: I will buy it for ₹2,50,000.

This is not acceptance, but a counter offer.

2. Acceptance

Meaning

According to Section 2(b):

When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent, the proposal becomes a promise.

Thus, offer + acceptance = agreement.

Essential Rules of Valid Acceptance

RuleExplanation
Must be absolute and unconditionalCannot change terms
Must be communicatedSilence is not acceptance
Must be in prescribed mannerFollow specified method
Must be given within reasonable timeDelayed acceptance invalid

Example

A offers to sell goods for ₹10,000.

If B replies:

“I accept your offer.”

This becomes valid acceptance.

But if B replies:

“I accept for ₹8,000.”

It becomes counter offer, not acceptance.

Communication of Acceptance

Acceptance must be communicated to the offeror.

Example

A sends offer letter to B.

B writes acceptance letter and posts it.

Contract is formed when the acceptance is dispatched.

3. Intention to Create Legal Relations

Introduction

The parties must intend that the agreement should create legal obligations.

Social or domestic agreements generally do not create legal relations.

Example

Social Agreement

A promises to take B to a movie.

If A fails, B cannot sue in court.

Business Agreement

A agrees to supply goods worth ₹1,00,000 to B.

If A fails, B can file a lawsuit.

Thus business agreements create legal intention.

4. Lawful Consideration

Meaning

According to Section 2(d):

Consideration is something of value given in exchange for a promise.

It may be:

  • Money

  • Goods

  • Service

  • Promise

Example

A sells his phone to B for ₹15,000.

  • Phone → consideration for B

  • ₹15,000 → consideration for A

Important Rule

“No consideration, no contract.”

However, there are some exceptions, such as:

  • Agreements made out of natural love and affection

  • Promise to pay a time-barred debt

  • Completed gifts

Visual Flow of Contract Formation

Offer → Acceptance → Agreement → Consideration + Legal Intention → Contract

Summary of This Section

  • A valid contract requires essential elements.

  • The most fundamental elements include:

    • Offer

    • Acceptance

    • Intention to create legal relations

    • Lawful consideration

  • Acceptance must be clear, unconditional, and communicated.

  • Consideration means something of value exchanged between parties.

5. Capacity to Contract

Introduction

For a contract to be valid, the parties entering into the contract must be competent or capable of understanding the agreement and its consequences. If a person lacks legal capacity, the agreement may become void or unenforceable.

According to Section 11 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, every person is competent to contract if they:

  1. Have attained the age of majority

  2. Are of sound mind

  3. Are not disqualified by law

Key Concepts

  • Competent parties

  • Minor

  • Person of unsound mind

  • Disqualified person

1. Minor

A minor is a person who has not attained the age of 18 years (as per Indian Majority Act).

Rule

A minor’s agreement is void from the beginning (void ab initio).

Famous Case

Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose (1903)

A minor mortgaged property to borrow money. Later the minor refused to repay the loan. The court held that the agreement was void, since the minor had no capacity to contract.

Important Points About Minor’s Agreements

RuleExplanation
Agreement with minorVoid
Minor cannot sueGenerally cannot enforce contract
Minor cannot be made liableNo legal obligation
Minor can be beneficiaryCan receive benefits

Example

A minor purchases a bicycle on credit.

The seller cannot force the minor to pay, because the contract is void.

2. Person of Unsound Mind

A person is considered of sound mind if they:

  • Understand the contract

  • Can form rational judgment regarding its effects

According to Section 12, a person is of sound mind when they are capable of understanding the contract at the time of making it.

Persons Considered Unsound

Examples include:

  • Lunatics

  • Idiots

  • Drunken persons (temporarily)

Example

If a person signs a contract while heavily intoxicated, and cannot understand the contract, it may be declared invalid.

3. Persons Disqualified by Law

Some persons are legally prohibited from entering into contracts.

Examples include:

  • Alien enemies

  • Insolvents

  • Convicts

  • Foreign sovereigns without permission

  • Corporations beyond their powers

Example

A person declared insolvent by court cannot enter contracts related to property management.

6. Free Consent

Introduction

Consent means that both parties agree to the same thing in the same sense.

According to Section 13:

Two persons are said to consent when they agree upon the same thing in the same sense.

This concept is called Consensus ad idem.

Example

A agrees to sell his car to B.

B believes A is selling his bike.

Here there is no real consent, because both parties mean different things.

When Consent is Not Free

Consent is not free if it is obtained by:

FactorSection
CoercionSection 15
Undue InfluenceSection 16
FraudSection 17
MisrepresentationSection 18
MistakeSection 20

1. Coercion

Meaning

According to Section 15, coercion means committing or threatening to commit any act forbidden by law to force someone into a contract.

Example

A threatens to harm B unless B signs a contract.

The contract is voidable because consent was obtained by coercion.

2. Undue Influence

Meaning

Undue influence occurs when one party dominates the will of another and uses that position unfairly.

Example Relationships

Undue influence often exists in relationships like:

  • Doctor and patient

  • Teacher and student

  • Parent and child

  • Lawyer and client

Example

A doctor convinces a patient to transfer property under pressure.

The contract may be voidable.

3. Fraud

Meaning

According to Section 17, fraud means intentional deception to induce another party into a contract.

Examples of Fraud

  • False statements

  • Active concealment of facts

  • Promise made without intention to perform

Example

A sells a car claiming it is accident-free, while knowing it has major accident damage.

This is fraud.

4. Misrepresentation

Misrepresentation occurs when false statements are made without intention to deceive.

Example

A seller mistakenly believes a machine is new and tells the buyer so.

If it turns out to be used, the contract may be voidable.

5. Mistake

A mistake occurs when parties misunderstand facts.

Types of Mistake

TypeMeaning
Bilateral MistakeBoth parties mistaken
Unilateral MistakeOne party mistaken

Example

A agrees to sell a specific horse to B.

Unknown to both, the horse had already died.

The agreement becomes void.

Visual Overview of Free Consent

   ↓
Free Consent

No fraud

Summary of This Section

  • Parties must have capacity to contract.

  • Persons competent to contract include:

    • Adults

    • Persons of sound mind

    • Persons not disqualified by law.

  • Minors cannot enter valid contracts.

  • Free consent is essential for validity.

  • Consent must not be obtained through:

    • Coercion

    • Undue influence

    • Fraud

    • Misrepresentation

    • Mistake.

7. Legality of Object

Introduction

For a contract to be valid, the purpose (object) of the agreement must be lawful. If the objective of the contract is illegal or forbidden by law, the agreement becomes void.

According to Section 23 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, the consideration or object of an agreement is lawful unless:

  • It is forbidden by law

  • It defeats the provisions of law

  • It involves fraud

  • It causes injury to a person or property

  • It is considered immoral

  • It is opposed to public policy

If any of these conditions exist, the agreement becomes void.

Key Concepts

  • Lawful object

  • Illegal agreement

  • Immoral agreements

  • Agreements against public policy

When Object is Unlawful

SituationExplanation
Forbidden by lawDirectly prohibited by law
Defeats provisions of lawCircumvents legal rules
FraudulentIntended to deceive
Injury to othersCauses harm to property/person
ImmoralAgainst moral standards
Against public policyHarmful to society

1. Forbidden by Law

An agreement is unlawful if it involves an act prohibited by law.

Example

A agrees to pay B ₹10,000 to commit theft.

This agreement is illegal and void.

2. Defeats Provisions of Law

If an agreement attempts to bypass or avoid the law, it becomes void.

Example

An employer asks a worker to sign a contract agreeing to work without wages to avoid labour laws.

Such an agreement is void.

3. Fraudulent Agreements

Agreements designed to cheat or deceive others are unlawful.

Example

Two people create a fake agreement to cheat a bank into granting a loan.

This contract is void due to fraud.

4. Agreements Causing Injury

If the contract causes harm to a person or their property, it becomes void.

Example

A agrees to pay B to damage C’s shop.

This agreement is illegal.

5. Immoral Agreements

Contracts that violate accepted moral standards of society are void.

Example:

Agreements involving prostitution or immoral activities.

6. Agreements Against Public Policy

Contracts that are harmful to society or public interest are void.

Examples include:

  • Agreements restraining marriage

  • Agreements interfering with justice

  • Agreements restricting trade

8. Contingent Contracts

Introduction

A contingent contract is a contract whose performance depends on the occurrence or non-occurrence of an uncertain future event.

According to Section 31 of the Indian Contract Act:

“A contingent contract is a contract to do or not to do something if some event collateral to the contract happens or does not happen.”

Key Features

FeatureExplanation
Depends on eventFuture uncertain event
Event must be collateralNot part of contract itself
Conditional performanceContract executed only if event occurs

Example

A agrees to pay B ₹50,000 if B’s house catches fire.

The contract becomes enforceable only if the fire occurs.

Rules of Contingent Contracts

RuleExplanation
Event must happenContract enforced when event occurs
Event must not happenContract enforced when event becomes impossible
Impossible eventContract becomes void

Example

A promises to pay B ₹10,000 if India wins a cricket match tomorrow.

  • If India wins → Contract valid

  • If India loses → Contract void

9. Performance of Contract

Introduction

After a valid contract is formed, the parties must fulfil their promises. This act of fulfilling contractual obligations is called performance of contract.

According to Section 37, parties to a contract must either:

  • Perform their promises, or

  • Offer to perform them.

Types of Performance

TypeMeaning
Actual PerformanceParties fulfill obligations
Attempted PerformanceOffer to perform but rejected

1. Actual Performance

Actual performance occurs when both parties complete their obligations.

Example

A agrees to deliver goods and B agrees to pay ₹20,000.

If A delivers goods and B pays money → contract performed.

2. Attempted Performance (Tender)

Attempted performance occurs when one party offers to perform but the other party refuses.

Example:

A offers to deliver goods to B.

B refuses to accept them.

A is not responsible for non-performance.

Who Must Perform the Contract?

The following persons may perform a contract:

PersonExplanation
PromisorPerson who made promise
AgentAuthorized representative
Legal representativeIf promisor dies
Third partyIf accepted by promisee

Contracts That Must Be Performed Personally

Some contracts require personal skills or qualifications.

Example:

  • Painting by a famous artist

  • Music performance by a singer

Such contracts cannot be performed by someone else.

Flow of Contract Execution

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
Contract Completed

Summary of This Section

  • The object of a contract must be lawful.

  • Agreements involving illegal, immoral, or fraudulent purposes are void.

  • Contingent contracts depend on uncertain future events.

  • Performance of contract means fulfilling contractual obligations.

  • Performance may be actual or attempted.

10. Discharge of Contract

Introduction

A contract is said to be discharged when the obligations of the parties come to an end. Once a contract is discharged, the parties are no longer bound to perform their promises.

Discharge can occur in several ways such as performance, agreement, impossibility, lapse of time, or breach.

Key Concepts

  • Discharge of contract

  • Performance

  • Mutual agreement

  • Impossibility of performance

  • Breach of contract

Modes of Discharge of Contract

ModeExplanation
PerformanceContract completed by fulfilling obligations
Mutual AgreementParties agree to cancel or modify contract
ImpossibilityPerformance becomes impossible
Lapse of TimeLimitation period expires
Breach of ContractOne party fails to perform

1. Discharge by Performance

Performance is the most common method of discharging a contract.

When both parties fulfill their promises, the contract is completed.

Example

A agrees to sell goods for ₹20,000.

  • A delivers goods

  • B pays ₹20,000

The contract is discharged by performance.

2. Discharge by Mutual Agreement

Parties may agree to cancel or modify the contract.

Forms of discharge by agreement include:

TypeMeaning
NovationReplacement of old contract with new contract
RescissionCancellation of contract
AlterationChanging terms of contract
RemissionAcceptance of lesser performance

Example (Novation)

A owes B ₹10,000.

Later C agrees to pay B instead of A.

A new contract replaces the old one.

3. Discharge by Impossibility of Performance

Sometimes performance becomes impossible due to unforeseen circumstances.

This is called Doctrine of Frustration.

Examples

SituationResult
Death of partyPersonal contract ends
Change in lawContract becomes void
Destruction of subject matterPerformance impossible

Example

A agrees to rent a hall for a concert.

Before the event, the hall burns down.

The contract becomes void due to impossibility.

4. Discharge by Lapse of Time

Every contract must be enforced within a specified time limit under the Limitation Act.

If a party fails to take legal action within this time, the contract becomes unenforceable.

Example

If a debt is not recovered within 3 years, the creditor cannot file a legal suit.

The contract is discharged by lapse of time.

5. Discharge by Breach of Contract

A breach occurs when a party fails to perform obligations.

Types of Breach

TypeExplanation
Actual BreachFailure to perform on due date
Anticipatory BreachRefusal before due date

Actual Breach

Occurs when a party does not perform on the agreed date.

Example:

A agrees to deliver goods on 1 July but fails to deliver.

Anticipatory Breach

Occurs when a party declares beforehand that they will not perform.

Example:

A agrees to supply goods next month but informs B today that he will not deliver.

11. Quasi Contracts

Introduction

A quasi contract is not a real contract but an obligation created by law to prevent unjust enrichment.

Even though no agreement exists, the law treats the situation as if a contract exists.

Key Principle

No person should benefit unfairly at the expense of another.

Examples of Quasi Contracts

SituationExplanation
Supply of necessitiesSupplier can claim payment
Payment by interested personPerson paying another’s debt can recover
Finder of lost goodsFinder has rights similar to bailee
Money paid by mistakeMust be returned

Example

A mistakenly transfers ₹5,000 to B’s account.

B must return the money, even though there was no contract.

12. Remedies for Breach of Contract

Introduction

When one party breaches a contract, the other party is entitled to legal remedies.

A remedy is the legal solution provided by court to protect the injured party.

Types of Remedies

RemedyMeaning
DamagesMonetary compensation
Specific PerformanceCourt orders performance
InjunctionCourt prevents certain action
RescissionContract cancelled
Quantum MeruitPayment for work done

1. Damages

Damages are financial compensation awarded to the injured party.

Types of damages include:

TypeExplanation
Ordinary damagesDirect loss
Special damagesSpecial circumstances
Nominal damagesSmall amount when loss minimal
Exemplary damagesPunitive damages

Example

A fails to deliver goods promised to B.

B buys similar goods at higher price.

The extra amount paid can be claimed as damages.

2. Specific Performance

Sometimes monetary damages are not enough.

The court may order the party to perform the contract.

Example:

Sale of unique property or land.

3. Injunction

An injunction is a court order preventing a party from doing something.

Example:

A singer agrees to perform only for one event organizer but tries to perform elsewhere.

Court may issue an injunction.

4. Rescission

The injured party may cancel the contract.

Example:

Contract formed by fraud or coercion.

5. Quantum Meruit

Meaning: “As much as deserved.”

A party can claim payment for work already completed.

Example

A contractor builds half a house before the contract is cancelled.

He can claim payment for the work already done.

Summary of This Section

  • A contract may be discharged in several ways, including performance, agreement, impossibility, lapse of time, or breach.

  • Quasi contracts arise from law to prevent unjust enrichment.

  • When breach occurs, courts provide remedies such as damages, injunction, specific performance, rescission, and quantum meruit.

13. Contract of Indemnity

Introduction

A contract of indemnity is a contract in which one party promises to protect the other party from loss caused by the conduct of the promisor or any other person.

According to Section 124 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872:

“A contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused to him by the conduct of the promisor or by the conduct of any other person is called a contract of indemnity.”

Key Concepts

  • Indemnified / Indemnity holder

  • Protection against loss

Parties in Contract of Indemnity

A agrees to compensate B for any loss caused during a business transaction.

If B suffers loss, A must compensate B.

Compromise paymentsAmount paid in settlement

14. Contract of Guarantee

Introduction

A contract of guarantee involves a promise to perform the obligation or discharge the liability of a third person if that person fails to perform.

A contract of guarantee is a contract to perform the promise or discharge the liability of a third person in case of his default.

15. Bailment

Introduction

A bailment occurs when goods are delivered by one person to another for a specific purpose, with the condition that the goods will be returned after the purpose is completed.

According to Section 148:

A bailment is the delivery of goods for some purpose upon a contract that the goods shall be returned when the purpose is accomplished.

Parties in Bailment

PartyMeaning
BailorPerson delivering goods
BaileePerson receiving goods

Example

A gives his car to a mechanic for repair.

  • A → Bailor

  • Mechanic → Bailee

After repair, the car must be returned.

Duties of Bailor

  • Disclose faults in goods

  • Bear extraordinary expenses

  • Indemnify bailee

Duties of Bailee

  • Take reasonable care of goods

  • Use goods according to contract

  • Return goods after purpose

16. Pledge

Introduction

A pledge is a special type of bailment where goods are delivered as security for a loan or debt.

According to Section 172:

“The bailment of goods as security for payment of debt or performance of promise is called pledge.”

Parties in Pledge

17. Contract of Agency

Introduction

A contract of agency arises when one person authorizes another to act on their behalf in dealings with third parties.

According to Section 182:

An agent is a person employed to do any act for another or represent another in dealings with third persons.

Parties in Agency

PartyMeaning
PrincipalPerson who appoints agent
AgentPerson acting on behalf of principal
Third PartyPerson dealing with agent

Example

A appoints B to sell goods on his behalf.

B sells goods to C.

  • A → Principal

  • B → Agent

  • C → Third Party

Types of Agents

TypeExplanation
General AgentPerforms general business
Special AgentPerforms specific task
BrokerArranges contracts
FactorAgent dealing with goods

Termination of Agency

Agency may end due to:

ReasonExplanation
AgreementParties agree to terminate
Completion of workPurpose fulfilled
Death or insanityPrincipal or agent dies
InsolvencyPrincipal becomes insolvent
