Unit 3: Company Law – Indian Companies Act, 1956

Introduction

A company is one of the most important forms of business organization. It provides a separate legal identity, limited liability, perpetual succession, and the ability to raise large amounts of capital. The Companies Act, 1956 (as prescribed in the syllabus) governs the formation, management, administration, and winding up of companies.

Understanding Company Law is essential for managers, entrepreneurs, investors, and business professionals because companies play a major role in economic development and business activities.

Topic 1: Meaning and Characteristics of a Company

Explanation

A company is an artificial legal person created by law and having a separate legal existence from its members.

According to Company Law, a company can own property, enter into contracts, sue, and be sued in its own name.

Characteristics of a Company

1. Separate Legal Entity

The company is distinct from its shareholders.

2. Perpetual Succession

The company continues to exist regardless of changes in membership.

3. Limited Liability

Members' liability is limited to the amount invested.

4. Common Seal

Acts as the official signature of the company.

5. Transferability of Shares

Shares can generally be transferred.

Example 1 (Business)

A company can own land in its own name even though shareholders own the company.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

If a shareholder dies, the company continues its existence.

Topic 2: Various Forms of Companies

Explanation

Companies can be classified on different bases.

A. On the Basis of Liability

Type Meaning
Company Limited by Shares Liability limited to unpaid share amount
Company Limited by Guarantee Liability limited to guaranteed amount
Unlimited Company Members have unlimited liability

B. On the Basis of Membership

Type Meaning
Private Company Restricts transfer of shares
Public Company Shares freely transferable

C. On the Basis of Ownership

Type Meaning
Government Company Majority ownership by government
Non-Government Company Owned by private individuals

Example 1 (Business)

Many large corporations operate as public companies.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A family-owned company is usually structured as a private company.

Topic 3: Formation of a Company

Explanation

Formation refers to the legal process of bringing a company into existence.

Stages of Formation

Promotion
↓
Incorporation
↓
Capital Subscription
↓
Commencement of Business

Stage 1: Promotion

Business idea is developed and preliminary arrangements are made.

Stage 2: Incorporation

Company is registered with the Registrar of Companies (ROC).

Stage 3: Capital Subscription

Funds are raised from investors.

Stage 4: Commencement

Business operations begin.

Example 1 (Business)

Entrepreneurs register a startup and complete legal formalities before operations begin.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A group of professionals incorporates a consulting company.

Topic 4: Memorandum of Association (MOA)

Explanation

The Memorandum of Association is the fundamental document of a company.

It defines the company's relationship with outsiders and specifies the scope of its activities.

Contents of MOA

Clause Description
Name Clause Company name
Registered Office Clause Registered office location
Objects Clause Business objectives
Liability Clause Liability of members
Capital Clause Authorized capital
Subscription Clause Subscribers' details

Importance

  • Defines company powers.
  • Protects shareholders and creditors.
  • Prevents unauthorized activities.

Example 1 (Business)

A manufacturing company's MOA specifies manufacturing as its main objective.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A charitable organization's objectives are clearly stated in its MOA.

Topic 5: Articles of Association (AOA)

Explanation

The Articles of Association contain internal rules and regulations governing company management.

Contents

  • Share issue procedures
  • Voting rights
  • Director appointments
  • Conduct of meetings
  • Dividend policies

Difference Between MOA and AOA

Basis MOA AOA
Nature External document Internal document
Purpose Defines powers Defines rules
Scope Broad Detailed

Example 1 (Business)

AOA specifies procedures for appointing directors.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Housing societies often have rules similar to AOA.

Topic 6: Prospectus

Explanation

A Prospectus is a document issued by a public company inviting the public to subscribe to its shares or debentures.

Contents of Prospectus

  • Company information
  • Financial details
  • Business objectives
  • Risk factors
  • Capital structure

Importance

  • Provides information to investors.
  • Ensures transparency.
  • Protects public interest.

Example 1 (Business)

A company issuing shares through an IPO publishes a prospectus.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Investors read prospectuses before investing in companies.

Topic 7: Share Allotment

Explanation

Share allotment refers to the allocation of shares to applicants after receiving applications.

Conditions for Valid Allotment

  • Proper authority
  • Within reasonable time
  • Communication to applicant
  • Compliance with legal requirements

Example 1 (Business)

Investors receive allotted shares after an IPO.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A person receives confirmation of shares allotted after applying.

Topic 8: Shares and Share Capital

Explanation

Shares represent ownership units in a company.

Types of Shares

Equity Shares

  • Voting rights
  • Variable dividends

Preference Shares

  • Fixed dividends
  • Priority in repayment

Share Capital Classification

Type Meaning
Authorized Capital Maximum capital company can issue
Issued Capital Capital offered to public
Subscribed Capital Capital accepted by investors
Paid-up Capital Amount actually paid

Example 1 (Business)

Investors buying shares become part owners of the company.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Individuals invest in stock markets by purchasing company shares.

Topic 9: Promoters

Explanation

Promoters are persons who conceive the idea of a company and take necessary steps for its formation.

Functions

  • Identify business opportunity
  • Arrange capital
  • Prepare legal documents
  • Register company

Duties

  • Act honestly
  • Disclose material facts
  • Avoid secret profits

Example 1 (Business)

Startup founders act as promoters during company formation.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A group initiating a cooperative business acts as promoters.

Explanation

A member is a person whose name appears in the register of members.

Ways to Become a Member

  • Share allotment
  • Share transfer

Rights of Members

  • Voting rights
  • Dividend rights
  • Access to company information

Example 1 (Business)

Shareholders become members after purchasing company shares.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

An investor holding shares enjoys membership rights.

Topic 15: Prevention of Oppression and Mismanagement

Explanation

Company law protects minority shareholders from unfair treatment.

Oppression

Unfair conduct harming minority shareholders.

Mismanagement

Improper management affecting company interests.

Remedies

  • Tribunal intervention
  • Regulation of affairs
  • Removal of directors

Example 1 (Business)

Minority shareholders seek protection against unfair decisions.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Members challenge unfair management practices in organizations.

Topic 16: Winding Up of a Company

Explanation

Winding up is the legal process of closing a company and distributing its assets.

Modes of Winding Up

1. Compulsory Winding Up

By court order.

2. Voluntary Winding Up

By members or creditors.

Winding-Up Process

Decision to Wind Up
↓
Appointment of Liquidator
↓
Collection of Assets
↓
Payment of Liabilities
↓
Distribution of Remaining Assets
↓
Dissolution

Example 1 (Business)

A financially distressed company is wound up by court order.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A business closes permanently after settling debts.

Quick Revision Summary

  • A company is a separate legal entity.
  • Main features include limited liability and perpetual succession.
  • Companies may be private, public, government, or unlimited.
  • Formation involves promotion, incorporation, capital subscription, and commencement.
  • MOA defines company powers; AOA governs internal management.
  • Prospectus invites public investment.
  • Shares represent ownership in a company.
  • Promoters establish companies.
  • Directors manage company affairs.
  • Company secretaries ensure compliance.
  • Law protects against oppression and mismanagement.
  • Winding up legally terminates a company.
