Unit 2: Law of Sale of Goods Act, 1930 and Indian Partnership Act, 1932

Unit Overview

The Sale of Goods Act, 1930 governs the sale and purchase of movable goods in India, defining the rights and obligations of buyers and sellers. The Indian Partnership Act, 1932 regulates partnerships, their formation, rights, liabilities, and dissolution. These laws are essential for understanding commercial transactions and business organizations.

Part A: Sale of Goods Act, 1930

1. Sale of Goods

Definition

According to Section 4 of the Sale of Goods Act, 1930:

A contract of sale is a contract whereby the seller transfers or agrees to transfer the ownership of goods to the buyer for a price.

Essentials

  • Two parties (Buyer and Seller).
  • Goods must exist.
  • Transfer of ownership.
  • Price must be paid.
  • Lawful object and consideration.

Example

A sells a laptop to B for ₹50,000.

3. Types of Goods

Existing Goods

Goods already owned by the seller.

Future Goods

Goods to be manufactured or acquired later.

Contingent Goods

Goods whose acquisition depends on uncertain events.

Example

A farmer agreeing to sell next season's crop is selling future goods.

4. Conditions and Warranties

Condition

Definition

A condition is an essential term of a contract whose breach allows contract termination.

Example

A customer buys a car expecting it to be new, but it is used.

Warranty

Definition

A warranty is a secondary term whose breach allows compensation but not contract cancellation.

Example

Minor defect in car accessories.

Difference Between Condition and Warranty

Basis Condition Warranty
Importance Essential Secondary
Breach Effect Contract may be terminated Compensation only
Remedy Rejection of goods Damages

5. Doctrine of Caveat Emptor

Meaning

"Caveat Emptor" means "Let the Buyer Beware."

Principle

The buyer must satisfy himself regarding the quality and suitability of goods before purchase.

Exceptions

  • Misrepresentation.
  • Fraud.
  • Sale by description.
  • Sale by sample.
  • Merchantable quality.

Example

A buyer cannot complain later if he fails to inspect goods properly.

Definition

Transfer of ownership means passing legal ownership from seller to buyer.

Duties of Buyer

  • Accept delivery.
  • Pay price.

8. Remedies Under Sale of Goods Act

Remedies Available to Seller

  • Suit for price.
  • Damages for non-acceptance.
  • Right of lien.
  • Stoppage in transit.
  • Resale of goods.

Remedies Available to Buyer

  • Damages for non-delivery.
  • Specific performance.
  • Recovery of price.
  • Damages for breach of warranty.

Part B: Indian Partnership Act, 1932

9. Partnership

Definition

According to Section 4:

Partnership is the relation between persons who agree to share profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all.

Essentials

  • Two or more persons.
  • Agreement.
  • Business activity.
  • Profit-sharing.
  • Mutual agency.

Example

A, B, and C start a trading business and share profits equally.

10. Formation of Partnership

Partnership Deed

A written document containing terms and conditions of partnership.

Contents of Partnership Deed

  • Firm name.
  • Nature of business.
  • Capital contribution.
  • Profit-sharing ratio.
  • Rights and duties.
  • Admission and retirement rules.

11. Rights of Partners

Major Rights

  • Participate in management.
  • Share profits.
  • Access books of accounts.
  • Express opinions.
  • Receive interest where applicable.

Example

Every partner can inspect partnership accounts.

12. Duties and Liabilities of Partners

Duties

  • Act honestly.
  • Work for firm's benefit.
  • Maintain true accounts.
  • Share losses.

Liabilities

Unlimited Liability

Partners are personally liable for firm debts.

Joint Liability

All partners are jointly responsible.

Example

If firm assets are insufficient, personal assets may be used to settle debts.

Rights vs Duties of Partners

13. Dissolution of Partnership Firm

Definition

Dissolution means termination of partnership relations among partners.

Modes of Dissolution

1. By Agreement

Partners mutually agree to dissolve the firm.

2. Compulsory Dissolution

Due to illegality or insolvency.

3. Dissolution on Contingencies

  • Expiry of term.
  • Completion of venture.
  • Death of partner.

4. By Notice

Applicable in partnership at will.

5. By Court Order

Court may dissolve under special circumstances.

Important Definitions

  • Sale – Transfer of ownership for a price.
  • Goods – Movable property.
  • Condition – Essential term of a contract.
  • Warranty – Secondary term of a contract.
  • Caveat Emptor – Let the buyer beware.
  • Partnership – Association of persons sharing profits.
  • Partner – Member of a partnership firm.
  • Partnership Deed – Written agreement among partners.
  • Dissolution – Termination of partnership relationship.
  • Mutual Agency – Each partner acts on behalf of others.
