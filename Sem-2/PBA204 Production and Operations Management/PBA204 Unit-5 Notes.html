Unit 5: Inspection, Statistical Quality Control, Six Sigma and Lean Production Systems

Introduction

Quality is one of the most important factors determining the success of an organization. Customers expect products and services to meet their requirements consistently. To ensure this, organizations use various quality control techniques such as inspection, statistical quality control (SQC), control charts, Six Sigma, and Lean Production Systems.

Modern organizations focus not only on detecting defects but also on preventing them. Techniques such as Toyota Production System (TPS), Just-In-Time (JIT), Kanban, and Theory of Constraints (TOC) help organizations improve quality, reduce waste, and increase efficiency.

Topic 1: Inspection

Explanation

Inspection is the process of checking products, materials, or services to ensure they conform to specified standards and requirements.

The primary purpose of inspection is to identify defective items and ensure only quality products reach customers.

Objectives of Inspection

  • Maintain product quality
  • Detect defects
  • Reduce customer complaints
  • Ensure compliance with standards
  • Improve customer satisfaction

Types of Inspection

  1. 100% Inspection
  2. Sample Inspection

Topic 2: 100% Inspection

Explanation

In 100% Inspection, every item produced is inspected individually.

Characteristics

  • Every unit is checked
  • Suitable for critical products
  • High inspection cost
  • Time-consuming

Advantages

  • High accuracy
  • Defective items are easily detected
  • Suitable for high-risk products

Limitations

  • Expensive
  • Time consuming
  • Human errors may occur

Example 1 (Business)

Aircraft components are often subjected to 100% inspection due to safety requirements.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A jeweler carefully checks every diamond before selling it.

Topic 3: Sample Inspection

Explanation

In Sample Inspection, only a selected sample from a batch is inspected.

The quality of the sample is used to estimate the quality of the entire lot.

Characteristics

  • Lower inspection cost
  • Faster inspection
  • Statistical approach

Advantages

  • Saves time
  • Lower cost
  • Suitable for large batches

Limitations

  • Possibility of accepting defective lots
  • Less accurate than 100% inspection

Example 1 (Business)

A pharmaceutical company inspects a sample of tablets from a production batch.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A teacher checks a few answer sheets to evaluate the overall performance of a class.

Topic 4: Operating Characteristic (OC) Curve

Explanation

An Operating Characteristic (OC) Curve is a graph that shows the probability of accepting a lot at different quality levels.

It helps evaluate the effectiveness of a sampling plan.

OC Curve Relationship

Lot Quality
↓
Sampling Inspection
↓
Probability of Acceptance

Importance

  • Helps design sampling plans
  • Measures inspection effectiveness
  • Assists quality decision-making

Example 1 (Business)

Manufacturers use OC curves to decide appropriate sample sizes.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Survey organizations use sampling acceptance principles similar to OC curves.

Topic 5: Statistical Quality Control (SQC)

Explanation

Statistical Quality Control is the application of statistical techniques to monitor and control quality.

The objective is to identify variations and maintain consistent product quality.

Objectives of SQC

  • Detect quality problems
  • Reduce variability
  • Improve process performance
  • Prevent defects

Components of SQC

Statistical Quality Control
↓
Process Control
↓
Acceptance Sampling

Benefits

  • Reduced defects
  • Improved quality
  • Lower costs
  • Better productivity

Example 1 (Business)

Automobile companies use SQC to monitor manufacturing processes.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A bakery regularly checks the weight of bread loaves to ensure consistency.

Topic 7: X̄ (X-Bar) Chart

Explanation

The X̄ Chart monitors variations in process averages.

It is used for variables such as weight, length, and temperature.

Control Limit Formula

UCL = X̄ + A₂R̄

LCL = X̄ - A₂R̄

Example 1 (Business)

Monitoring the average diameter of machine parts.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Checking average marks of students in different tests.

Topic 8: R Chart

Explanation

The R Chart monitors process variability or dispersion.

It is usually used together with the X̄ Chart.

Control Limit Formula

UCL = D₄R̄

LCL = D₃R̄

Example 1 (Business)

Monitoring variation in thickness of steel sheets.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Tracking variation in daily study hours.

Topic 9: p Chart

Explanation

A p Chart measures the proportion of defective items in a sample.

Formula

p = Number of Defectives / Sample Size

Applications

  • Defective products
  • Service errors
  • Customer complaints

Example 1 (Business)

Monitoring defective mobile phones in production.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Measuring incorrect answers in a test.

Topic 10: np Chart

Explanation

An np Chart measures the actual number of defective units in a sample.

Characteristics

  • Sample size remains constant
  • Counts defective items

Example 1 (Business)

Counting defective bulbs in each production lot.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Counting incorrect forms in a batch of applications.

Topic 11: c Chart

Explanation

A c Chart monitors the number of defects per unit.

Characteristics

  • Used when sample size remains constant
  • Counts defects rather than defective units

Example

A product may have multiple defects but still be counted as one unit.

Example 1 (Business)

Counting scratches on automobile body panels.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Counting spelling mistakes in an essay.

Topic 12: Numerical Example on Control Charts

Example (p Chart)

Sample Size = 100

Defective Items = 8

Then,

p = 8 / 100 = 0.08

Thus, the proportion defective is 0.08 or 8%.

Topic 13: Six Sigma

Explanation

Six Sigma is a quality improvement methodology focused on reducing defects and process variation.

The goal is to achieve near-perfect quality.

Objective

Only 3.4 defects per million opportunities (DPMO).

Six Sigma Methodology (DMAIC)

Define
↓
Measure
↓
Analyze
↓
Improve
↓
Control

Benefits

  • Reduced defects
  • Improved customer satisfaction
  • Lower costs
  • Increased profitability

Example 1 (Business)

Motorola pioneered Six Sigma to improve manufacturing quality.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A delivery service reducing late deliveries through process improvement.

Topic 14: Lean Production Systems

Explanation

Lean Production is a management philosophy that focuses on eliminating waste while maximizing customer value.

It originated in Japan and became famous through the Toyota Production System.

Objectives

  • Reduce waste
  • Improve efficiency
  • Increase customer value
  • Reduce inventory

Seven Major Wastes

WasteMeaning
OverproductionProducing more than needed
WaitingIdle time
TransportationUnnecessary movement
OverprocessingExtra processing
InventoryExcess stock
MotionUnnecessary movement
DefectsRework and corrections

Example 1 (Business)

Toyota minimizes inventory and eliminates unnecessary activities.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Organizing a study desk to reduce time spent searching for materials.

Topic 16: Just-In-Time (JIT)

Explanation

JIT is a production system where materials arrive exactly when needed.

Objectives

  • Reduce inventory
  • Minimize waste
  • Improve efficiency

JIT Flow

Customer Demand
↓
Production Trigger
↓
Material Supply
↓
Manufacturing
↓
Delivery

Advantages

  • Lower inventory costs
  • Better quality
  • Faster response

Limitations

  • Supplier dependency
  • Risk of stock shortages

Example 1 (Business)

Toyota receives parts only when production requires them.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Buying groceries only when needed instead of storing excessive stock.

Topic 17: Kanban

Explanation

Kanban is a visual signaling system used to control production and material movement.

The word "Kanban" means "signal card."

Kanban Process

Demand Signal
↓
Kanban Card
↓
Production Authorization
↓
Material Movement

Benefits

  • Reduced inventory
  • Better workflow visibility
  • Improved coordination

Example 1 (Business)

Toyota uses Kanban cards to signal replenishment.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A shopping list signaling items that need replenishment.

Topic 18: Theory of Constraints (TOC)

Explanation

Theory of Constraints, developed by Eliyahu Goldratt, focuses on identifying and managing bottlenecks that limit system performance.

TOC Steps

Identify Constraint
↓
Exploit Constraint
↓
Subordinate Other Activities
↓
Elevate Constraint
↓
Repeat Process

Benefits

  • Improved throughput
  • Better resource utilization
  • Faster production flow

Example 1 (Business)

A factory increases output by improving its slowest machine.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A student improves exam preparation by focusing on the weakest subject first.

  • TPS, JIT, Kanban, and TOC improve efficiency and quality.
