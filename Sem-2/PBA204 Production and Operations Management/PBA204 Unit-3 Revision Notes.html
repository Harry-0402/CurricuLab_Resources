Unit 3: Facilities Location & Layout

Unit Overview

Facilities Location and Layout are critical decisions in Operations Management because they directly affect production costs, efficiency, customer service, and profitability. A good location ensures easy access to resources and markets, while an effective layout ensures smooth workflow, reduced material handling, and better utilization of resources.

Key Concepts

1. Facilities Location

Definition

Facilities Location refers to selecting the most suitable geographical place for setting up a plant, office, warehouse, or service facility.

Strategic Importance of Location

  • Influences operating costs.
  • Affects transportation expenses.
  • Impacts customer service levels.
  • Determines availability of labor and raw materials.
  • Influences future business growth.
  • Provides competitive advantage.

Example

An automobile manufacturer may choose a location near suppliers to reduce transportation costs.

2. Objectives of Facility Location

Key Objectives

  • Minimize total operating cost.
  • Ensure smooth supply of raw materials.
  • Provide easy market access.
  • Obtain skilled workforce.
  • Facilitate future expansion.
  • Improve customer service.

Example

A warehouse located near major highways ensures faster product delivery.

3. Factors Affecting Facility Location

A. Primary Factors

1. Availability of Raw Materials

  • Important for manufacturing industries.
  • Reduces transportation cost.
  • Ensures continuous production.

Example

Sugar factories are usually located near sugarcane-producing regions.

2. Market Proximity

  • Reduces delivery time.
  • Improves customer service.
  • Lowers distribution costs.

Example

Fast-food outlets are located near densely populated areas.

3. Availability of Labor

  • Skilled and unskilled workforce.
  • Labor cost considerations.
  • Industrial relations environment.

Example

IT companies establish offices where skilled software professionals are available.

4. Transportation Facilities

  • Road, rail, air, and sea connectivity.
  • Reduces logistics cost.
  • Ensures timely supply.

Example

Ports attract export-oriented industries.

5. Utilities and Infrastructure

  • Electricity.
  • Water supply.
  • Internet connectivity.
  • Communication facilities.

Example

Manufacturing plants require uninterrupted power supply.

6. Government Policies

  • Tax benefits.
  • Subsidies.
  • Environmental regulations.
  • Industrial development policies.

Example

Special Economic Zones (SEZs) offer incentives to industries.

7. Community and Environmental Factors

  • Local community support.
  • Environmental regulations.
  • Quality of life.

Example

Industries must comply with pollution control norms.

5. Single Location Decision

Definition

A decision involving the selection of one facility location for business operations.

Characteristics

  • Simpler management.
  • Lower coordination requirements.
  • Easier supervision.
  • Limited market reach.

Advantages

  • Better control.
  • Lower administrative cost.
  • Simplified communication.

Example

A local bakery operating from a single manufacturing facility.

6. Multi-Location Decision

Definition

A decision involving multiple facilities across different locations.

Characteristics

  • Wider market coverage.
  • Better customer service.
  • Increased flexibility.
  • Complex management.

Advantages

  • Reduced delivery time.
  • Increased market penetration.
  • Risk diversification.

Disadvantages

  • Higher investment.
  • Coordination challenges.
  • Increased operating costs.

Example

A multinational company operating factories in several countries.

7. Facilities Layout

Definition

Facilities Layout is the physical arrangement of machines, departments, workstations, storage areas, and equipment within a facility.

Objectives

  • Smooth workflow.
  • Reduce material handling.
  • Utilize space effectively.
  • Increase productivity.
  • Improve safety.
  • Minimize delays.

Importance

  • Lowers production costs.
  • Improves quality.
  • Reduces congestion.
  • Enhances employee morale.
  • Supports future expansion.

Principles of Good Layout

1. Integration Principle

All resources should work together efficiently.

2. Minimum Movement Principle

Reduce unnecessary movement of materials and workers.

3. Smooth Flow Principle

Ensure continuous workflow.

4. Space Utilization Principle

Use available space effectively.

5. Safety Principle

Provide a safe working environment.

6. Flexibility Principle

Allow future modifications and expansion.

Types of Facilities Layout

1. Product Layout (Line Layout)

Definition

Machines and equipment are arranged according to the sequence of operations.

Flow

Raw Material
↓
Operation 1
↓
Operation 2
↓
Operation 3
↓
Finished Product

Characteristics

  • Continuous workflow.
  • High production volume.
  • Standardized products.

Advantages

  • High productivity.
  • Low material handling cost.
  • Simple supervision.

Disadvantages

  • Less flexibility.
  • Breakdown affects entire line.
  • High initial investment.

Example

Automobile assembly lines.

2. Process Layout (Functional Layout)

Definition

Similar machines or functions are grouped together.

Characteristics

  • Suitable for varied products.
  • High flexibility.
  • Specialized departments.

Advantages

  • Flexible operations.
  • Better machine utilization.
  • Suitable for customized products.

Disadvantages

  • Higher material handling cost.
  • Complex scheduling.
  • Longer production time.

Example

Hospitals where departments are grouped by function.

3. Fixed Position Layout

Definition

The product remains at one location while resources move to it.

Characteristics

  • Used for large products.
  • Product does not move.
  • Equipment and workers move.

Advantages

  • Minimal product movement.
  • Suitable for large projects.

Disadvantages

  • Difficult supervision.
  • High coordination requirements.

Example

Shipbuilding and aircraft manufacturing.

4. Cellular Layout

Definition

Machines are grouped into cells to process similar products.

Characteristics

  • Combines product and process layouts.
  • Reduces movement.
  • Improves flexibility.

Advantages

  • Faster production.
  • Reduced inventory.
  • Better quality control.

Disadvantages

  • Requires careful planning.
  • Equipment duplication may occur.

Example

Electronics manufacturing units.

Important Definitions

  • Facility Location – Selection of the best geographical site for operations.
  • Facility Layout – Physical arrangement of resources within a facility.
  • Single Location – Business operates from one facility.
  • Multi-Location – Business operates from multiple facilities.
  • Product Layout – Arrangement according to operation sequence.
  • Process Layout – Arrangement by function.
  • Fixed Position Layout – Product remains stationary.
  • Cellular Layout – Machines grouped into manufacturing cells.
