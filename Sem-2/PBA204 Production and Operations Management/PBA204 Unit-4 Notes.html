Unit 4: Types of Industries and Manufacturing Systems

Introduction

Industries are the backbone of economic development. They convert raw materials into finished products and provide services that satisfy customer needs. Different industries operate in different ways depending on the nature of products, customer requirements, production volume, and technology used.

Understanding the various types of industries, business operations, manufacturing methods, and production systems helps managers select the most suitable production process for achieving efficiency, quality, and profitability.

Topic 1: Types of Industries

Explanation

Industries can be classified based on the nature of activities they perform.

Major Types of Industries

1. Primary Industries

These industries extract natural resources directly from nature.

Examples

  • Agriculture
  • Fishing
  • Mining
  • Forestry

Example 1 (Business)

Coal mining companies extract coal from mines for industrial use.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Farmers growing crops for food production belong to primary industries.

2. Secondary Industries

These industries convert raw materials into finished products.

Examples

  • Automobile manufacturing
  • Textile production
  • Steel manufacturing
  • Electronics manufacturing

Example 1 (Business)

A steel plant converts iron ore into steel products.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A bakery converts flour into bread and cakes.

3. Tertiary Industries (Service Industries)

These industries provide services rather than physical products.

Examples

  • Banking
  • Insurance
  • Healthcare
  • Education
  • Transportation

Example 1 (Business)

Banks provide financial services to customers.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A hospital provides healthcare services.

Topic 4: Scale of Operations

Explanation

Scale of operations refers to the size and capacity of business activities.

Types of Scale

1. Small Scale Operations

Characteristics:

  • Limited capital
  • Small workforce
  • Local market focus

Advantages

  • Flexibility
  • Personalized service

Limitations

  • Limited resources
  • Lower production volume

Example 1

Local furniture workshops.

Example 2

Home-based food businesses.

2. Medium Scale Operations

Characteristics:

  • Moderate investment
  • Regional market coverage
  • Balanced flexibility and efficiency

Example 1

Regional manufacturing companies.

Example 2

Medium-sized garment factories.

3. Large Scale Operations

Characteristics:

  • High investment
  • Advanced technology
  • National or global market reach

Advantages

  • Economies of scale
  • High productivity

Limitations

  • Complex management
  • Large capital requirements

Example 1

Automobile manufacturing plants.

Example 2

Large e-commerce distribution centers.

Topic 5: Methods of Manufacturing

Explanation

Manufacturing methods determine how products are produced and delivered to customers.

The selection of a manufacturing method depends on product variety, production volume, customer requirements, and process complexity.

A. Project Production

Explanation

Project Production is used when a unique product is produced for a specific customer or purpose.

The product remains fixed while resources move around it.

Characteristics

  • One-time production
  • Large-scale projects
  • High customization
  • Long completion time

Advantages

  • High flexibility
  • Customized output

Limitations

  • High cost
  • Complex coordination

Example 1 (Business)

Construction of a bridge.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Building a residential house.

B. Jobbing Production

Explanation

Jobbing Production involves producing customized products in small quantities according to customer specifications.

Characteristics

  • High variety
  • Low volume
  • Skilled workforce required
  • Flexible equipment

Advantages

  • Customized products
  • High customer satisfaction

Limitations

  • High production cost
  • Complex scheduling

Example 1 (Business)

Custom furniture manufacturing.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A tailor stitching a custom-designed suit.

C. Batch Production

Explanation

Batch Production involves manufacturing products in groups or batches.

After one batch is completed, the next batch is produced.

Characteristics

  • Moderate volume
  • Moderate variety
  • Repeated production

Advantages

  • Better machine utilization
  • Flexibility

Limitations

  • Setup time between batches
  • Inventory accumulation

Example 1 (Business)

Pharmaceutical companies producing medicines in batches.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A bakery producing 500 cookies at a time.

D. Flow Production (Continuous Production)

Explanation

Flow Production involves continuous movement of products through sequential operations.

Characteristics

  • High production volume
  • Standardized products
  • Continuous workflow

Advantages

  • Low unit cost
  • High efficiency
  • Consistent quality

Limitations

  • Low flexibility
  • High initial investment

Example 1 (Business)

Automobile assembly lines.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Mineral water bottling plants.

E. Process Production

Explanation

Process Production involves continuous processing of materials where output is difficult to separate into individual units.

Characteristics

  • Continuous operations
  • Chemical or physical transformations
  • Large-scale production

Advantages

  • High productivity
  • Efficient resource utilization

Limitations

  • Expensive equipment
  • Difficult process changes

Example 1 (Business)

Petroleum refining.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Milk processing plants.

Quick Revision Summary

  • Industries are classified into primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors.
  • Businesses may be manufacturing, service, trading, retail, or wholesale.
  • Modern organizations integrate manufacturing and services.
  • Scale of operations can be small, medium, or large.
  • Project production is suitable for unique projects.
  • Jobbing production is used for customized products.
  • Batch production manufactures products in groups.
  • Flow production supports mass production.
  • Process production is common in chemical industries.
  • Manufacturing methods differ in volume, variety, and flexibility.
