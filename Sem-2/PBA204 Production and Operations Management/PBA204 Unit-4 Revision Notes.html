Unit 4: Types of Industries and Manufacturing Methods

Unit Overview

Industries produce goods and services that satisfy human needs and contribute to economic development. Depending on the nature of products, customer requirements, and production volume, industries adopt different manufacturing methods. Understanding various industries and production systems helps managers select the most suitable process for efficient operations.

Key Concepts

1. Types of Industries

Definition

An industry is a group of businesses engaged in similar economic activities related to the production of goods or services.

Major Types of Industries

A. Primary Industries

These industries extract natural resources directly from nature.

Examples

  • Agriculture
  • Mining
  • Fishing
  • Forestry

Characteristics

  • Resource-based.
  • Depend on natural conditions.
  • Supply raw materials to other industries.

B. Secondary Industries

These industries convert raw materials into finished products.

Examples

  • Automobile manufacturing
  • Textile manufacturing
  • Steel industry
  • Food processing

Characteristics

  • Value addition to raw materials.
  • Use machinery and labor.
  • Produce consumer and industrial goods.

C. Tertiary Industries (Service Industries)

These industries provide services rather than physical products.

Examples

  • Banking
  • Education
  • Healthcare
  • Transportation

Characteristics

  • Intangible output.
  • Customer interaction is high.
  • Focus on service quality.

D. Quaternary Industries

Knowledge-based industries involving information and technology.

Examples

  • IT Services
  • Research & Development
  • Consulting
  • Data Analytics

Characteristics

  • Knowledge-intensive.
  • Highly skilled workforce.
  • Innovation-driven.

2. Variety of Businesses

Businesses differ based on their products, services, customers, and operational scale.

Types of Businesses

Manufacturing Businesses

  • Produce tangible goods.
  • Require production facilities.
  • Focus on production efficiency.

Example: Car manufacturers.

Service Businesses

  • Provide intangible services.
  • Customer interaction is important.
  • Quality depends on service delivery.

Example: Hospitals and banks.

Trading Businesses

  • Buy and sell goods.
  • No manufacturing involved.
  • Focus on distribution.

Example: Retail stores.

Hybrid Businesses

Combine products and services.

Example: Automobile companies provide cars along with maintenance services.

3. Integration of Manufacturing and Services

Definition

Modern businesses increasingly integrate manufacturing and services to create greater customer value.

Reasons for Integration

  • Increased customer expectations.
  • Better customer support.
  • Competitive advantage.
  • Improved customer satisfaction.

Examples

Product Associated Service
Car Warranty & Maintenance
Mobile Phone Technical Support
Computer Installation & Training
Machinery After-Sales Service

Benefits

  • Customer loyalty.
  • Additional revenue.
  • Improved brand image.
  • Long-term customer relationships.

4. Scale of Operations

Definition

Scale of Operations refers to the size and volume of business activities.

Types of Scale

Small Scale Operations

Characteristics

  • Limited production.
  • Lower investment.
  • Local market focus.

Example

Local bakery.

Medium Scale Operations

Characteristics

  • Moderate production capacity.
  • Regional market coverage.
  • Balanced investment.

Example

Regional food processing company.

Large Scale Operations

Characteristics

  • Mass production.
  • High investment.
  • National or international markets.

Example

Automobile manufacturing companies.

Manufacturing Methods

5. Project Production

Definition

Project Production involves producing a unique product or undertaking a one-time activity.

Characteristics

  • Large-scale projects.
  • Unique output.
  • Long completion time.
  • High customization.

Advantages

  • Highly customized.
  • Suitable for complex jobs.

Disadvantages

  • High cost.
  • Difficult scheduling.

Examples

  • Construction of dams
  • Bridge construction
  • Building airports

6. Jobbing Production (Job Production)

Definition

Production of individual products according to customer specifications.

Characteristics

  • Customized products.
  • Skilled workforce required.
  • Low production volume.
  • High flexibility.

Advantages

  • Meets customer needs.
  • High product variety.

Disadvantages

  • High production cost.
  • Longer completion time.

Examples

  • Tailoring shops
  • Furniture making
  • Specialized machinery

7. Batch Production

Definition

Products are manufactured in groups or batches.

Characteristics

  • Moderate volume.
  • Similar products produced together.
  • Flexibility in production.

Advantages

  • Better machine utilization.
  • Reduced setup costs.
  • Suitable for varying demand.

Disadvantages

  • Inventory accumulation.
  • Production scheduling complexity.

Examples

  • Bakery products
  • Pharmaceutical manufacturing
  • Garment production

8. Flow Production (Continuous Production)

Definition

Production occurs continuously through a sequence of operations.

Characteristics

  • High production volume.
  • Standardized products.
  • Continuous workflow.

Advantages

  • Low production cost per unit.
  • High productivity.
  • Efficient material flow.

Disadvantages

  • High initial investment.
  • Low flexibility.

Examples

  • Automobile assembly lines
  • Bottling plants
  • Electronics manufacturing

9. Process Production

Definition

Production based on chemical or physical transformation processes that operate continuously.

Characteristics

  • Continuous operation.
  • Highly automated.
  • Large-scale production.

Advantages

  • Very high output.
  • Consistent quality.
  • Low unit cost.

Disadvantages

  • Expensive setup.
  • Difficult modifications.

Examples

  • Oil refineries
  • Cement plants
  • Chemical industries

Important Definitions

  • Industry – Group of businesses engaged in similar activities.
  • Manufacturing Industry – Converts raw materials into finished products.
  • Service Industry – Provides intangible services.
  • Scale of Operations – Size and volume of business activities.
  • Project Production – One-time production of a unique product.
  • Jobbing Production – Customized production for specific orders.
  • Batch Production – Production in groups or batches.
  • Flow Production – Continuous movement through production stages.
  • Process Production – Continuous processing of materials.
