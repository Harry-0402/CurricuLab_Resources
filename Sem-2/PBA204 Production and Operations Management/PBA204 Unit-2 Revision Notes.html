Unit 2: Productivity and Work Study

Unit Overview

Productivity is a key measure of efficiency in operations management. Organizations improve productivity by optimizing resources, reducing waste, and improving work methods. Work Study combines Method Study and Work Measurement to achieve higher efficiency and establish standard times for tasks. This unit covers productivity concepts, process analysis tools, work measurement techniques, and numerical concepts related to standard time.

Key Concepts

1. Productivity

Definition

Productivity is the ratio of output produced to the input used.

Formula

[ \text{Productivity} = \frac{\text{Output}}{\text{Input}} ]

Key Points

  • Measures efficiency of resource utilization.
  • Higher productivity leads to lower costs.
  • Improves competitiveness.
  • Enhances profitability.
  • Important for economic growth.

Types of Productivity

Type Formula
Labour Productivity Output / Labour Input
Machine Productivity Output / Machine Hours
Material Productivity Output / Material Used
Total Productivity Total Output / Total Input

Example

If a worker produces 100 units in 10 hours:

Labour Productivity = 100 ÷ 10 = 10 units/hour

2. Work Study

Definition

Work Study is the systematic examination of work methods to improve efficiency and establish performance standards.

Components of Work Study

Work Study
↓
Method Study
+
Work Measurement

Objectives

  • Increase productivity.
  • Reduce unnecessary work.
  • Improve resource utilization.
  • Establish standard times.
  • Reduce production costs.

Scope

  • Manufacturing industries.
  • Service organizations.
  • Government organizations.
  • Healthcare institutions.
  • Logistics operations.

Uses

  • Cost reduction.
  • Capacity planning.
  • Production scheduling.
  • Workforce planning.
  • Performance evaluation.

Example

A factory studies worker movements to reduce unnecessary walking and improve output.

3. Method Study

Definition

Method Study is the systematic recording and examination of existing and proposed methods of performing work.

Objectives

  • Eliminate unnecessary activities.
  • Simplify operations.
  • Reduce fatigue.
  • Improve workflow.
  • Save time and cost.

Procedure of Method Study

Select Work
↓
Record Facts
↓
Examine Critically
↓
Develop New Method
↓
Install Method
↓
Maintain Method

Example

Rearranging machine positions to reduce material handling time.

5. Flow Diagram

Definition

A Flow Diagram is a graphical representation showing the movement of materials, workers, or equipment.

Key Points

  • Shows physical movement.
  • Identifies unnecessary travel.
  • Helps improve layout.
  • Reduces transportation costs.

Example

A factory floor layout showing movement of raw materials from warehouse to production.

Process Mapping visually represents the sequence of activities in a process.

Benefits

  • Improves understanding.
  • Standardizes processes.
  • Supports continuous improvement.

Customer Order Process:

↓

7. Work Measurement

Definition

Work Measurement determines the time required by a qualified worker to perform a task at a defined level of performance.

Objectives

  • Establish standard times.
  • Improve planning.
  • Determine labor requirements.
  • Set performance standards.
  • Assist wage administration.

Techniques of Work Measurement

Technique Purpose
Time Study Measures task duration
Work Sampling Estimates activity distribution
Synthetic Time Standards Predetermined task times
Standard Data Uses previously established times

8. Elements of Work

Definition

An element is a distinct part of an operation selected for observation and measurement.

Characteristics

  • Clearly identifiable.
  • Short duration.
  • Repetitive in nature.
  • Easy to observe.

Example

In assembling a pen:

  • Pick body
  • Insert refill
  • Attach cap
  • Inspect product

Each step is an element.

9. Performance Rating

Definition

Performance Rating is the assessment of a worker's speed and effectiveness compared to standard performance.

Formula

[ \text{Normal Time} = \text{Observed Time} \times \frac{\text{Performance Rating}}{100} ]

Example

Observed Time = 5 minutes

Performance Rating = 120%

Normal Time

= 5 × 120/100

= 6 minutes

10. Allowances

Definition

Allowances are additional time added to normal time to account for unavoidable delays and worker needs.

Types of Allowances

Type Purpose
Personal Allowance Personal needs
Fatigue Allowance Physical and mental fatigue
Delay Allowance Unavoidable interruptions

Example

Workers are allowed rest periods during long working hours.

11. Standard Time

Definition

Standard Time is the time allowed for completing a task, including allowances.

Formula

[ \text{Standard Time} = \text{Normal Time} \times (1 + Allowance) ]

Alternative Formula

[ \text{Standard Time} = \frac{\text{Normal Time}}{1 - \text{Allowance}} ]

Numerical Example

Observed Time = 10 minutes

Performance Rating = 110%

Allowance = 10%

Step 1: Calculate Normal Time

[ 10 \times \frac{110}{100} = 11 \text{ minutes} ]

Step 2: Calculate Standard Time

[ 11 \times (1 + 0.10) = 12.1 \text{ minutes} ]

Standard Time = 12.1 minutes

12. Synthetic Time Standards

Definition

Synthetic Time Standards use predetermined times from previously measured work elements.

Advantages

  • Saves study time.
  • Improves consistency.
  • Useful for repetitive jobs.
  • Provides quick estimates.

Example

Using standard data from previous assembly operations to estimate task time.

13. Work Sampling

Definition

Work Sampling is a statistical technique used to estimate the proportion of time spent on various activities.

Procedure

Define Activities
↓
Take Random Observations
↓
Record Activity
↓
Analyze Results
↓
Determine Utilization

Advantages

  • Suitable for long-cycle jobs.
  • Less expensive than time study.
  • Useful for indirect work.
  • Requires fewer observations.

Example

A supervisor observes workers randomly throughout the day to determine productive and idle time percentages.
