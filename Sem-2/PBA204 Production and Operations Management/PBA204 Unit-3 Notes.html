Unit 3: Facilities Location & Layout

Introduction

The success of any manufacturing or service organization depends greatly on where its facilities are located and how those facilities are arranged. A poor location may increase transportation costs, delay deliveries, and reduce customer satisfaction. Similarly, an inefficient layout can lead to unnecessary movement of materials, increased production time, and higher operating costs.

Facility Location and Layout decisions are strategic decisions because they involve long-term investments and significantly affect organizational efficiency, productivity, and profitability.

Topic 1: Facilities Location

Explanation

Facility Location refers to the selection of a suitable geographical area for establishing a plant, warehouse, office, retail outlet, or service center.

The objective is to choose a location that minimizes costs and maximizes operational efficiency and customer satisfaction.

Importance of Facility Location

  • Reduces transportation costs
  • Improves customer service
  • Ensures availability of resources
  • Enhances profitability
  • Supports future expansion
  • Improves competitive advantage

Example 1 (Business)

A manufacturing company sets up its factory near raw material suppliers to reduce transportation expenses.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A restaurant located near a busy market attracts more customers than one located in a remote area.

Topic 3: Factors Affecting Facility Location

Explanation

Several factors influence the selection of a facility location.

Major Factors

1. Availability of Raw Materials

Industries dependent on bulky raw materials often locate near supply sources.

2. Availability of Labor

Access to skilled and unskilled workers is essential.

3. Transportation Facilities

Good road, rail, air, and port connectivity reduce logistics costs.

4. Market Proximity

Being closer to customers reduces delivery time.

5. Utilities

Availability of electricity, water, and communication facilities.

6. Government Policies

Tax incentives, regulations, and industrial policies influence location decisions.

7. Climate and Environmental Conditions

Suitable environmental conditions support smooth operations.

8. Community Attitude

Local support helps business growth.

Topic 5: Single Location Decision

Explanation

A Single Location Decision involves selecting one location from several alternatives for establishing operations.

Factors Considered

  • Cost
  • Market access
  • Labor availability
  • Infrastructure
  • Government support

Advantages

  • Simpler management
  • Easier coordination
  • Lower administrative costs

Limitations

  • Higher risk concentration
  • Limited geographical coverage

Topic 7: Facility Layout

Explanation

Facility Layout refers to the physical arrangement of machines, equipment, departments, workstations, and service areas within a facility.

The objective is to ensure smooth workflow with minimum movement of materials and people.

Objectives of Layout

  • Minimize material handling
  • Improve productivity
  • Reduce delays
  • Ensure worker safety
  • Utilize space efficiently
  • Facilitate supervision

Topic 8: Factors Affecting Facility Layout

Explanation

Several factors influence layout decisions.

Important Factors

  1. Nature of product
  2. Production volume
  3. Manufacturing process
  4. Material handling requirements
  5. Available space
  6. Safety considerations
  7. Flexibility requirements
  8. Employee convenience

Topic 9: Principles of Facility Layout

Explanation

Facility layout should follow certain principles to maximize efficiency.

Major Principles

1. Principle of Integration

All resources should work together as a unified system.

2. Principle of Minimum Movement

Reduce unnecessary movement of materials and workers.

3. Principle of Flow

Ensure smooth workflow.

4. Principle of Cubic Space Utilization

Utilize both horizontal and vertical space.

5. Principle of Flexibility

Allow future modifications.

6. Principle of Safety

Ensure safe working conditions.

Topic 10: Types of Facility Layout

Explanation

Different layouts are used depending on product type, production volume, and process requirements.

A. Product Layout (Line Layout)

Explanation

Machines and equipment are arranged according to the sequence of operations.

Characteristics

  • Continuous workflow
  • High production volume
  • Standardized products

Advantages

  • High efficiency
  • Low material handling cost
  • Easy supervision

Limitations

  • Low flexibility
  • Breakdown affects entire line

B. Process Layout (Functional Layout)

Explanation

Similar machines are grouped together.

Characteristics

  • Suitable for varied products
  • Flexible operations
  • Specialized departments

Advantages

  • High flexibility
  • Better machine utilization

Limitations

  • Complex scheduling
  • Higher material handling cost

C. Fixed Position Layout

Explanation

The product remains stationary while resources move around it.

Characteristics

  • Used for large products
  • Resources travel to product location

Advantages

  • Reduced product movement
  • Suitable for large projects

Limitations

  • Difficult coordination
  • Space constraints

D. Cellular Layout

Explanation

Machines are grouped into cells to process similar product families.

Characteristics

  • Combination of product and process layouts
  • Faster production
  • Reduced movement

Advantages

  • Improved productivity
  • Reduced inventory
  • Better quality

Limitations

  • Initial setup cost
  • Complex planning

Quick Revision Summary

  • Facility location determines where operations will be established.
  • Location decisions are strategic and long-term.
  • Factors affecting location include labor, raw materials, transportation, and market access.
  • Facility layout concerns the arrangement of resources within a facility.
  • Good layouts reduce movement and improve productivity.
  • Major layout principles include integration, flow, flexibility, and safety.
  • Product layout suits mass production.
  • Process layout suits customized production.
  • Fixed position layout suits large projects.
  • Cellular layout combines flexibility and efficiency.
