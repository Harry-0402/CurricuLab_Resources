Unit 3: Setting of Targets

Introduction

Every organization aims to achieve specific business objectives such as increasing profits, improving customer satisfaction, reducing costs, or expanding market share. To achieve these objectives, firms must identify what drives value, measure performance accurately, compare results with competitors, and maintain high-quality data.

This unit focuses on identifying key value drivers, understanding metrics and KPIs, constructing meaningful performance measures, benchmarking performance, understanding the importance of data, and governing master data effectively.

Topic 1: Identifying a Firm's Key Value Drivers

Explanation

Value Drivers are factors that directly influence the success, profitability, and growth of a business. They help organizations understand which activities create value and contribute to achieving business goals.

Definition

A Value Driver is any activity, process, or factor that significantly impacts organizational performance.

Types of Value Drivers

1. Financial Value Drivers

Factors affecting profitability and financial performance.

Examples:

  • Revenue Growth
  • Cost Reduction
  • Profit Margin
  • Cash Flow

2. Operational Value Drivers

Factors affecting efficiency and productivity.

Examples:

  • Production Efficiency
  • Delivery Time
  • Resource Utilization

3. Customer Value Drivers

Factors affecting customer satisfaction.

Examples:

  • Product Quality
  • Customer Service
  • Brand Reputation

4. Innovation Value Drivers

Factors supporting future growth.

Examples:

  • Research and Development
  • New Product Launches
  • Technology Adoption

Process of Identifying Value Drivers

↓
Analyze Operations
↓
Identify Critical Success Factors
↓
Determine Value Drivers
↓
Measure Performance

Example 1 (Business): Amazon considers fast delivery, customer satisfaction, and operational efficiency as major value drivers.

Example 2 (Practical): A restaurant identifies food quality, service speed, and customer reviews as important value drivers.

Topic 2: Metrics vs KPIs

Explanation

Organizations use metrics and KPIs to measure performance and monitor progress toward goals. Although related, they are not identical.

What are Metrics?

Metrics are measurable values used to track business activities.

Examples:

  • Number of customers
  • Revenue
  • Website visitors
  • Employee attendance

What are KPIs?

KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) are critical metrics directly linked to strategic objectives.

Examples:

  • Customer Retention Rate
  • Net Profit Margin
  • Employee Productivity
  • Market Share Growth

Difference Between Metrics and KPIs

Basis Metrics KPIs
Purpose Measure activities Measure strategic success
Importance General Critical
Scope Broad Specific
Link to Goals Not always Directly linked
Example Number of Website Visits Conversion Rate

Characteristics of Good KPIs

  • Specific
  • Measurable
  • Achievable
  • Relevant
  • Time-bound
  • (SMART Framework)

Example 1 (Business): Netflix tracks subscriber growth as a KPI because it directly reflects business success.

Example 2 (Practical): A student tracks daily study hours as a metric and examination score improvement as a KPI.

Topic 4: Benchmarking

Explanation

Benchmarking is the process of comparing an organization's performance, processes, or products with industry leaders or competitors.

Objectives

  • Identify best practices.
  • Improve performance.
  • Enhance competitiveness.
  • Reduce inefficiencies.

Types of Benchmarking

Internal Benchmarking

Comparison within the organization.

Competitive Benchmarking

Comparison with competitors.

Functional Benchmarking

Comparison with organizations performing similar functions.

Strategic Benchmarking

Comparison of business strategies.

Benchmarking Process

Identify Area for Improvement
↓
Select Benchmark Partner
↓
Collect Data
↓
Compare Performance
↓
Implement Improvements

Benefits

  • Continuous improvement
  • Better performance standards
  • Increased efficiency
  • Competitive advantage

Example 1 (Business): Toyota is often benchmarked by manufacturers for its production efficiency.

Example 2 (Practical): A school compares its examination results with top-performing schools to improve academic performance.

Topic 5: Importance of Data

Explanation

Data is often called the "new oil" because it drives modern business decisions. Without accurate data, organizations cannot effectively analyze performance or predict future outcomes.

Why Data is Important

  • Decision Making: Supports evidence-based decisions.
  • Business Growth: Identifies opportunities and risks.
  • Customer Understanding: Improves customer experience.
  • Operational Efficiency: Identifies inefficiencies and bottlenecks.
  • Competitive Advantage: Provides market insights.

Topic 6: Governance of Master Data

Explanation

Master Data refers to core business information used across an organization.

Examples include:

  • Customer Data
  • Product Data
  • Supplier Data
  • Employee Data

Master Data Governance ensures that this information remains accurate, consistent, and secure.

Objectives of Master Data Governance

  • Improve data quality.
  • Ensure consistency.
  • Reduce duplication.
  • Enhance compliance.
  • Improve decision-making.

Components of Master Data Governance

Data Standards

↓
Data Ownership

Data Security
↓
Continuous Monitoring

Benefits

Better Data Quality Accurate information
Reduced Errors Consistent records
Improved Reporting Reliable insights
Regulatory Compliance Meets legal requirements

Challenges

  • Data duplication
  • Poor data quality
  • Lack of ownership
  • Integration issues

Example 1 (Business): SAP provides Master Data Management solutions that help organizations maintain consistent data across departments.

Example 2 (Practical): A university maintains a centralized database for student information to avoid duplicate records.

Quick Revision Summary

  • Value Drivers are factors that influence business success.
  • Financial, operational, customer, and innovation drivers affect performance.

  • Master Data Governance ensures data quality, consistency, and security.
