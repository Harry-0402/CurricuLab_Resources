Unit 2: Understanding Business

Introduction

Before analyzing data, it is important to understand how a business operates. Every organization consists of stakeholders, processes, departments, and decision-makers who work together to achieve business goals. Data becomes meaningful only when it is connected to business operations and objectives.

This unit introduces stakeholder mapping, business intelligence, end-to-end process mapping, major business processes such as Hire-to-Retire, Source-to-Pay, Record-to-Report, Order-to-Cash, and the role of Global Process Owners.

Topic 1: Stakeholder Mapping

Explanation

A stakeholder is any individual, group, or organization that can affect or be affected by a business's activities.

Stakeholder Mapping is the process of identifying stakeholders and understanding their influence, interests, and relationships with the organization.

Objectives of Stakeholder Mapping

  • Identify key stakeholders.
  • Understand stakeholder expectations.
  • Improve communication.
  • Reduce conflicts.
  • Support decision-making.

Types of Stakeholders

Internal Stakeholders

People within the organization.

Examples:

  • Employees
  • Managers
  • Owners
  • Shareholders

External Stakeholders

People outside the organization.

Examples:

  • Customers
  • Suppliers
  • Government
  • Investors
  • Society

Stakeholder Mapping Matrix

Stakeholder Interest Influence
Customers High Medium
Shareholders High High
Employees High Medium
Government Medium High
Suppliers Medium Medium

Example 1 (Business): Amazon manages relationships with customers, employees, investors, suppliers, and regulators through stakeholder management practices.

Example 2 (Practical): A college considers students, teachers, parents, and university authorities as important stakeholders.

Topic 2: Business Intelligence (BI)

Explanation

Business Intelligence (BI) refers to technologies, processes, and tools used to collect, analyze, and present business information for decision-making.

Definition

Business Intelligence transforms raw data into useful information that supports strategic and operational decisions.

Components of Business Intelligence

Data Sources
↓
Data Warehouse
↓
Data Processing
↓
Reporting & Dashboards
↓
Decision Making

Objectives of BI

  • Improve business performance.
  • Support strategic planning.
  • Identify trends.
  • Monitor KPIs.
  • Enhance customer satisfaction.

Benefits of BI

Benefit Description
Better Decisions Data-driven decisions
Increased Efficiency Faster reporting
Improved Planning Better forecasting
Competitive Advantage Market insights

Example 1 (Business): Walmart uses Business Intelligence systems to analyze sales trends and inventory levels.

Example 2 (Practical): A retail shop owner uses sales dashboards to monitor daily revenue and product performance.

Topic 3: Introduction to End-to-End Process Mapping

Explanation

End-to-End Process Mapping is the visual representation of all activities involved in completing a business process from beginning to end.

It helps organizations understand workflows, identify inefficiencies, and improve performance.

Objectives

  • Understand business processes.
  • Identify bottlenecks.
  • Improve efficiency.
  • Standardize operations.
  • Support automation.

Generic Process Mapping Flow

Input
↓
Process
↓
Activity
↓
Output
↓
Customer

Advantages

  • Improved transparency
  • Better coordination
  • Faster operations
  • Reduced costs

Example 1 (Business): A manufacturing company maps its procurement process to identify delays in purchasing raw materials.

Example 2 (Practical): A hospital maps patient admission and discharge processes to reduce waiting time.

Topic 4: End-to-End Process Mapping in a Firm

Business organizations perform several standard end-to-end processes.

A. Hire-to-Retire (H2R)

Explanation

Hire-to-Retire covers the complete employee lifecycle from recruitment to retirement.

Process Flow

Job Requirement
↓
Recruitment
↓
Selection
↓
Hiring
↓
Training
↓
Performance Management
↓
Promotion
↓
Retirement/Exit

Objectives

  • Efficient workforce management.
  • Employee development.
  • Talent retention.

Example 1 (Business): Infosys manages employee recruitment, training, and career development through a structured H2R process.

Example 2 (Practical): A company recruits a graduate, trains them, evaluates performance, and eventually processes retirement benefits.

B. Source-to-Pay (S2P)

Explanation

Source-to-Pay manages procurement activities from identifying suppliers to making payments.

Process Flow

Requirement Identification
↓
Supplier Selection
↓
Purchase Order
↓
Goods Receipt
↓
Invoice Verification
↓
Payment

Benefits

  • Better supplier management
  • Cost control
  • Improved procurement efficiency

Example 1 (Business): Toyota uses Source-to-Pay systems to manage supplier relationships and procurement operations.

Example 2 (Practical): A restaurant purchases ingredients from vendors and processes payments after receiving supplies.

C. Record-to-Report (R2R)

Explanation

Record-to-Report is the financial process that collects, processes, and reports financial information.

Process Flow

Transaction Recording
↓
Ledger Posting
↓
Trial Balance
↓
Financial Statements
↓
Reporting

Objectives

  • Ensure accurate reporting.
  • Support compliance.
  • Improve financial transparency.

Example 1 (Business): A multinational company prepares monthly financial reports using an R2R process.

Example 2 (Practical): A small business records transactions and prepares financial statements at year-end.

D. Order-to-Cash (O2C)

Explanation

Order-to-Cash covers the complete customer sales cycle from receiving an order to collecting payment.

Process Flow

Customer Order
↓
Order Processing
↓
Product Delivery
↓
Invoice Generation
↓
Payment Collection

Benefits

  • Faster revenue generation.
  • Improved customer service.
  • Better cash flow management.

Example 1 (Business): Flipkart processes customer orders, delivers products, and collects payments through an O2C system.

Example 2 (Practical): A local electronics store receives an order, delivers the product, and collects payment.

Topic 5: Global Process Owners (GPOs)

Explanation

Global Process Owners are senior managers responsible for managing and standardizing business processes across an organization.

Responsibilities

  • Process design
  • Process improvement
  • Standardization
  • Performance monitoring
  • Compliance management

Importance

Benefit Explanation
Consistency Uniform processes
Efficiency Improved operations
Accountability Clear ownership
Standardization Global best practices

Role of a Global Process Owner

Define Process Standards
↓
Monitor Performance
↓
Identify Improvements
↓
Implement Changes
↓
Measure Results

Example 1 (Business): Unilever appoints global process owners to standardize procurement and finance operations worldwide.

Example 2 (Practical): A large educational institution assigns process owners to standardize admission procedures across campuses.

  • Global Process Owners ensure process standardization and efficiency.
