Unit 1: Business Correspondence

Introduction

Business Correspondence refers to the formal exchange of information between individuals, organizations, customers, suppliers, employees, and other stakeholders. It plays a vital role in establishing professional relationships, conveying information, resolving issues, and supporting business operations.

Effective business correspondence helps organizations maintain goodwill, improve communication efficiency, and create a professional image. In the modern business environment, correspondence takes various forms such as letters, e-mails, memoranda, proposals, reports, and meeting documents.

For MBA (Business Analytics) students, business correspondence is important because managers and analysts frequently communicate findings, recommendations, project updates, and business decisions through formal communication channels.

Topic 1: Principles of Effective Writing

Explanation

Effective writing is the foundation of successful business communication. A well-written message ensures that the receiver understands the intended information accurately and promptly.

Business writing should be professional, clear, concise, and purposeful.

Principles of Effective Writing

1. Clarity

The message should be easy to understand.

Example:
Instead of writing:
"The issue concerning inventory management requires immediate attention."

Write:
"Inventory shortages need immediate action."

2. Conciseness

Avoid unnecessary words and lengthy explanations.

3. Completeness

Provide all necessary information.

4. Correctness

Ensure grammatical accuracy and factual correctness.

5. Courtesy

Maintain politeness and respect.

6. Concreteness

Use specific facts rather than vague statements.

7. Coherence

Ideas should flow logically.

The 7 C's of Business Communication

PrincipleMeaning
ClarityEasy to understand
ConcisenessBrief and direct
CompletenessFull information
CorrectnessAccurate information
CourtesyPolite communication
ConcretenessSpecific details
CoherenceLogical flow

Example 1 (Business Example)

A business analyst presenting sales trends should use clear and concise language so management can make quick decisions.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A student writing an assignment follows the 7 C's to ensure the professor understands the content effectively.

Topic 2: Letter Writing

Explanation

Business letters are formal written communications exchanged between organizations, customers, suppliers, and other stakeholders.

They are used for inquiries, complaints, requests, confirmations, and official communication.

Parts of a Business Letter

Sender's Address
↓
Date
↓
Receiver's Address
↓
Subject
↓
Salutation
↓
Body of Letter
↓
Closing
↓
Signature

Types of Business Letters

1. Inquiry Letter

Used to obtain information.

2. Complaint Letter

Used to report dissatisfaction.

3. Adjustment Letter

Response to complaints.

4. Sales Letter

Promotes products or services.

5. Order Letter

Places an order.

6. Acknowledgement Letter

Confirms receipt of information.

Sample Business Letter Format

ABC Pvt. Ltd.
Mumbai

Date: 15 January 2025

To,
The Sales Manager
XYZ Ltd.

Subject: Inquiry Regarding Software Solutions

Dear Sir/Madam,

We would like to obtain information regarding your business analytics software solutions.

Kindly provide product details and pricing information.

Thank you.

Yours Faithfully,
Manager
ABC Pvt. Ltd.

Example 1 (Business Example)

A company writes to a software vendor requesting details about data analytics tools.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A student writes a formal letter requesting an internship certificate.

Topic 3: CVs and Applications

Explanation

A Curriculum Vitae (CV) is a document that presents a person's educational qualifications, skills, achievements, and work experience.

An application letter accompanies the CV and explains why the candidate is suitable for a position.

Components of a CV

  • Personal Information
  • Name
  • Contact Details
  • Career Objective
  • Educational Qualifications
  • Skills
  • Experience
  • Certifications
  • Achievements
  • References

Sample CV Structure

SectionDetails
Personal DetailsName, Contact
ObjectiveCareer Goal
EducationAcademic Qualifications
SkillsTechnical & Soft Skills
ExperiencePrevious Work
AchievementsAwards & Certifications

Tips for Effective CV Writing

  • Keep it concise.
  • Use professional language.
  • Highlight achievements.
  • Avoid spelling mistakes.
  • Customize according to the job.

Example 1 (Business Example)

A Business Analytics graduate highlights skills such as Power BI, Excel, SQL, and Python in the CV.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A student applying for an internship prepares a CV showcasing academic projects and certifications.

Topic 4: Memoranda (Memo)

Explanation

A Memorandum (Memo) is an internal communication document used within an organization.

It is shorter than a formal letter and is commonly used to communicate policies, instructions, announcements, or updates.

Features of Memo

Topic 5: Business Claims and Responses

Explanation

A Business Claim is a formal complaint made by a customer regarding defective products, delayed services, or unsatisfactory performance.

Topic 6: Business Proposals

Explanation

A Business Proposal is a document that presents a business idea, project, service, or solution to a potential client or decision-maker.

Its purpose is to persuade the reader to approve a project or business opportunity.

Components of Business Proposal

  1. Title
  2. Executive Summary
  3. Problem Statement
  4. Proposed Solution
  5. Benefits
  6. Cost Estimate
  7. Conclusion

Proposal Flow

↓
Analysis
↓
Solution
↓
Benefits
↓
Approval

Example 1 (Business Example)

A consulting company submits a proposal for implementing a Business Intelligence system.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

Topic 7: Meetings

Explanation

A Meeting is a gathering of individuals for discussion, decision-making, information sharing, or problem-solving.

Meetings are important for organizational coordination.

Objectives of Meetings

  • Review performance

Topic 8: Agenda

Explanation

An Agenda is a list of topics to be discussed during a meeting.

It ensures that meetings remain organized and productive.

Components of Agenda

  • Meeting title
  • Date and time
  • Venue
  • List of discussion points
  • Responsible persons

Sample Agenda

Agenda

Meeting: Project Review Meeting

1. Review Previous Decisions
2. Project Status Update
3. Budget Discussion
4. Action Plan
5. Closing Remarks

Example 1 (Business Example)

A board meeting agenda includes financial review and strategic planning discussions.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

Students preparing for an event create an agenda to allocate responsibilities.

Topic 9: Minutes of Meeting (MoM)

Explanation

Minutes of Meeting (MoM) are the official written records of discussions, decisions, and actions taken during a meeting.

They provide accountability and future reference.

Contents of Minutes

  • Meeting details
  • Attendees
  • Discussion points
  • Decisions made
  • Action items

Format of Minutes

Meeting Details
↓
Attendance
↓
Discussion Summary
↓
Decisions Taken
↓
Action Items

Importance

  • Maintains official records.
  • Tracks responsibilities.
  • Ensures follow-up actions.

Example 1 (Business Example)

Project managers record meeting minutes to track project progress and assigned tasks.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

Student committee members record decisions regarding annual event planning.
