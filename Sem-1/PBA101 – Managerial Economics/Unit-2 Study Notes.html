Unit 2: Demand Analysis and Demand Forecasting

Introduction

Demand is one of the most important concepts in Managerial Economics. Every business decision related to production, pricing, marketing, and investment depends on the expected demand for a product or service. Understanding consumer demand helps managers estimate future sales, allocate resources efficiently, and maximize profits.

This unit explains the theory of demand, utility analysis, elasticity of demand, revenue concepts, and various techniques used for demand forecasting.

Topic 1: Theory of Demand

Explanation

Demand refers to the quantity of a product or service that consumers are willing and able to purchase at a given price during a specific period.

For demand to exist, three conditions must be satisfied:

  • Desire for a product
  • Ability to pay
  • Willingness to pay

Definition

Demand is the quantity of a commodity that consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices during a given period.

Determinants of Demand

  • Price of the Product
  • Consumer Income
  • Prices of Related Goods
  • Consumer Preferences and Tastes
  • Population Size
  • Advertising and Promotion
  • Future Expectations
  • Seasonal Factors

Law of Demand

The Law of Demand states:

"Other things remaining constant, demand for a product increases when its price falls and decreases when its price rises."

Thus, price and demand have an inverse relationship.

Demand Curve

The demand curve slopes downward from left to right because lower prices encourage higher purchases.

Example 1 (Business)

A smartphone company experiences increased sales when it reduces the price of its latest model.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A consumer buys more fruits when prices are lower in the market.

Topic 2: Types of Demand and Their Characteristics

Explanation

Demand can take different forms depending on the product and consumer behavior.

1. Individual Demand

Demand by a single consumer.
Example: A student's demand for notebooks.

2. Market Demand

Total demand of all consumers in a market.
Example: Total demand for smartphones in India.

3. Direct Demand

Demand for goods used directly for consumption.
Example: Food products.

4. Derived Demand

Demand arising due to demand for another product.
Example: Demand for steel depends on demand for automobiles.

5. Joint Demand

Two or more products demanded together.
Example: Car and fuel.

6. Composite Demand

A product used for multiple purposes.
Example: Electricity used in homes, offices, and industries.

Example 1 (Business)

Demand for automobile tyres increases when automobile production rises.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Demand for printers increases with demand for computers.

Topic 3: Utility Analysis

Explanation

Utility refers to the satisfaction obtained from consuming a product or service. The theory explains how consumers make purchasing decisions.

Features of Utility

  • Subjective in nature
  • Varies from person to person
  • Depends on wants
  • Cannot be measured physically

Types of Utility

Type Meaning
Form Utility Created by production
Place Utility Created by transportation
Time Utility Created by storage
Possession Utility Created by transfer of ownership

Example 1 (Business)

A furniture manufacturer creates form utility by converting wood into furniture.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Online delivery services create place utility by bringing products to customers.

Topic 4: Cardinal Utility Analysis

Explanation

Cardinal Utility assumes that utility can be measured numerically in units called "Utils."

Assumptions

  • Utility is measurable.
  • Consumer is rational.
  • Marginal utility decreases with consumption.
  • Income remains constant.

Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility

The law states:

As consumption of a commodity increases, marginal utility obtained from each additional unit decreases.

Illustration

Units Consumed Total Utility Marginal Utility
1 20 20
2 35 15
3 45 10
4 50 5
5 50 0

Example 1 (Business)

A restaurant customer enjoys the first pizza slice more than the fifth slice.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

The first glass of water provides greater satisfaction than subsequent glasses.

Topic 5: Ordinal Utility Analysis

Explanation

Ordinal Utility argues that utility cannot be measured numerically but can be ranked. Consumers can compare preferences and arrange them according to satisfaction levels.

Features

  • Utility is not measurable.
  • Preferences can be ranked.
  • More realistic than Cardinal Utility.
  • Forms the basis of Indifference Curve Analysis.

Example 1 (Business)

A customer prefers Brand A over Brand B and Brand B over Brand C.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A student ranks career options based on personal preference.

Topic 6: Elasticity of Demand and Its Measurement

Explanation

Elasticity of Demand measures the responsiveness of demand to changes in various factors such as price, income, and prices of related goods.

A. Price Elasticity of Demand

Meaning

Measures responsiveness of demand to changes in price.

Formula:

$$E_d=\frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta P}$$

Types

Type Elasticity
Perfectly Elastic Infinite
Perfectly Inelastic Zero
Relatively Elastic Greater than 1
Relatively Inelastic Less than 1
Unitary Elastic Equal to 1

Example 1 (Business)

Luxury cars have highly elastic demand.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Salt has highly inelastic demand.

B. Income Elasticity of Demand

Meaning

Measures change in demand due to change in income.

Formula:

$$E_y=\frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta Y}$$

Where:

  • $Y$ = Income

Example 1 (Business)

Demand for premium smartphones rises as income increases.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

People buy more branded clothes when income increases.

C. Cross Elasticity of Demand

Meaning

Measures change in demand for one product due to change in price of another product.

Formula:

$$E_{xy}=\frac{\%\Delta Q_x}{\%\Delta P_y}$$

Example 1 (Business)

Increase in coffee prices may increase demand for tea.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Increase in petrol prices may reduce demand for cars.

D. Arc Elasticity of Demand

Meaning

Measures elasticity between two points on a demand curve.

Formula:

$$E_d=\frac{Q_2-Q_1}{P_2-P_1}\times\frac{P_1+P_2}{Q_1+Q_2}$$

Example 1 (Business)

Used when a company changes prices significantly.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Useful for comparing demand before and after a discount sale.

E. Advertising Elasticity of Demand

Meaning

Measures responsiveness of demand to advertising expenditure.

Formula:

$$E_a=\frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta A}$$

Where:

  • $A$ = Advertising Expenditure

Example 1 (Business)

A major advertising campaign increases demand for a soft drink.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Social media advertisements influence purchase decisions.

Topic 7: Estimation of Revenue

Explanation

Revenue refers to income earned from sales.

Total Revenue (TR)

$$TR = Price \times Quantity\ Sold$$

Average Revenue (AR)

$$AR=\frac{TR}{Q}$$

Marginal Revenue (MR)

$$MR=\frac{\Delta TR}{\Delta Q}$$

Relationship Between Revenue and Elasticity

Elasticity Marginal Revenue
>1 Positive
=1 Zero
<1 Negative

Example 1 (Business)

An e-commerce company evaluates revenue changes after reducing prices.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A local shopkeeper estimates daily revenue based on sales volume.

Topic 8: Techniques of Demand Forecasting

Explanation

Demand Forecasting is the process of estimating future demand for a product. It helps businesses plan production, inventory, manpower, and investments.

Importance of Demand Forecasting

  • Production Planning
  • Inventory Management
  • Capacity Planning
  • Budget Preparation
  • Profit Planning

Techniques of Demand Forecasting

A. Survey Method

Future demand is estimated through customer surveys.

Advantages

  • Direct information
  • Easy interpretation

Limitations

  • Expensive
  • Biased responses

B. Expert Opinion Method

Forecasts are based on experts' judgments.

Example

Market research consultants predict future demand.

C. Delphi Method

A group of experts independently provide forecasts until consensus is reached.

D. Trend Projection Method

Uses past sales data to estimate future demand.

Flowchart:

Past Sales Data
↓
Trend Analysis
↓
Future Demand Estimate

E. Regression Analysis

Uses statistical relationships between variables.

Example:

Demand depends on income and price.

F. Barometric Method

Uses economic indicators to predict demand.

Examples:

  • GDP Growth
  • Inflation
  • Employment Levels

Example 1 (Business)

An automobile company forecasts future sales using historical sales data and economic indicators.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A retailer increases stock before the festive season based on expected demand.
