Unit 4: Market Structure and Product Pricing

Introduction

Every business operates within a market structure that influences pricing, production, competition, and profitability. Understanding different market structures helps managers make effective pricing decisions and develop competitive strategies.

This unit explains perfect and imperfect markets, price determination, pricing under different market structures, factor pricing, and the Marginal Productivity Theory.

Topic 1: Market Structure and Product Pricing

Explanation

A market structure refers to the characteristics of a market that influence the behavior of buyers and sellers.

Features Determining Market Structure

  • Number of buyers and sellers
  • Nature of products
  • Freedom of entry and exit
  • Availability of information
  • Degree of competition

Classification of Market Structures

Perfect Market

A market where competition is very high and no individual buyer or seller can influence price.

Imperfect Market

A market where sellers have some degree of control over prices.

Example 1 (Business)

The agricultural wheat market often resembles perfect competition.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Branded smartphone markets operate under imperfect competition.

Topic 2: Conditions of Perfect Competition

Explanation

Perfect Competition is a market structure characterized by a large number of buyers and sellers dealing in homogeneous products.

Conditions

  1. Large Number of Buyers and Sellers: No single participant can influence market price.
  2. Homogeneous Product: Products offered by all sellers are identical.
  3. Free Entry and Exit: Firms can enter or leave the market freely.
  4. Perfect Knowledge: Buyers and sellers possess complete market information.
  5. Perfect Mobility of Factors: Resources can move freely from one use to another.
  6. No Selling Costs: Advertising and promotional expenses are absent.

Example 1 (Business)

Farmers selling identical wheat in an open market.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Vegetable sellers in a highly competitive local market.

Topic 3: Price Determination Through Demand and Supply

Explanation

Price is determined by the interaction of demand and supply forces.

Law of Supply

The Law of Supply states:

"Other things remaining constant, supply increases when price increases."

Price Determination Process

Demand > Supply
↓
Price Rises
↓
Demand < Supply
↓
Price Falls
↓
Demand = Supply (Equilibrium Price)

Example 1 (Business)

Shortage of semiconductor chips increases market prices.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Vegetable prices rise when supply decreases due to heavy rainfall.

Topic 4: Equilibrium Price

Explanation

Equilibrium Price is the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.

At this point:

$$\text{Quantity Demanded} = \text{Quantity Supplied}$$

Neither shortage nor surplus exists.

Example Table

Price (₹) Demand (Units) Supply (Units)
10 100 40
20 80 80
30 60 120

At ₹20, demand equals supply, creating equilibrium.

Example 1 (Business)

The market price of crude oil adjusts according to global demand and supply.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Fruit prices stabilize when supply matches consumer demand.

Topic 5: Price Behaviour and Time Element

Explanation

Alfred Marshall emphasized that time plays an important role in price determination.

Time Periods

A. Market Period

Very short period where supply is fixed.

B. Short Run

Some factors are fixed.

C. Long Run

All factors become variable.

D. Secular Period

Very long period involving technological and population changes.

Impact of Time on Price

Time Period Supply Response
Market Period Fixed
Short Run Limited
Long Run Flexible
Secular Period Fully Adjustable

Example 1 (Business)

A sudden increase in demand for masks during a pandemic causes immediate price increases.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Housing supply adjusts gradually over several years.

Topic 6: Market Price and Normal Price

Explanation

Market Price

Price prevailing at a particular time due to temporary demand and supply conditions.

Normal Price

Price prevailing in the long run under normal market conditions.

Comparison

Basis Market Price Normal Price
Time Period Short Run Long Run
Influenced By Temporary Factors Permanent Factors
Stability Less Stable More Stable

Example 1 (Business)

Airfare increases during holiday seasons.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Umbrella prices rise during the rainy season but return to normal afterward.

Topic 7: Pricing Under Perfect Competition

Explanation

Under perfect competition, firms are price takers. They cannot set prices independently.

Short-Run Pricing

A firm continues production if:

$$\text{Price} \ge \text{Average Variable Cost}$$

Profit may be:

  • Supernormal Profit
  • Normal Profit
  • Loss

Long-Run Pricing

In the long run:

  • Firms earn only normal profits.
  • Entry and exit of firms eliminate excess profits.

Example 1 (Business)

Agricultural producers generally accept prevailing market prices.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Milk sellers often charge market-determined prices.

Topic 8: Pricing Under Monopoly

Explanation

A monopoly exists when a single seller dominates the market.

Features

  • Single seller
  • No close substitutes
  • High barriers to entry
  • Price maker

Pricing in Monopoly

The monopolist determines output first and then price.

Profit is maximized where:

$$\text{Marginal Revenue} = \text{Marginal Cost}$$ $$\text{MR} = \text{MC}$$

Example 1 (Business)

A public utility company supplying water in a city.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A patented medicine produced by a single company.

Topic 9: Pricing Under Monopolistic Competition

Explanation

Monopolistic Competition combines elements of competition and monopoly.

Features

  • Many sellers
  • Product differentiation
  • Moderate entry barriers
  • Advertising plays an important role

Pricing

Firms have some control over prices because products are differentiated.

Example 1 (Business)

Restaurant industry.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Different brands of toothpaste.

Topic 10: Pricing Under Oligopoly

Explanation

An oligopoly exists when a few large firms dominate the market.

Features

  • Few sellers
  • Interdependence among firms
  • Significant barriers to entry
  • Strategic decision-making

Pricing Behavior

Each firm's pricing decision affects competitors.

Examples include:

  • Automobile industry
  • Airline industry
  • Telecom sector

Example 1 (Business)

Major telecom companies compete while monitoring rivals' pricing decisions.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Smartphone manufacturers respond quickly to competitors' price changes.

Topic 11: Kinked Demand Curve

Explanation

The Kinked Demand Curve Theory explains price rigidity in oligopoly markets.

Assumption

  • Competitors follow price decreases.
  • Competitors do not follow price increases.

Result:

  • Prices tend to remain stable.

Flowchart

Firm Raises Price
↓
Competitors Do Not Follow
↓
Customers Shift Away → Sales Fall
↓
Firm Lowers Price
↓
Competitors Follow
↓
Little Gain in Market Share

Example 1 (Business)

Telecom companies often maintain stable pricing plans.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Petrol stations in the same area often charge similar prices.

Topic 12: Discriminating Prices (Price Discrimination)

Explanation

Price Discrimination refers to charging different prices to different customers for the same product.

Conditions

  • Market segmentation
  • No resale possibility
  • Monopoly power

Types

First-Degree

Different price for each customer.

Second-Degree

Price varies according to quantity purchased.

Third-Degree

Different prices for different customer groups.

Example 1 (Business)

Airlines charge different fares for the same flight.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Student discounts at cinemas.

Topic 13: Factor Pricing

Explanation

Factor Pricing refers to determining prices of factors of production:

  • Land → Rent
  • Labour → Wages
  • Capital → Interest
  • Entrepreneurship → Profit

Importance

  • Resource allocation
  • Income distribution
  • Productivity improvement

Example 1 (Business)

Companies determine employee wages based on skill levels.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Banks determine interest rates paid on deposits.

Topic 14: Pricing of Labour as a Factor of Production

Explanation

Labour pricing refers to determination of wages.

Factors Affecting Wages

  • Demand for labour
  • Supply of labour
  • Skill level
  • Productivity
  • Government regulations
  • Trade unions

Example 1 (Business)

Software engineers receive higher wages due to specialized skills.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

Experienced electricians earn more than unskilled workers.

Topic 15: Marginal Productivity Theory of Factor Pricing

Explanation

The Marginal Productivity Theory states that a factor is paid according to its contribution to production.

Principle

A firm employs additional units of a factor until:

$$\text{Value of Marginal Product} = \text{Factor Price}$$

Formula

$$\text{VMP} = \text{MP} \times \text{P}$$

Where:

  • $\text{VMP}$ = Value of Marginal Product
  • $\text{MP}$ = Marginal Product
  • $\text{P}$ = Product Price

Importance

  • Wage determination
  • Resource allocation
  • Productivity evaluation

Example 1 (Business)

A company hires workers until the value generated by an additional worker equals wage cost.

Example 2 (Daily Life)

A freelancer is paid based on the value of services provided.

Quick Revision Summary

  • Market structure affects pricing and competition.
  • Perfect competition involves many sellers and homogeneous products.
  • Equilibrium price occurs when demand equals supply.
  • Market price is short-term; normal price is long-term.
  • Monopoly has a single seller with pricing power.
  • Monopolistic competition involves differentiated products.
  • Oligopoly consists of a few interdependent firms.
  • Kinked demand curve explains price rigidity.
  • Price discrimination charges different prices to different customers.
  • Marginal Productivity Theory explains factor pricing.

Important Terms and Definitions

  • Market Structure: Characteristics of a market affecting competition.
  • Perfect Competition: Market with many sellers and identical products.
  • Monopoly: Market with a single seller.
  • Monopolistic Competition: Market with many sellers and differentiated products.
  • Oligopoly: Market dominated by a few firms.
  • Equilibrium Price: Price where demand equals supply.
  • Price Discrimination: Charging different prices for the same product.
  • Factor Pricing: Determination of payments to factors of production.
  • Labour Pricing: Determination of wages.
  • Marginal Productivity Theory: Payment based on contribution to production.
