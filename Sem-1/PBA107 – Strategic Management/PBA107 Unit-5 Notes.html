Unit 5: Portfolio and Analytical Models, Strategy Evaluation and Implementation

Introduction

After formulating business-level and corporate-level strategies, organizations must evaluate whether those strategies are suitable and capable of achieving organizational goals. Strategic managers also need tools that help allocate resources among different business units and identify future growth opportunities.

Portfolio analysis models such as the BCG Matrix and GE/McKinsey Matrix help organizations manage multiple businesses effectively. Additionally, strategy evaluation and implementation ensure that strategies are not only designed properly but are also executed successfully.

For Business Analytics students, these models are important because they support data-driven decision-making, performance measurement, resource allocation, and strategic planning.

Topic 1: BCG Matrix (Boston Consulting Group Matrix)

Explanation

The BCG Matrix is a portfolio analysis tool developed by the Boston Consulting Group (BCG). It helps organizations evaluate different business units or products based on:

  1. Market Growth Rate
  2. Relative Market Share

The purpose is to decide where resources should be invested.

BCG Matrix Structure

Market Growth Rate High Market Share Low Market Share
High Growth Star Question Mark
Low Growth Cash Cow Dog

1. Stars

  • High market share
  • High market growth
  • Require significant investment
  • Future cash generators

Example

A rapidly growing technology product leading its market.

2. Cash Cows

  • High market share
  • Low market growth
  • Generate stable cash flow
  • Require limited investment

Example

A well-established consumer product.

3. Question Marks

  • Low market share
  • High market growth
  • Require heavy investment
  • Future success uncertain

Example

A startup entering a rapidly growing industry.

4. Dogs

  • Low market share
  • Low market growth
  • Generate limited profits
  • Often candidates for divestment

Example

An outdated product with declining demand.

Advantages of BCG Matrix

  • Simple to understand
  • Supports resource allocation
  • Helps identify growth opportunities
  • Assists portfolio management

Limitations

  • Considers only two variables
  • Oversimplifies business situations
  • Ignores external factors

Example 1 (Business Example)

Coca-Cola manages a portfolio of products where some brands generate stable cash flows while others require investment for growth.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A student may classify subjects into strong, average, developing, and weak categories to allocate study time effectively.

Topic 2: GE/McKinsey Matrix

Explanation

The GE/McKinsey Matrix is a more advanced portfolio analysis model developed by General Electric and McKinsey & Company.

Unlike the BCG Matrix, it uses:

  1. Industry Attractiveness
  2. Business Unit Strength

GE/McKinsey Matrix Structure

Industry Attractiveness Business Strength Strategic Decision
High High Invest/Grow
Medium Medium Selective Growth
Low Low Harvest/Divest

Factors Affecting Industry Attractiveness

  • Market size
  • Market growth
  • Profitability
  • Competitive intensity
  • Technological developments

Factors Affecting Business Strength

  • Brand image
  • Market share
  • Innovation capability
  • Financial resources
  • Customer loyalty

Advantages

  • More comprehensive than BCG Matrix
  • Considers multiple factors
  • Supports strategic decision-making

Limitations

  • Complex analysis
  • Requires extensive data
  • Subjective evaluation

Example 1 (Business Example)

A multinational company evaluates its various business divisions using industry attractiveness and competitive strength before allocating investments.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A student evaluates career options based on industry demand and personal skills before choosing a specialization.

Topic 3: Corporate Parenting

Explanation

Corporate Parenting refers to the role of corporate headquarters in creating value for its business units.

The parent company provides support, guidance, resources, and expertise to improve overall performance.

Objectives of Corporate Parenting

  • Improve business performance
  • Share resources
  • Develop synergies
  • Enhance competitiveness

Parenting Functions

Strategic Guidance
↓
Resource Allocation
↓
Performance Monitoring
↓
Knowledge Sharing
↓
Business Growth

Advantages

  • Better coordination
  • Improved resource utilization
  • Enhanced synergy
  • Stronger performance

Example 1 (Business Example)

Alphabet Inc. provides strategic direction and resources to its various business units.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A university administration providing resources and support to different departments demonstrates corporate parenting.

Topic 4: CIT (Critical Issues Tracking)

Explanation

CIT (Critical Issues Tracking) is a strategic monitoring approach used to identify, track, and manage critical issues that may affect organizational performance.

It helps management focus on important strategic challenges and opportunities.

Steps in CIT

Identify Critical Issues
↓
Analyze Impact
↓
Assign Responsibility
↓
Monitor Progress
↓
Take Corrective Action

Importance

  • Early problem identification
  • Better decision-making
  • Improved risk management
  • Continuous monitoring

Example 1 (Business Example)

A company continuously monitors cybersecurity risks and supply chain disruptions as critical issues.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A student preparing for examinations identifies weak subjects and tracks improvement regularly.

Topic 5: CASCADE

Explanation

CASCADE is a strategic alignment framework that ensures organizational goals are translated into departmental and individual objectives.

It helps connect strategic vision with daily activities.

CASCADE Process

Vision
↓
Mission
↓
Strategic Objectives
↓
Department Goals
↓
Individual Goals
↓
Performance Measurement

Benefits

  • Better coordination
  • Goal alignment
  • Increased accountability
  • Improved performance management

Example 1 (Business Example)

A company converts its strategic goal of customer satisfaction into departmental KPIs and employee targets.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A university's goal of academic excellence is translated into faculty objectives and student performance targets.

Topic 6: Strategy Evaluation

Explanation

Strategy Evaluation is the process of assessing whether a strategy is achieving desired objectives.

It helps organizations determine whether changes are required.

Importance of Strategy Evaluation

  • Measures performance
  • Identifies deviations
  • Supports corrective actions
  • Improves strategic effectiveness

Criteria for Strategy Evaluation

1. Suitability

Examines whether the strategy addresses environmental opportunities and threats.

Question: Is this the right strategy?

2. Acceptability

Determines whether stakeholders accept the expected outcomes.

Question: Is the strategy acceptable to stakeholders?

3. Feasibility

Evaluates whether the organization possesses sufficient resources and capabilities.

Question: Can the strategy be implemented successfully?

SAF Framework

Suitability
↓
Acceptability
↓
Feasibility
↓
Strategy Approval

Example 1 (Business Example)

Before launching a new product, management evaluates whether the strategy is suitable, acceptable, and feasible.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A student deciding to pursue higher education evaluates career relevance, family support, and financial feasibility.

  • Achieves organizational goals
  • Enhances coordination
  • Improves accountability

Topic 8: Resource Allocation

Explanation

Resource Allocation involves distributing organizational resources among different departments, projects, and business units.

Resources include:

  • Financial resources
  • Human resources
  • Technological resources
  • Physical resources

Objectives

  • Maximize efficiency
  • Support strategic priorities
  • Improve productivity

Example 1 (Business Example)

A company investing more funds in high-growth products than declining products.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A student allocating more study time to difficult subjects before examinations.

Topic 9: Structure and Strategy

Explanation

Organizational structure determines how responsibilities, authority, and communication are organized.

According to strategic management principles:

"Structure follows Strategy."

This means organizational structure should support strategic objectives.

Types of Organizational Structures

Structure Description
Functional Organized by departments
Divisional Organized by products or regions
Matrix Combination of functional and divisional structures

Relationship Between Structure and Strategy

Strategy Formulation
↓
Organizational Structure Design
↓
Role Assignment
↓
Coordination
↓
Performance Achievement

Example 1 (Business Example)

A multinational company may adopt a divisional structure to support global expansion.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A college creating separate departments for different academic programs.

Topic 10: Organizational Culture

Explanation

Organizational Culture refers to shared values, beliefs, norms, and behaviors that influence how employees work and interact.

Culture significantly affects strategy implementation.

Components of Organizational Culture

  • Values
  • Beliefs
  • Traditions
  • Work practices
  • Leadership style

Importance of Organizational Culture

  • Improves employee commitment
  • Supports strategic objectives
  • Enhances teamwork
  • Encourages innovation

Positive Culture vs Negative Culture

Positive Culture Negative Culture
Innovation Resistance to change
Teamwork Internal conflicts
Employee Engagement Low morale
Learning Orientation Poor adaptability

Example 1 (Business Example)

Google is known for an innovative culture that supports creativity and experimentation.

Example 2 (Practical Example)

A college promoting collaboration and continuous learning develops a positive academic culture.
