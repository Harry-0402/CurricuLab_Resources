Unit 2: Assignment Models and Transportation Models

Introduction

Organizations frequently face allocation and distribution problems. For example, a company may need to assign employees to jobs, machines to tasks, or distribute products from factories to warehouses at minimum cost.

Assignment Models help allocate resources efficiently, while Transportation Models help determine the most economical method of transporting goods from sources to destinations. Both techniques are important tools in Operations Research and managerial decision-making.

Part A: Assignment Models

Topic 1: Concept and Approach of Assignment Model

Explanation

An Assignment Problem is a special type of Linear Programming Problem where resources are assigned to tasks in the most efficient way.

The objective may be:

  • Minimize cost
  • Minimize time
  • Maximize profit
  • Maximize efficiency

Characteristics of Assignment Problems

  • Equal number of resources and tasks (in balanced cases).
  • One resource assigned to one task.
  • One task assigned to one resource.
  • Costs or profits are known.

General Assignment Structure

Resource Job 1 Job 2 Job 3
A 5 8 6
B 4 7 3
C 6 5 4

The objective is to find the best assignment.

Applications

  • Employee assignment
  • Machine scheduling
  • Project allocation
  • Sales territory allocation

Example 1 (Business)

A company assigns salespersons to different regions to maximize sales performance.

Example 2 (Practical)

A school assigns teachers to classes based on expertise.

Topic 2: Hungarian Method (Flood's Technique)

Explanation

The Hungarian Method is the most widely used technique for solving assignment problems.

It systematically reduces the cost matrix to identify the optimal assignment.

Steps of Hungarian Method

Step 1: Row Reduction

Subtract the smallest element in each row from all elements of that row.

Step 2: Column Reduction

Subtract the smallest element in each column from all elements of that column.

Step 3: Cover Zeros

Draw minimum number of lines covering all zeros.

Step 4: Test Optimality

If the number of lines equals the order of the matrix, an optimal solution exists.

Step 5: Adjust Matrix

If optimality is not achieved, revise the matrix and repeat.

Flowchart

↓
Row Reduction
↓
Column Reduction
↓
Cover Zeros
↓
Check Optimality
↓
Optimal Assignment

Example 1 (Business)

A manufacturing company assigns machines to production jobs using the Hungarian Method.

Example 2 (Practical)

A university assigns invigilators to examination halls to minimize supervision costs.

Topic 3: Minimization Assignment Problems

Explanation

In minimization problems, the objective is to minimize total cost, time, or effort.

Objective

Minimize:

Total Assignment Cost

Applications

  • Transportation scheduling
  • Labor allocation
  • Machine assignment

Example 1 (Business)

A logistics company assigns drivers to routes to minimize transportation cost.

Example 2 (Practical)

A hospital assigns nurses to wards to reduce workload imbalance.

Topic 4: Maximization Assignment Problems

Explanation

In maximization problems, the objective is to maximize profit, productivity, or benefits.

These problems are converted into minimization problems before applying the Hungarian Method.

Conversion Procedure

Highest Value − Individual Value

This converts profit values into cost values.

Example 1 (Business)

A company assigns sales executives to territories to maximize revenue.

Example 2 (Practical)

A sports coach assigns players to positions to maximize team performance.

Topic 5: Unbalanced Assignment Problems

Explanation

An Assignment Problem is unbalanced when the number of resources and tasks are unequal.

Solution

Introduce a dummy row or dummy column with zero cost.

Example

Employees Job A Job B
E1 5 4
E2 7 6
E3 3 5

Since employees exceed jobs, a dummy job is added.

Example 1 (Business)

A company has more workers than available projects.

Example 2 (Practical)

A school has more teachers than subjects to be assigned.

Topic 6: Restricted Assignment Problems

Explanation

In some situations, certain assignments are prohibited.

These are called restricted assignments.

Handling Restrictions

A very large cost (M) is assigned to prohibited cells.

This prevents the assignment from being selected.

Example 1 (Business)

A machine cannot perform a specific task due to technical limitations.

Example 2 (Practical)

A teacher cannot supervise an examination due to scheduling conflicts.

Part B: Transportation Models

Topic 7: Concept and Formulation of Transportation Model

Explanation

The Transportation Model determines the most economical way to transport goods from multiple sources to multiple destinations.

Objectives

  • Minimize transportation cost.
  • Minimize transportation time.
  • Maximize profit.

Transportation Structure

Sources
↓
Transportation Routes
↓
Destinations

Components

Sources

Supply points.

Destinations

Demand points.

Transportation Cost

Cost per unit transported.

Example 1 (Business)

A company transports products from factories to warehouses.

Example 2 (Practical)

A dairy distributes milk from processing plants to retail stores.

Topic 8: Types of Transportation Problems

A. Balanced Transportation Problem

Explanation

Total Supply = Total Demand

Example

Supply = 100 Units

Demand = 100 Units

Balanced problem.

B. Unbalanced Transportation Problem

Explanation

Total Supply ≠ Total Demand

Solution

Add a dummy source or destination.

Example

Supply = 120 Units

Demand = 100 Units

Add dummy destination for 20 units.

C. Minimization Transportation Problem

Objective: Minimize transportation cost.

D. Maximization Transportation Problem

Objective: Maximize profit.

Requires conversion into minimization form.

Example 1 (Business)

A company minimizes freight expenses between warehouses.

Example 2 (Practical)

A delivery service optimizes routes to reduce fuel costs.

Topic 9: Initial Basic Feasible Solutions (IBFS)

Explanation

Before finding the optimal solution, an initial feasible solution must be obtained.

A. North-West Corner Method (NWCM)

Explanation

Allocation begins from the top-left corner of the transportation table.

Procedure

Start at North-West Cell
↓
Allocate Maximum Possible Units
↓
Adjust Supply and Demand
↓
Move to Next Cell

Advantages

  • Very simple.
  • Fast computation.

Limitation

  • May not produce low-cost solutions.

B. Least Cost Method (LCM)

Explanation

Allocation is made to the cell with the lowest transportation cost.

Procedure

Identify Lowest Cost Cell
↓
Allocate Maximum Quantity
↓
Adjust Supply and Demand
↓
Repeat

Advantages

Produces better solutions than NWCM.

C. Vogel's Approximation Method (VAM)

Explanation

VAM generally provides the best initial solution.

Procedure

Compute Penalties
↓
Select Highest Penalty
↓
Allocate to Lowest Cost Cell
↓
Adjust Supply and Demand
↓
Repeat

Advantages

  • Usually closest to optimal solution.
  • Reduces total transportation cost.

Comparison of IBFS Methods

Method Complexity Solution Quality
NWCM Easy Lowest
LCM Moderate Better
VAM Higher Best

Topic 10: Optimal Solution Using MODI Method

Explanation

After obtaining an initial feasible solution, optimality is tested using the MODI (Modified Distribution) Method.

MODI Procedure

Obtain IBFS
↓
Calculate Opportunity Costs
↓
Test Optimality
↓
Improve Allocation
↓
Reach Optimal Solution

Advantages

  • Efficient.
  • Widely accepted.
  • Provides optimal transportation plan.

Example 1 (Business)

A logistics company minimizes total freight cost using MODI.

Example 2 (Practical)

A distributor determines the least-cost route network for product delivery.

Topic 11: Special Cases in Transportation Problems

Explanation

Certain special situations may arise.

1. Degeneracy

Occurs when occupied cells are fewer than required.

2. Multiple Optimal Solutions

More than one optimal solution exists.

3. Unbalanced Problems

Supply and demand are unequal.

4. Maximization Problems

Need conversion into minimization form.

Example 1 (Business)

A transportation network may have several equally economical shipping plans.

Example 2 (Practical)

A warehouse distribution system may require dummy destinations to balance demand.

Quick Revision Summary

  • Assignment Models allocate resources efficiently.
  • Hungarian Method is the standard technique for assignment problems.
  • Assignment problems may be minimization, maximization, unbalanced, or restricted.
  • Transportation Models optimize distribution costs.
  • Transportation problems may be balanced or unbalanced.
  • NWCM, LCM, and VAM provide initial feasible solutions.
  • MODI Method is used to find optimal transportation solutions.
  • Special cases include degeneracy and multiple optimal solutions.
