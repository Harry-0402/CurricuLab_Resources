Unit 3: Decision Theory and Game Theory

Introduction

Managers frequently make decisions under different conditions such as certainty, risk, and uncertainty. In many business situations, the future cannot be predicted with complete accuracy. Therefore, quantitative techniques are used to evaluate alternatives and select the best course of action.

Decision Theory provides systematic methods for making decisions under risk and uncertainty, while Game Theory helps analyze competitive situations where the outcome depends on the actions of competitors.

These techniques are widely used in marketing, finance, operations, strategic planning, and competitive business environments.

Part A: Decision Theory

Topic 1: Concept of Decision Theory

Explanation

Decision Theory is a branch of quantitative analysis that helps decision-makers choose the best alternative among several possible options.

Definition

Decision Theory is the systematic approach to making decisions by evaluating alternatives, outcomes, and uncertainties.

Elements of Decision Theory

1. Decision Maker

The person responsible for making decisions.

2. Alternatives

Different courses of action available.

3. States of Nature

Possible future events beyond the decision maker's control.

4. Payoffs

Results obtained from selecting an alternative.

Importance

  • Reduces uncertainty.
  • Improves decision quality.
  • Supports rational choices.
  • Helps evaluate risks.

A company decides whether to launch a new product based on expected market demand.

Topic 3: Expected Monetary Value (EMV)

Explanation

Expected Monetary Value (EMV) is one of the most important decision criteria under risk.

It measures the average expected payoff considering probabilities.

Formula

EMV = Σ(Pi × Vi)

Where:

  • (Pi) = Probability of outcome
  • (Vi) = Value (payoff) of outcome

Steps for EMV Calculation

Identify Outcomes
↓
Assign Probabilities
↓
Multiply Payoffs by Probabilities
↓
Sum Results
↓
Select Highest EMV

Example

A company has two alternatives:

Alternative A

  • Profit ₹100,000 with probability 0.6
  • Profit ₹40,000 with probability 0.4

EMV = (0.6 × 100,000) + (0.4 × 40,000)

= 60,000 + 16,000

= ₹76,000

Advantages

  • Simple and logical.
  • Considers probabilities.
  • Widely used in business decisions.

Limitations

  • Requires accurate probabilities.
  • Ignores risk preferences.

Example 1 (Business)

An investment company evaluates different investment opportunities using EMV.

Example 2 (Practical)

A student estimates expected outcomes of various career options.

Topic 4: Decision Making Under Uncertainty

Explanation

Decision making under uncertainty occurs when probabilities of future events are unknown.

The decision maker cannot estimate the likelihood of outcomes.

Characteristics

  • No probability information.
  • Future conditions unknown.
  • Greater decision complexity.

Common Decision Criteria

1. Maximax Criterion (Optimistic)

Choose alternative with highest possible payoff.

2. Maximin Criterion (Pessimistic)

Choose alternative with best worst-case payoff.

3. Minimax Regret Criterion

Minimize maximum regret.

4. Laplace Criterion

Assume equal probability for all outcomes.

Comparison of Criteria

Criterion Approach
Maximax Most Optimistic
Maximin Most Conservative
Minimax Regret Least Regret
Laplace Equal Probability

Example 1 (Business)

A company entering a new market where demand probabilities are unavailable.

Example 2 (Practical)

A student choosing a career path without reliable future information.

Part B: Game Theory

Topic 5: Concept of Game Theory

Explanation

Game Theory is a mathematical technique used to analyze situations involving competition between two or more decision makers.

Each participant attempts to maximize their gains while considering competitors' actions.

Definition

Game Theory is the study of strategic interactions where the outcome for one participant depends on the actions of others.

Components of Game Theory

Players

Participants in the game.

Strategies

Possible actions available.

Payoffs

Rewards or losses resulting from strategies.

Rules

Conditions governing the game.

Game Theory Process

Identify Players
↓
Identify Strategies
↓
Construct Payoff Matrix
↓
Analyze Outcomes
↓
Select Optimal Strategy

Example 1 (Business)

Two competing companies decide whether to reduce prices.

Example 2 (Practical)

Two students compete for the highest examination rank.

Topic 6: 2 × 2 Zero Sum Games

Explanation

A Zero Sum Game is a situation where one player's gain equals another player's loss.

The total gain and loss equals zero.

Structure

Player A / Player B B1 B2
A1 5 -2
A2 -1 4

Positive values represent gains for Player A.

Negative values represent losses for Player A.

Characteristics

  • Competitive environment.
  • One winner and one loser.
  • Total payoff equals zero.

Example 1 (Business)

Two firms compete for the same market share.

Example 2 (Practical)

A chess match where one player's victory is another's defeat.

Topic 7: Pure Strategy

Explanation

A Pure Strategy occurs when a player consistently chooses a particular strategy.

The optimal solution exists at a saddle point.

Saddle Point

A saddle point exists when:

Maximum of row minima = Minimum of column maxima

Decision Process

Construct Payoff Matrix
↓
Find Row Minimums
↓
Find Column Maximums
↓
Identify Saddle Point
↓
Select Strategy

Example 1 (Business)

A company consistently chooses a stable pricing strategy.

Example 2 (Practical)

A player repeatedly selects the same move because it provides the best outcome.

Topic 8: Mixed Strategy

Explanation

When no saddle point exists, players use a Mixed Strategy.

A mixed strategy involves selecting strategies according to probabilities.

Characteristics

  • Randomized decisions.
  • Prevents predictability.
  • Used in competitive environments.

Process

No Saddle Point
↓
Determine Probabilities
↓
Calculate Expected Payoffs
↓
Choose Optimal Mix

Example 1 (Business)

Companies vary advertising campaigns to avoid predictable competitor responses.

Example 2 (Practical)

A cricket bowler alternates between different bowling styles.

Topic 9: Dominance Principle

Explanation

The Dominance Principle simplifies game problems by eliminating inferior strategies.

A strategy is dominated if another strategy performs equally well or better in all situations.

Procedure

Construct Payoff Matrix
↓
Identify Dominated Rows/Columns
↓
Eliminate Inferior Strategies
↓
Reduce Matrix
↓
Solve Game

Advantages

  • Simplifies calculations.
  • Reduces matrix size.
  • Speeds up analysis.

Example 1 (Business)

A company eliminates pricing strategies that consistently produce lower profits.

Example 2 (Practical)

A sports team removes ineffective game plans before a match.
