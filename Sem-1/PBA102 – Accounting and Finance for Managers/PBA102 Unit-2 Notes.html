Unit 2: Financial Statements

Introduction

Financial statements are formal records that summarize the financial activities and position of a business. They provide useful information to owners, investors, creditors, managers, and government authorities for decision-making.

The main financial statements are the Trial Balance, Profit & Loss Account, Profit & Loss Appropriation Account, and Balance Sheet. These statements help in assessing profitability, financial stability, and operational efficiency.

Topic 1: Trial Balance

Explanation

A Trial Balance is a statement prepared to verify the arithmetic accuracy of ledger accounts. It lists all account balances on a particular date.

Objectives of Trial Balance

  • Check accuracy of ledger postings.
  • Detect arithmetic errors.
  • Facilitate preparation of final accounts.
  • Summarize ledger balances.

Format of Trial Balance

Account Name Debit (₹) Credit (₹)
Cash A/c xxx
Capital A/c xxx
Sales A/c xxx
Purchases A/c xxx
Total xxx xxx

Features

  • Prepared after ledger balancing.
  • Total debits must equal total credits.
  • Acts as a bridge between ledger and final accounts.

Limitations

Trial Balance cannot detect:

  • Errors of omission
  • Errors of principle
  • Compensating errors
  • Errors of commission in some cases

Example 1 (Business): A manufacturing company prepares a trial balance at the end of the month before preparing financial statements.

Example 2 (Practical): A retail shop owner checks whether total debit balances equal total credit balances before calculating profit.

Topic 3: Profit & Loss Account

Explanation

The Profit & Loss Account is prepared to determine the net profit or net loss of a business during an accounting period.

Purpose

  • Measure profitability.
  • Record indirect expenses.
  • Record indirect incomes.
  • Determine net profit or loss.

Structure

Expenses Amount (₹) Incomes Amount (₹)
Salaries xxx Commission Received xxx
Rent xxx Interest Received xxx
Depreciation xxx Other Income xxx
Net Profit xxx

Formula

$$\text{Net Profit} = \text{Total Income} - \text{Total Expenses}$$

If expenses exceed income, the result is a net loss.

Importance

  • Measures operating efficiency.
  • Assists management decisions.
  • Helps investors evaluate performance.

Example 1 (Business): Hindustan Unilever prepares a Profit & Loss Account annually to determine profitability and report earnings.

Example 2 (Practical): A coaching institute calculates annual income from fees and deducts rent, salaries, and utilities to determine profit.

Topic 4: Profit & Loss Appropriation Account

Explanation

The Profit & Loss Appropriation Account shows how the net profit is distributed among owners, partners, or shareholders. It is prepared after the Profit & Loss Account.

Items Included

  • Transfer to reserves
  • Dividend payment
  • Partner's salary
  • Interest on capital
  • Retained earnings

Format

Particulars Amount (₹)
Net Profit xxx
Less: Dividends xxx
Less: Reserves xxx
Balance Carried Forward xxx

Importance

  • Shows utilization of profits.
  • Indicates retained earnings.
  • Helps assess dividend policy.

Example 1 (Business): ITC Limited allocates part of its profits as dividends and retains the remaining amount for expansion.

Example 2 (Practical): Partners in a firm divide annual profits according to the partnership agreement.

Topic 5: Balance Sheet

Explanation

A Balance Sheet is a statement showing the financial position of a business on a specific date. It presents assets, liabilities, and owner's equity.

Basic Equation

$$\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}$$

Structure

Liabilities Amount (₹) Assets Amount (₹)
Capital xxx Cash xxx
Loans xxx Inventory xxx
Creditors xxx Machinery xxx
Total xxx Total xxx

Objectives

  • Determine financial position.
  • Assess solvency.
  • Assist investors and creditors.
  • Support financial planning.

Characteristics

  • Prepared on a particular date.
  • Summarizes assets and liabilities.
  • Reflects business net worth.

Example 1 (Business): Asian Paints reports its assets, liabilities, and shareholder equity in its annual balance sheet.

Example 2 (Practical): A shopkeeper prepares a balance sheet to determine total assets and outstanding loans.

Topic 7: Different Types of Assets and Liabilities

Explanation

Assets and liabilities can be classified into different categories depending on their purpose and nature.

A. Financial Assets

Financial assets represent contractual claims to receive cash or another financial asset.

Examples

  • Cash
  • Bank Deposits
  • Investments
  • Accounts Receivable

Example 1 (Business): A company invests surplus funds in government securities.

Example 2 (Practical): Money deposited in a savings account is a financial asset.

B. Operating Investments

Investments used to support core business operations.

Examples

  • Production Equipment
  • Technology Systems
  • Warehouses

Example 1 (Business): An automobile company purchases robots for manufacturing.

Example 2 (Practical): A bakery buys an oven to increase production capacity.

C. Operating Liabilities

Liabilities arising from daily business operations.

Examples

  • Trade Creditors
  • Outstanding Salaries
  • Outstanding Rent

Example 1 (Business): A company purchases raw materials on credit from suppliers.

Example 2 (Practical): A shop owes one month's rent to the landlord.

D. Financial Liabilities

Obligations involving repayment of borrowed funds.

Examples

  • Bank Loans
  • Debentures
  • Bonds Payable

Example 1 (Business): A company takes a term loan from a bank for expansion.

Example 2 (Practical): An entrepreneur borrows money from a bank to start a business.

E. Equity

Equity represents the owner's residual interest in the business.

Components

  • Share Capital
  • Retained Earnings
  • Reserves and Surplus

Formula

$$\text{Equity} = \text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities}$$

Example 1 (Business): Shareholders of Wipro collectively own the company's equity.

Example 2 (Practical): If a business has assets worth ₹10 lakh and liabilities worth ₹4 lakh, equity equals ₹6 lakh.
