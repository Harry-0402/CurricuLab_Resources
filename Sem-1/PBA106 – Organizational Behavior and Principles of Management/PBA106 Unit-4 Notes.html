Unit 4: Motivation, Communication & Control

Introduction

Motivation and communication are essential for organizational success. Motivation encourages employees to work enthusiastically and achieve goals, while communication ensures the smooth flow of information within the organization. Control helps managers monitor activities, compare actual performance with planned performance, and take corrective actions when necessary. Together, these functions improve organizational efficiency and effectiveness.

Topic 1: Motivation

Meaning of Motivation

Motivation is the process of stimulating and encouraging individuals to perform their duties effectively and willingly to achieve organizational goals.

The word "motivation" is derived from the word "motive", which means a need, desire, or drive that prompts action.

Features of Motivation

  • It is a psychological process.
  • It is goal-oriented.
  • It is continuous in nature.
  • It varies from person to person.
  • It influences employee performance.

Importance of Motivation

Importance Explanation
Increases Productivity Motivated employees work more efficiently.
Improves Morale Employees feel satisfied and positive.
Reduces Absenteeism Employees attend work regularly.
Encourages Innovation Employees contribute new ideas.
Improves Organizational Growth Better performance leads to growth.

Example 1

Google motivates employees through career development opportunities, recognition programs, and employee-friendly policies.

Example 2

A student studies harder when motivated by the goal of achieving top grades.

Topic 2: Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory

Explanation

Frederick Herzberg developed the Two-Factor Theory of Motivation, also known as the Motivation-Hygiene Theory.

According to Herzberg, employee satisfaction and dissatisfaction are influenced by two separate sets of factors.

Hygiene Factors

These factors prevent dissatisfaction but do not necessarily motivate employees.

Hygiene Factors Include

  • Salary
  • Company policies
  • Job security
  • Working conditions
  • Supervision
  • Interpersonal relationships

Motivating Factors

These factors create job satisfaction and encourage higher performance.

Motivators Include

  • Achievement
  • Recognition
  • Responsibility
  • Promotion
  • Growth opportunities
  • Challenging work

Comparison

Hygiene Factors Motivators
Prevent dissatisfaction Create satisfaction
External factors Internal factors
Salary Achievement
Working conditions Recognition
Job security Responsibility

Example 1

A company may increase salaries (hygiene factor), but employees become truly motivated when given promotions and recognition (motivators).

Example 2

A student requires a comfortable study environment but feels motivated when receiving appreciation for achievements.

Topic 3: Vroom's Expectancy Theory

Explanation

Victor Vroom proposed that employees are motivated when they believe that their effort will lead to good performance and desired rewards.

Basic Concept

Motivation depends on three factors:

  1. Expectancy
  2. Instrumentality
  3. Valence

Components of Expectancy Theory

Component Meaning
Expectancy Effort will lead to performance
Instrumentality Performance will lead to rewards
Valence Value attached to rewards

Flow of Theory

Effort
↓
Performance
↓
Reward
↓
Satisfaction

Example 1

A salesperson works harder because they believe achieving targets will result in bonuses.

Example 2

A student studies diligently because good grades may lead to scholarships.

Topic 4: Porter and Lawler Model of Motivation

Explanation

Porter and Lawler expanded Vroom's theory by suggesting that performance leads to satisfaction rather than satisfaction leading to performance.

The model considers:

  • Effort
  • Ability
  • Role perception
  • Performance
  • Rewards
  • Satisfaction

Porter and Lawler Model

Effort
↓
Performance
↓
Rewards
↓
Satisfaction

Key Features

  • Effort alone does not guarantee performance.
  • Ability influences performance.
  • Rewards affect satisfaction.
  • Satisfaction depends on perceived fairness of rewards.

Example 1

An employee works hard, performs well, receives promotion, and becomes satisfied.

Example 2

A student studies effectively, scores high marks, receives recognition, and feels satisfied.

Topic 5: Communication

Definition

Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts, feelings, and messages between two or more persons to achieve mutual understanding.

Features of Communication

  • Two-way process
  • Continuous process
  • Goal-oriented
  • Involves sender and receiver
  • Essential for coordination

Importance of Communication

Importance Explanation
Coordination Helps departments work together
Decision Making Provides necessary information
Motivation Encourages employees
Control Helps monitor activities
Better Relationships Improves workplace harmony

Example 1

Microsoft uses effective communication systems to coordinate global operations.

Example 2

Teachers communicate instructions clearly to students to ensure learning.

Topic 7: Types of Communication

Based on Direction

Downward Communication

Information flows from higher levels to lower levels.

Examples:

  • Instructions
  • Policies
  • Orders

Upward Communication

Information flows from subordinates to superiors.

Examples:

  • Reports
  • Suggestions
  • Complaints

Horizontal Communication

Communication between employees at the same level.

Examples:

  • Department coordination
  • Team discussions

Diagonal Communication

Communication across departments and levels.

Examples:

  • HR communicating directly with production staff.

Based on Method

Formal Communication Informal Communication
Official channels Unofficial channels
Written policies Casual conversations
Structured Flexible

Example 1

Official circulars are examples of formal communication.

Example 2

Workplace discussions during breaks represent informal communication.

Topic 10: Barriers to Communication

Explanation

Communication barriers are obstacles that prevent messages from being understood correctly.

Types of Barriers

Barrier Explanation
Physical Noise, distance
Semantic Language differences
Psychological Emotions, attitudes
Organizational Complex hierarchy
Technological Equipment failure

Example 1

Background noise during a meeting may distort communication.

Example 2

Different interpretations of technical terms may create misunderstandings.

Topic 11: Remedies for Communication Barriers

Solutions

  • Use simple language.
  • Encourage feedback.
  • Improve listening skills.
  • Reduce organizational complexity.
  • Select appropriate communication channels.
  • Develop interpersonal relationships.
  • Provide communication training.

Example 1

Managers conducting regular feedback sessions improve communication effectiveness.

Example 2

Teachers using simple language enhance student understanding.

Topic 12: Control

Definition

Control is the process of measuring actual performance, comparing it with standards, and taking corrective action when necessary.

Importance of Control

  • Achieves organizational objectives.
  • Identifies deviations.
  • Improves efficiency.
  • Supports planning.
  • Ensures resource utilization.

Control Process

Establish Standards
↓
Measure Performance
↓
Compare with Standards
↓
Identify Deviations
↓
Take Corrective Action

Types of Control

Type Meaning
Feedforward Control Before activity begins
Concurrent Control During activity
Feedback Control After activity completion

Example 1

A manufacturing company checks product quality during production.

Example 2

A teacher evaluates students through periodic tests.

Quick Revision Summary

  • Motivation stimulates employees to achieve goals.
  • Herzberg classified factors into hygiene factors and motivators.
  • Vroom's theory emphasizes expectancy, instrumentality, and valence.
  • Porter and Lawler linked performance, rewards, and satisfaction.
  • Communication is the exchange of information for mutual understanding.
  • Communication can be upward, downward, horizontal, or diagonal.
  • Various barriers can affect communication effectiveness.
  • Control ensures activities are performed according to plans.
  • The control process involves standards, measurement, comparison, and corrective action.
