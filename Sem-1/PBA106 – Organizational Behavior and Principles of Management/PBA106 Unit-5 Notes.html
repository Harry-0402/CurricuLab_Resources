Unit 5: Job Satisfaction & Employee Behaviour

Introduction

Employees are the most valuable asset of any organization. Their satisfaction, attitude, morale, and behaviour directly influence productivity and organizational success. Organizations today focus not only on profits but also on employee well-being and quality of work life. Understanding job satisfaction and employee behaviour helps managers create a positive work environment, improve performance, and retain talented employees.

Topic 1: Job Satisfaction

Meaning

Job satisfaction refers to the positive emotional state or feeling that an employee experiences as a result of their job and work environment.

It indicates the extent to which employees like or dislike their jobs.

Definitions

  • Job satisfaction is the level of contentment employees feel regarding their work.
  • It reflects an employee's attitude toward their job, colleagues, supervisors, and organization.

Factors Affecting Job Satisfaction

1. Salary and Benefits

Fair compensation increases satisfaction.

2. Working Conditions

A safe and comfortable workplace improves satisfaction.

3. Promotion Opportunities

Career growth enhances motivation and satisfaction.

4. Leadership Style

Supportive leadership creates positive feelings.

5. Job Security

Employees prefer stable employment.

6. Recognition

Appreciation for good performance increases satisfaction.

7. Interpersonal Relationships

Healthy relationships improve workplace happiness.

8. Nature of Work

Interesting and meaningful work enhances satisfaction.

Importance of Job Satisfaction

Importance Explanation
Higher Productivity Satisfied employees perform better.
Lower Turnover Employees are less likely to leave.
Better Morale Creates a positive environment.
Improved Loyalty Employees remain committed.
Reduced Absenteeism Employees attend work regularly.

Example 1

Google focuses on employee well-being, career growth, and recognition, resulting in high job satisfaction.

Example 2

A teacher who receives appreciation from students and management often experiences greater job satisfaction.

Topic 2: Job Simplification

Meaning

Job simplification involves breaking a job into smaller, specialized tasks to improve efficiency and productivity.

Objectives

  • Increase efficiency
  • Reduce training requirements
  • Improve specialization
  • Reduce work complexity

Advantages

  • Higher productivity
  • Easier supervision
  • Faster training

Disadvantages

  • Employee boredom
  • Reduced creativity
  • Monotony

Example 1

Assembly line workers in manufacturing perform specific repetitive tasks.

Example 2

A cashier focuses only on billing activities throughout the day.

Topic 3: Job Enlargement

Meaning

Job enlargement involves increasing the number of tasks performed by an employee at the same level of responsibility.

Features

  • Adds variety to work
  • Reduces boredom
  • Expands job scope
  • Maintains same authority level

Advantages

  • Greater job variety
  • Reduced monotony
  • Improved employee interest

Limitations

  • Increased workload
  • May not increase motivation significantly

Example 1

A bank employee handles both customer service and account processing.

Example 2

A receptionist also manages appointment scheduling and visitor records.

Topic 4: Job Enrichment

Meaning

Job enrichment involves increasing the depth of a job by giving employees greater responsibility, authority, and decision-making power.

Features

  • Higher responsibility
  • Greater autonomy
  • Increased decision-making authority
  • Opportunities for growth

Advantages

  • Increased motivation
  • Improved job satisfaction
  • Better employee development

Example 1

A software developer is allowed to make project-related decisions independently.

Example 2

A teacher is given authority to design classroom activities and assessments.

Comparison: Job Simplification, Job Enlargement and Job Enrichment

Basis Job Simplification Job Enlargement Job Enrichment

Topic 5: Job Design

Meaning

Job design refers to the process of organizing duties, responsibilities, and tasks into a productive unit of work.

Objectives of Job Design

  • Improve efficiency
  • Increase motivation
  • Enhance job satisfaction
  • Reduce fatigue
  • Improve productivity

Components of Job Design

  • Task variety
  • Task identity
  • Task significance
  • Autonomy
  • Feedback

Importance

  • Improves performance
  • Enhances employee satisfaction
  • Reduces turnover
  • Encourages commitment

Example 1

A company redesigns jobs to allow employees greater autonomy and decision-making.

Example 2

A school redesigns teaching schedules to reduce workload stress.

Topic 6: Morale

Meaning

Morale refers to the overall attitude, confidence, enthusiasm, and satisfaction of employees toward their work and organization.

Characteristics of High Morale

  • Positive attitude
  • Cooperation
  • Commitment
  • Enthusiasm
  • Loyalty

Factors Affecting Morale

  • Leadership
  • Working conditions
  • Compensation
  • Communication
  • Recognition
  • Job security

Importance of Morale

  • Improves productivity
  • Reduces absenteeism
  • Enhances teamwork
  • Improves organizational climate

Example 1

Employees in organizations with supportive management often display high morale.

Example 2

Students participating actively in class usually exhibit high morale.

Topic 7: Employee Attitude

Meaning

Employee attitude refers to an individual's positive or negative feelings, beliefs, and opinions about their job, organization, and workplace.

Components of Attitude

Component Meaning
Cognitive Beliefs and thoughts
Affective Feelings and emotions
Behavioral Actions and responses

Types of Employee Attitudes

  • Positive attitude
  • Negative attitude
  • Neutral attitude

Importance

  • Influences productivity
  • Affects workplace relationships
  • Impacts organizational performance

Example 1

Employees who believe in organizational goals often display positive attitudes.

Example 2

A student who enjoys learning develops a positive attitude toward education.

Topic 8: Employee Behaviour

Meaning

Employee behaviour refers to how employees act, interact, and perform within the workplace.

Types of Employee Behaviour

Productive Behaviour

Contributes positively to organizational goals.

Examples:

  • Cooperation
  • Initiative
  • Teamwork

Counterproductive Behaviour

Negatively affects organizational objectives.

Examples:

  • Absenteeism
  • Conflict
  • Poor performance

Factors Influencing Employee Behaviour

  • Personality
  • Perception
  • Motivation
  • Leadership
  • Organizational culture
  • Work environment

Example 1

Employees who actively participate in team projects demonstrate productive behaviour.

Example 2

Students helping classmates during group assignments show positive behaviour.

Explanation

Employee attitude and behaviour directly affect productivity and organizational performance.

Positive Attitude Leads To

  • Better work quality
  • Increased productivity
  • Improved teamwork
  • Higher customer satisfaction

Negative Attitude Leads To

  • Low productivity
  • Workplace conflicts
  • Absenteeism
  • Employee turnover

Relationship

Positive Attitude
↓
Positive Behaviour
↓

Example 1

Organizations with motivated and cooperative employees achieve better results.

Example 2

Students with positive learning attitudes often achieve higher academic performance.

Topic 10: Quality of Work Life (QWL)

Meaning

Quality of Work Life (QWL) refers to the degree of satisfaction employees derive from their jobs, working conditions, and overall work environment.

Objectives of QWL

  • Improve employee satisfaction
  • Enhance productivity
  • Reduce stress
  • Promote work-life balance
  • Improve organizational effectiveness

Elements of QWL

Element Description
Fair Compensation Adequate wages and benefits
Safe Environment Healthy workplace
Career Development Growth opportunities
Job Security Stable employment
Work-Life Balance Balance between work and personal life
Employee Participation Involvement in decisions

Benefits of QWL

  • Higher job satisfaction
  • Increased motivation
  • Reduced turnover
  • Better employee health
  • Improved organizational image

Example 1

Microsoft offers flexible work arrangements to improve quality of work life.

Example 2

A company introducing hybrid work policies helps employees balance personal and professional responsibilities.

Quick Revision Summary

  • Job satisfaction reflects employees' feelings toward their jobs.
  • Salary, leadership, recognition, and working conditions influence satisfaction.
  • Job simplification increases specialization but may create monotony.
  • Job enlargement increases task variety.
  • Job enrichment increases responsibility and autonomy.
  • Job design improves productivity and satisfaction.
  • Morale represents employee confidence and enthusiasm.
  • Employee attitudes and behaviours significantly influence productivity.
  • Quality of Work Life improves employee well-being and organizational effectiveness.
