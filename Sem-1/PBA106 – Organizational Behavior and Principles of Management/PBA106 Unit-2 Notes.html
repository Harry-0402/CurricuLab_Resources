Unit 2: Organizational Behaviour, Perception & Motivation

Introduction

Organizational Behaviour (OB) is the study of human behaviour in organizations. It helps managers understand how individuals and groups behave at work, why employees react differently in similar situations, and how motivation and perception influence performance. Understanding OB enables organizations to improve productivity, employee satisfaction, and workplace relationships.

Topic 1: Concept of Organizational Behaviour

Explanation

Organizational Behaviour is the systematic study of human behaviour within organizations. It examines how individuals, groups, and organizational structures affect employee performance.

Objectives of Organizational Behaviour

  • Understand employee behaviour.
  • Predict future behaviour.
  • Improve employee performance.
  • Build effective teams.
  • Enhance organizational effectiveness.

Characteristics of Organizational Behaviour

  • Human-oriented
  • Goal-oriented
  • Scientific approach
  • Interdisciplinary in nature
  • Dynamic and adaptive

Example 1

Infosys studies employee behaviour and satisfaction to improve workplace productivity.

Example 2

A college administration observes student and teacher interactions to improve academic performance.

Topic 3: Individual Differences

Explanation

Every individual is unique. Differences in intelligence, personality, attitudes, values, and experiences result in different behaviours at work.

Factors Causing Individual Differences

  • Age
  • Gender
  • Education
  • Personality
  • Intelligence
  • Culture
  • Experience
  • Values and beliefs

Importance

  • Helps managers assign suitable jobs.
  • Improves teamwork.
  • Enhances employee satisfaction.
  • Reduces workplace conflicts.

Example 1

An organization may assign analytical tasks to employees with strong problem-solving skills.

Example 2

In a classroom, some students learn quickly while others require additional guidance.

Explanation

Intelligence refers to the ability to learn, understand, reason, and solve problems.

Nature of Intelligence Tests

  • Measure mental ability.
  • Assess reasoning skills.
  • Predict learning capability.
  • Assist in employee selection.

Types of Intelligence Tests

Type Description
Individual Tests Conducted for one person
Group Tests Conducted for multiple people
Verbal Tests Use language-based questions
Non-Verbal Tests Use diagrams and symbols

Uses of Intelligence Tests

  • Recruitment and selection
  • Career guidance
  • Training assessment
  • Employee placement

Example 2

A university may conduct aptitude tests before admission to specialized courses.

Nature of Personality Tests

  • Measure behavioural tendencies.
  • Identify strengths and weaknesses.
  • Predict workplace behaviour.

Types of Personality Tests

Type Description
Objective Tests Structured questions
Projective Tests Interpretation of images or situations
Self-Report Inventories Individuals assess themselves
Situational Tests Behaviour in hypothetical situations

Uses of Personality Tests

  • Employee selection
  • Career planning
  • Leadership development
  • Team formation

Example 1

Many organizations use personality assessments before hiring managerial candidates.

Example 2

A sports team evaluates players' personalities to build effective teamwork.

Topic 11: McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y

Explanation

Douglas McGregor proposed two assumptions about employees.

Theory X

Managers believe employees:

  • Dislike work.
  • Avoid responsibility.
  • Need strict supervision.
  • Prefer direction.

Theory Y

Managers believe employees:

  • Enjoy work.
  • Accept responsibility.
  • Are creative.
  • Can self-direct.

Comparison

Feature Theory X Theory Y
View of Employees Negative Positive
Motivation External control Self-motivation
Responsibility Avoided Accepted
Supervision Strict Participative

Example 1

Modern technology companies often adopt Theory Y practices.

Example 2

A teacher allowing students to participate in classroom decisions follows Theory Y.

Topic 12: Financial and Non-Financial Motivation

Financial Motivation

Monetary rewards given to employees.

Examples

  • Salary
  • Bonus
  • Incentives
  • Profit sharing
  • Commission

Example 1

Sales employees receive commission for achieving targets.

Example 2

Workers receive performance bonuses.

Non-Financial Motivation

Non-monetary rewards that satisfy psychological and social needs.

Examples

  • Recognition
  • Promotion
  • Job security
  • Participation
  • Appreciation
  • Career development

Example 1

Employee-of-the-Month awards increase morale.

Example 2

Public appreciation motivates students and employees alike.
