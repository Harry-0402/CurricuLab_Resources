Unit 1: Evolution of Management & Planning

Introduction

Management is the process of getting work done efficiently and effectively through people. Every organization, whether it is a business, hospital, educational institution, or government office, requires management to achieve its goals. The study of management evolved over time through the contributions of various thinkers and researchers. Understanding management principles helps managers make better decisions, utilize resources effectively, and achieve organizational objectives.

Topic 1: Evolution of Management

Management thought has developed gradually through different schools of thought. The major contributors include Frederick W. Taylor, Elton Mayo, and Henri Fayol.

Contribution of Frederick W. Taylor (Scientific Management)

Taylor is known as the Father of Scientific Management.

Main Contributions

  1. Scientific study of work methods.
  2. Standardization of tools and equipment.
  3. Scientific selection and training of workers.
  4. Division of work between management and workers.
  5. Introduction of incentive wage systems.

Principles of Scientific Management

Principle Explanation
Science, not Rule of Thumb Use scientific methods instead of traditional practices.
Harmony Maintain cooperation between workers and management.
Cooperation Employees and managers should work together.
Maximum Output Increase productivity.
Development of Workers Train employees scientifically.

Example 1

Toyota uses standardized production processes to improve efficiency, reflecting Taylor's principles.

Example 2

A restaurant using standard recipes and cooking procedures ensures consistent food quality and faster service.

Contribution of Elton Mayo (Human Relations Approach)

Elton Mayo emphasized the importance of human factors in organizations.

Hawthorne Studies

The Hawthorne experiments revealed that employee productivity is influenced by social and psychological factors.

Findings

  • Employees perform better when they receive attention.
  • Group relationships influence productivity.
  • Employee satisfaction improves performance.
  • Communication and participation are important.

Example 1

Google provides employee-friendly workplaces, increasing motivation and productivity.

Example 2

A school principal who regularly appreciates teachers may observe improved teaching performance.

Topic 5: Management and Its Functions

Definition

Management is the process of planning, organizing, staffing, directing, and controlling organizational resources to achieve objectives efficiently.

Functions of Management

Planning
↓
Organizing
↓
Staffing
↓
Directing
↓
Controlling

Explanation

Planning

Determining future objectives and actions.

Organizing

Arranging resources and assigning duties.

Staffing

Recruiting and selecting employees.

Directing

Guiding and motivating employees.

Controlling

Comparing actual performance with standards.

Example 1

A manufacturing company plans production, organizes machinery, recruits workers, directs activities, and controls quality.

Example 2

A college organizes faculty and monitors academic performance.

Topic 6: Levels of Management

Organizations generally have three levels of management.

Level Responsibilities
Top-Level Management Strategic decisions and policy formulation
Middle-Level Management Implement policies and coordinate departments
Lower-Level Management Supervise workers and daily operations

Example 1

  • CEO → Top Level
  • Department Head → Middle Level
  • Supervisor → Lower Level

Example 2

  • Director → Top Level
  • Medical Superintendent → Middle Level

Topic 8: Planning

Definition

Planning is deciding in advance what to do, how to do it, when to do it, and who will do it.

Nature of Planning

  • Future-oriented
  • Continuous process
  • Goal-oriented
  • Intellectual activity
  • Primary management function

Importance of Planning

  • Provides direction
  • Reduces uncertainty
  • Improves efficiency
  • Facilitates control
  • Helps decision making

Process of Planning

Analyzing Environment
↓
Developing Alternatives
↓
Evaluating Alternatives
↓

↓

A company planning a new product launch studies market demand before implementation.

Example 2
